This paper tests whether women integrate fundamental frequency (voice pitch) and formant frequencies (apparent vocal tract length) when judging the attractiveness of men's voices, using Praat-manipulated stimuli with ±0.5 ERB pitch shifts and ±15% formant shifts. The key finding is a significant interaction: low pitch is more attractive when paired with large apparent vocal tracts, and large vocal tracts are more attractive with low pitch — supporting a cue amplification model rather than independent processing of these cues. For Qlatt, this establishes that voice presets should manipulate F0 and formant frequencies coherently (both lowered for masculine, both raised for feminine) and provides specific manipulation magnitudes for perceptually meaningful voice quality variation.
