Coker describes a complete articulatory speech-synthesis system built from a spatial vocal-tract model with seven to ten degrees of freedom, a mode-oriented linear dynamics model obtained by inverting the classical principal-axis problem, and an open-loop feed-forward controller. Its central contribution is the "priority" mechanism, in which each phoneme assigns per-articulator lead and lag times, measured in milliseconds, that offset the nominal segment boundary and thereby produce correct anticipatory and carryover coarticulation; the paper also gives a context-dependent priority rule for /g/ that reproduces the observed circular tongue-body motion, and a scalar allophonic control digit driving stress- and boundary-dependent variation. For a Klatt-lineage formant synthesizer the articulatory geometry is not directly transplantable, but the priority timing model, the allophonic control design, and concrete numbers such as position-dependent /t/ devoicing times, the 175 Hz minimum formant separation, and the roughly 200 Hz yielding-wall F1 floor apply directly to parameter-track generation.
