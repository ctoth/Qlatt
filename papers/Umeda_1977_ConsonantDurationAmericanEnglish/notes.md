# Umeda 1977 -- Consonant Duration in American English

## Implementation-Relevant Notes

### Overview

Comprehensive study of consonant durations in connected American English speech. One male speaker (SP) read ~20 min of essay text, producing ~650 spectrograms. Three measurers checked results. Consonant duration measured from spectrogram discontinuities in excitation, formant structure, or both.

### Key Finding: Additive Duration Model

Consonant duration is a function of the following factors, which combine **additively** from a base duration in the word-initial stressed condition:

1. **Position in word** (initial/medial/final)
2. **Relation to lexical stress and morpheme boundary**
3. **Whether in postpausal position** (after silence)
4. **Whether in prepausal position** (phrase-final)
5. **Content-function word difference**
6. **Effect of adjacent consonant** (cluster rules)

### Standard Deviations (Table III)

Standard deviations per consonant per condition range from ~7 to ~27 msec. Key findings:
- Stops (/p/, /t/, /k/, /b/, /d/, /g/, /tf/, /d3/) show **smaller** variance than other consonants
- /f/ scatters more in intraword situations
- Word-initial stressed consonants show larger variance than word-medial
- Consonant shows the greatest variance after a pause
- Intervocalic situation has the **smallest** standard deviation

### Consonant Clusters -- Duration Modifications

#### Word-Initial Clusters (Table IV)

/s/ in a word-initial cluster is **~40 msec shorter** than single /s/.

Fricatives in initial stressed syllable:
- /s/ in /V'sV/ = 120 msec; in /V'sp/ = 80 msec; in /V'st/ = 83 msec

#### Stops in Initial Clusters

/t/ becomes considerably shorter (30-36 msec) as second member of cluster.
/p/ and /k/ shorten only about 15-20 msec.
/tr/ is recognized as an affricate; closure time is shorter than single /t/ but aspiration is greater.

### Content vs. Function Words (Section I.C)

Content words show significantly longer word-initial consonant durations than function words:
- 20-40 msec difference in word-initial position
- Word-final /n/ shows ~16 msec difference between content and function words
- Word-final position shows no consistent content/function difference (except /n/)

### Closure vs. Aspiration in Stops (Section III)

Linear relationship between closure time and aspiration period. Three groups visible in the data:

For /p/:
- Word-initial and word-medial allophones: linear trend, closure 20-100 msec, aspiration 0-100 msec
- Word-final: minimum aspiration regardless of closure
- Preceded by nasal: same aspiration as oral counterpart but shorter closure

For /t/:
- Most tokens; linear closure-aspiration relationship
- Word-final and morpheme-final: minimum aspiration
- Nasals preceded: aspiration maintained, closure shortened

For /k/:
- Similar pattern but more scattered
- Third group (nasal-preceded) positioned above main group

### Key Rules for Duration Synthesis

1. **Base duration** differs per consonant (Table II, column V#'xV)
2. **Position adjustments** are additive deviations from base (Table II)
3. **Cluster shortening** is typically 25-50 msec for the consonant adjacent to another consonant
4. **Stops**: closure and aspiration have a near-linear relationship; total duration = closure + aspiration
5. **/s/ in clusters** shortens by ~40 msec
6. **Content/function** distinction: word-initial consonants 20-40 msec longer in content words
7. **Prepausal lengthening**: consonants are significantly lengthened before a physical pause but NOT before a pseudopause
8. **Word-final**: followed by voiced consonant = shorter than followed by voiceless
9. Model cannot use fixed constants for all consonants (contra Klatt 1976); different consonants need different constants

### Notation Key

- V = vowel
- C = consonant
- \# = word boundary
- ' = primary stress
- phi = pause (silence)
- x = consonant in question
- N = nasal
- Notation example: V#'xV = vowel, word boundary, stressed syllable, consonant, vowel
