# Abstract

Energy density spectra of gated segments of fricative consonants were measured. The spectral data were used as a basis for developing objective identification criteria which yielded fair results when tested. As a further check gated segments of fricatives were presented for identification to a group of listeners and their responses evaluated in terms of the objective identification criteria.

## Interpretation

This paper establishes empirical spectral characteristics that distinguish English fricatives by place of articulation. The key insight for synthesis is that fricative identity resides primarily in the gross spectral shape — specifically the frequency location and relative prominence of the spectral peak — rather than in fine spectral detail. The three-measurement classification scheme (high-frequency ratio, low-frequency contribution, peak prominence) provides concrete frequency-band boundaries and dB thresholds that can be used to verify whether a synthesizer's frication output has the right spectral balance for each fricative class. The finding that voiced and unvoiced fricatives are spectrally equivalent above 1 kHz simplifies the synthesis problem: the voicing distinction can be handled solely by adding or removing the voicing source, without changing the frication spectral shape.
