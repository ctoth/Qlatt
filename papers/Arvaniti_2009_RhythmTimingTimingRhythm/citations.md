## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Klatt, D.H. (1976).** Linguistic uses of segmental duration in English. J. acoust. Soc. Am. 59: 1208-1221. — Cited as the review of the factors that determine English segment duration. This is the canonical duration-rule source for a Klatt-style formant synthesizer, and the paper's argument implies duration must be modeled as this explicit inventory rather than as a single variability parameter.

2. **Ramus, F.; Nespor, M.; Mehler, J. (1999).** Correlates of linguistic rhythm in the speech signal. Cognition 73: 265-292. — Origin of %V and ΔC and of the controlled corpus reproduced in figure 1. Needed to see the metric definitions the present paper only describes verbally.

3. **Grabe, E.; Low, E.L. (2002).** Acoustic correlates of rhythm class. Laboratory Phonology 7: 515-546. — Origin of nPVI/rPVI and the 18-language dataset behind figure 2 and tables on pp.56-57. The source of every correlation and score difference Arvaniti recomputes.

4. **Dauer, R.M. (1983).** Stress-timing and syllable-timing reanalyzed. J. Phonet. 11: 51-62. — Supplies the interstress interval means (380-493 ms), the speaking rates (5.0-7.5 syll/s), and the stressed/unstressed duration ratios (1.3 Spanish, 1.5 English) that the positive proposal rests on.

5. **Dilley, L.C.; McAuley, J.D. (2008).** Distal prosodic context affects word segmentation and lexical processing. J. Mem. Lang. 59: 294-311. — Demonstrates that earlier prosody changes how listeners segment later words. Directly relevant to how a synthesizer's phrase-initial rhythm propagates into downstream parsing.

6. **Sluijter, A.M.C.; van Heuven, V.J. (1996).** Spectral balance as an acoustic correlate of linguistic stress. J. acoust. Soc. Am. 100: 2471-2485. — Spectral tilt as the primary stress cue in Dutch; actionable for driving glottal source parameters from stress in a source-filter synthesizer.
