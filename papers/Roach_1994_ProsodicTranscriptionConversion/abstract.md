# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

Although systems for the transcription of speech prosody have existed for a long time, the need to represent prosodic information in large-scale speech databases places new demands on such systems. The ToBI system recently developed in the USA differs in many interesting ways from conventional systems such as the "Standard British" system that has been in use for several decades. This paper discusses the differences in the context of current research on a machine-readable corpus of spoken English, and examines the possibility of converting automatically between the two types of transcription.

---

## Our Interpretation

The paper tackles a practical interoperability problem: the Spoken English Corpus (SEC) uses British intonation notation while modern speech technology standardizes on ToBI. Roach builds mapping tables and a conversion program, finding that most tones convert cleanly but non-final fall-rises expose a genuine gap between the systems. For Qlatt, this provides a concrete reference for how British-style prosodic labels relate to the ToBI framework already used in our prosodic annotation pipeline.
