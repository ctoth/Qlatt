Cooper et al. (1952) present systematic perception experiments using the Haskins pattern playback to determine the acoustic cues for stop consonant place of articulation, testing burst frequency position and second-formant transition direction across seven vowel contexts with over 30 listeners per experiment. The key findings establish that high-frequency bursts cue /t/, low-frequency bursts cue /p/, bursts near the vowel's F2 cue /k/, and that rising F2 transitions signal labial place while falling transitions signal velar place, with these burst and transition cues functioning as complementary trading cues that operate on the consonant-vowel syllable as a perceptual unit. This foundational work directly informs Qlatt's stop consonant synthesis rules by validating context-dependent burst frequency placement, vowel-dependent F2 transition targets, and the dual-cue (burst + transition) architecture used in the cascade/parallel formant synthesizer.
