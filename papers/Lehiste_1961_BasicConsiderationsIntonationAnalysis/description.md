Lehiste and Peterson (1961) present a detailed acoustic analysis of one intonation contour in American English, using 1263 CNC words in a carrier frame spoken by one speaker plus 350 utterances by five speakers. The study establishes that each vowel has a characteristic intrinsic fundamental frequency (high vowels /i,u/ highest, open vowels /ae,A/ lowest), that voiceless initial consonants raise F0 on the following vowel while voiced consonants lower it, and that final consonants have no systematic effect on the preceding vowel's F0 peak. The paper also demonstrates that intonation levels are not based on fixed musical intervals and that the low target of a falling contour is stable regardless of vowel quality, while the peak varies with intrinsic vowel F0.
