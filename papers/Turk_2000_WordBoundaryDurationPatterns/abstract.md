# Turk & Shattuck-Hufnagel 2000 — Abstract

## Verbatim Abstract

Segments and syllables with identical distinctive feature specifications in the lexicon can be produced with strikingly different durations in different contexts, depending for example on the location of a lexical word boundary. In a study of 11 triads like *tune acquire*, *tuna choir* and *tune a choir*, durational differences between, e.g., *tune acquire* and *tuna choir* occurred on either side of the word boundary in a variety of prominence contexts, broadly confirming earlier findings in the literature for a more limited set of stimuli. In general, duration measurements for *tune#a#choir* tokens were found to be intermediate between those of *tune#acquire* and *tuna#choir*, suggesting that boundaries at function words are weaker than boundaries between two content words. In addition, an investigation of the distribution of durational differences across and within syllables was conducted to evaluate five duration adjustment mechanisms proposed in the literature. Support was found for word-initial lengthening, polysyllabic shortening, accentual lengthening, and syllable ratio equalization, but not for word-final lengthening.

## Interpretation

This paper directly challenges the common assumption in TTS duration models that word-final segments should be lengthened. The controlled experimental design -- using triads where the same phoneme sequence spans different word boundary configurations -- provides strong evidence that what looks like word-final lengthening is actually the combined effect of word-initial lengthening on the following word, polysyllabic shortening differences between words of different lengths, and vowel nucleus equalization within words. For Qlatt's duration rules, this means: (1) do not implement word-final lengthening as a duration rule, (2) do implement word-initial lengthening on onset consonants, (3) polysyllabic shortening should be symmetric across all syllables in a word, and (4) function word boundaries should receive attenuated duration effects compared to content word boundaries. Note that phrase-final and utterance-final lengthening (driven by prosodic phrase boundaries) are separate phenomena not addressed here.
