This paper reviews and extends acoustic models of nonmodal phonation by linking four glottal configurations (modal, posterior opening, nonsimultaneous closure, combined) to five corrected spectral measures (H1*-H2*, H1*-A1, H1*-A3*, B1, noise ratings), validating predictions against data from 43 normal adult speakers and describing HLsyn, a 13-parameter quasi-articulatory controller for the Klatt synthesizer. Key findings include a 23 dB H1*-A3* threshold that divides female speakers into two voice quality groups, demonstration that reduced vowels exhibit 7-13 dB greater spectral tilt than stressed vowels (indicating prosody-dependent glottal configuration changes), and successful copy synthesis of nonmodal phenomena including irregular vibration and phrase-final breathiness. For Klatt synthesis, this work provides the theoretical bridge between quasi-articulatory voice quality control parameters (subglottal pressure, glottal area, posterior opening) and low-level Klatt parameters (AV, AH, TL, B1, OQ, DI), enabling principled voice quality variation across speakers, genders, and prosodic contexts.
