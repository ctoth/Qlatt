Nakata (1959) demonstrates that a cascade terminal-analog synthesizer can produce perceptually acceptable nasal consonants (/m/, /n/, /ng/) by widening the first formant bandwidth to ~300 Hz without requiring antiresonances, establishing that spectral zeros are not essential for nasal identification. Through systematic listening tests with synthetic CV syllables, the study shows that the second formant locus is the primary cue for distinguishing nasal place of articulation, with /m/ associated with low F2 loci (~1100 Hz), /n/ with medium (~1700 Hz), and /ng/ with high (~2300 Hz). The paper also documents effects of consonant duration, transition duration, and vowel context on nasal identification, providing specific parameter values directly applicable to formant synthesizer implementations.
