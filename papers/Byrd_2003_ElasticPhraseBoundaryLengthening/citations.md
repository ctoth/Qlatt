## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Edwards, Beckman, & Fletcher (1991)** - The articulatory kinematics of final lengthening. Primary empirical source for boundary lengthening kinematics: increased duration, decreased peak velocity, larger spatial displacement. Directly relevant to validating Qlatt's duration multiplier values.

2. **Byrd & Saltzman (1998)** - Intragestural dynamics of multiple phrasal boundaries. Provides empirical data on how lengthening scales with boundary strength (multiple boundaries at different prosodic levels), directly informing the mapping from boundary type to duration multiplier magnitude.

3. **Klatt (1976)** - Structure of a phonological rule component. The original rule-based duration model that Qlatt's duration.yaml extends. Byrd & Saltzman explicitly contrast their dynamical approach with Klatt's extrinsic timing model.

4. **Cho (2001)** - Effects of prosody on articulation in English. Comprehensive PhD dissertation on boundary effects in English articulation, providing detailed measurements of initial strengthening and final lengthening across boundary types.

5. **Cambier-Langeveld (1997)** - The domain of final lengthening in Dutch. Key empirical finding that lengthening magnitude increases with boundary strength but the domain (extent) of lengthening does not change, supporting the pi-gesture model where alpha scales but duration of the pi-gesture is fixed.
