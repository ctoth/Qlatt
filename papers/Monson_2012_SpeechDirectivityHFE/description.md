This paper uses simultaneous multi-channel recordings from 13 horizontally arrayed microphones to measure the directivity of speech and singing across the full audible bandwidth (125 Hz to 16 kHz octave bands) for 15 standing subjects, finding that high-frequency energy (HFE) is highly directional with front-to-back attenuation exceeding 26 dB in the 8 and 16 kHz octave bands. The most significant finding is that voiceless fricatives exhibit large, phoneme-specific directivity differences: /s/ (DI=5.3 dB) and /sh/ (DI=4.6 dB) are substantially more directional than /f/ (DI=3.2 dB) and /h/ (DI=2.5 dB), with differences approaching 9 dB at off-axis angles in the 8 kHz band, while gender, production level, and speech-vs-singing mode produce only minor directivity variations (<3 dB). For synthesis, this provides empirical confirmation that the spectral energy distribution differences between fricative places of articulation (/s/ concentrated at 8 kHz, /sh/ at 4 kHz, /f/ broadband) are robust acoustic features, and supplies complete octave-band SPL tables at all angles that could inform radiation filter design for spatially aware synthesis.
