## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Deligne, S., Bimbot, F. (1995, 1997)** — the original multigram formulation and the EM / forward-backward inference of variable-length units from unaligned data. This is the direct ancestor of the estimation algorithm; read it to understand what the discounted-EM contribution actually adds.
- **Chen, S. F. (2003)** — conditional and joint maximum-entropy models for G2P. The closest competitor at nearly identical accuracy but higher computational cost; the reference point for whether max-ent is worth the complexity.
- **Kneser, R., Ney, H. (1995)** and **Chen, S. F., Goodman, J. (1999)** — the smoothing results that discounted evidence adapts from integer counts to fractional evidence. Essential background for implementing equations 15 through 18 correctly.
- **Sejnowski, T. J., Rosenberg, C. R. (1987)** — NETtalk. The origin of data-driven G2P and the source of the corpus used throughout this paper's ablations.
- **Galescu, L., Allen, J. F. (2001, 2002)** — bidirectional joint n-gram grapheme/phoneme conversion, the comparison point for the phoneme-to-grapheme results and a simpler variant that disallows null grapheme units in exchange for a simple dynamic-programming decoder.
- **Ziegenhain, U. (2005)** and **Bisani, M., Jolles, F., Popovic, M. (2005)** — the LC-Star German lexicon, the production project where stress and syllabification markers were carried through the phoneme inventory and where the confidence measures were evaluated.
