# Morphology and development of the human vocal tract: A study using magnetic resonance imaging

**Authors:** W. Tecumseh Fitch, Jay Giedd
**Year:** 1999
**Venue:** Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 106(3), 1511-1522
**DOI:** 10.1121/1.427148

## One-Sentence Summary
Provides MRI-derived normative data on vocal tract length (VTL) and segment proportions across age (2-25 years) and sex in 129 subjects, establishing quantitative relationships between body size, VTL, and the sex-specific pharyngeal elongation that occurs at male puberty.

## Problem Addressed
Prior to this study, the relationship between vocal tract morphology, body size, age, and sex had been predicted on theoretical grounds (Fant, 1966; Peterson & Barney, 1952) but never directly measured in a large sample. Previous MRI and radiographic studies used small samples (1-5 subjects) and focused on adults. No normative dataset existed for children's vocal tract dimensions, and the specific anatomical basis of male-female VTL differences was unresolved.

## Key Contributions
- First large-scale (N=129) MRI study of human vocal tract morphology spanning ages 2-25
- Establishes strong positive correlation between VTL and body size (height: r=0.926, adj. r^2=0.86; log weight: r=0.941, adj. r^2=0.89)
- Demonstrates that male-female VTL difference arises at puberty and is primarily due to disproportionate pharyngeal lengthening in males
- Provides normative VTL and segment length data by age group and sex (Tables I-V)
- Shows vocal tract grows allometrically with body size (VTL scales as cube root of body mass)
- Identifies that oral:pharyngeal ratio changes with development, with pharynx growing disproportionately

## Methodology
- 129 midsagittal MRI scans (53 females, 76 males, ages 2.8-25.1)
- Part of NIH brain development study (Giedd et al., 1996)
- GE 1.5T scanner, sagittal spin-echo, 1.5mm slices, TR=500ms, TE=16ms
- Subjects supine during quiet respiration (not speech)
- Computer-graphic measurement of vocal tract using custom Macintosh software
- Two measurement techniques: "curved line" (along midline) and "line segment" (straight segments between landmarks)
- Five vocal tract segments measured: lip, tongue blade, tongue dorsum, velum, pharynx
- Measurement reliability: standard errors 0.23-0.46 mm (mean 0.32 mm), max deviation <3% of total length

## Key Equations

### VTL vs Height regression
$$\text{VTL} = 0.68 \times \text{Height(cm)} + \text{intercept}$$
Linear regression: $r = 0.926$, adj. $r^2 = 0.86$, $p < 0.0001$ (N=121)

Note: slope of 0.68 means VTL does not increase as rapidly as body size (child's head is disproportionately large).

### VTL vs Body Mass (allometric)
$$\log_{10}(\text{VTL}) = 0.318 \times \log_{10}(\text{body mass in g}) + \text{intercept}$$
RMA slope = 0.318, which is close to the expected 1/3 power law (cube root) for isometric scaling.

$N = 129$, $r = 0.941$, adj. $r^2 = 0.89$, $p < 0.0001$

### Key Derived Values
- Adult male mean VTL: ~155 mm (postpubertal)
- Adult female mean VTL: ~139-146 mm (postpubertal)
- Postpubertal sex difference: 12.9 mm on average (p<0.0001)
- Pharynx accounts for most of the sex difference (~6.8 mm pharynx difference vs 0.7-1.6 mm lip difference)
- Oral segments (lip, blade, dorsum) enlarge 6-14% between childhood and puberty
- Pharynx increases 22% between childhood and puberty
- Pharynx increases another 25% between puberty and adulthood

## Implementation Details

### Vocal Tract Length Estimation from Body Size
For a synthesizer that needs VTL as input for formant scaling:
1. If height is known: `VTL_mm = 0.68 * height_cm + intercept` (use group-specific intercept from Table V)
2. If weight is known: `VTL_mm = 10^(0.318 * log10(weight_g) + intercept)`
3. For children (prepubertal): use the "Children" regression line
4. For adult females: use the "Females" regression line
5. For adult males: use the "Males" regression line (steeper slope reflects pubertal pharyngeal elongation)

### Segment Proportions for Tube Model
The five-segment model (lip, blade, dorsum, velum, pharynx) can be used to derive area function proportions:
- Child (age 5): pharynx is 29% of total VTL
- Adult male: pharynx is 38% of total VTL
- Adult female: pharynx is 34% of total VTL
- The oral-to-pharyngeal ratio shifts significantly with age and sex

### Tanner Stage Grouping
- Prepubertal: Tanner stage 1-2, mean age <10.3 years (N=54)
- Peripubertal: Tanner stage 2-4, age 10.3-14.5 years (N=39)
- Postpubertal: Tanner stage 5, age 14.7-25.1 years (N=33)

### ANCOVA Results
- Height explains most VTL variance (F=71.7, p<0.001)
- Age adds significant variance beyond height (F=16.9, p<0.001)
- Sex adds further variance beyond height+age (F=14, p=0.0003)
- No significant interactions among height, age, sex

### MANOVA on Segment Lengths (Table IV)
Only lip and pharynx show significant sex effects:
- Lip: Sex F=7.91, p=0.006
- Pharynx: Sex F=8.42, p=0.004
- Pharynx: Age group * Sex interaction F=7.96, p=0.0006 (the key finding)

## Results Summary
1. VTL correlates strongly with body size (r~0.9) regardless of whether height or weight is used
2. The relationship is allometric: VTL scales approximately as the cube root of body mass (slope ~0.318)
3. Before puberty, no significant sex differences in VTL exist
4. At puberty, males develop disproportionately longer pharynx (secondary "descent of the larynx")
5. The postpubertal male VTL averages 12.9 mm longer than female, primarily from pharyngeal elongation
6. Oral cavity segments grow roughly uniformly; pharynx grows disproportionately at each developmental transition
7. The oral-to-pharyngeal ratio is a key sex-differentiating feature in adults

## Limitations
- Subjects were supine (quiet respiration), not upright and speaking — VTL may differ during speech due to lip/larynx movement
- Supine position may cause dorsal shift of hyolaryngeal complex, systematically shortening measured VTL
- Cross-sectional design (not longitudinal), so individual growth trajectories not tracked
- No acoustic measurements were made (morphology only)
- VTL measured to bottom of glottis; Fant's (1960) VTL includes "larynx cavity" below glottis, adding ~1 cm
- 2D midsagittal measurements only — no volumetric or cross-sectional area data

## Testable Properties
- VTL must increase monotonically with height across the population (r > 0.8)
- log VTL must scale linearly with log body mass, with slope near 0.33 (cube root)
- For prepubertal subjects, male and female VTL should not differ significantly when controlling for body size
- For postpubertal subjects, males must have significantly longer VTL than females (~13 mm difference)
- Pharynx must show the largest absolute growth of any segment between pre- and postpubertal stages
- Pharynx segment must show significant sex*age interaction; other segments (blade, dorsum) should not
- The oral:pharyngeal ratio must be higher in children than adults, and higher in adult females than adult males
- Adult male pharynx length (~55-60 mm) must exceed adult female pharynx length (~47-53 mm)
- "Curved line" and "line segment" VTL measures must correlate at r > 0.98

## Relevance to Project
This paper provides the empirical foundation for parameterizing vocal tract length in the Qlatt speaker profile system. The regression equations allow VTL to be derived from speaker body size parameters (height or weight). The segment-level data enables proper scaling of individual tube sections (oral vs pharyngeal) when modeling different speaker types, which is critical because the oral:pharyngeal ratio directly affects formant frequencies (particularly F3 and F4 spacing). The pubertal-stage data establishes that child vs adult voice differences are not simply a uniform scaling — the pharynx grows disproportionately, particularly in males, which means formant patterns for male speakers cannot be derived by simply scaling female patterns.

### Conceptual Links (not citation-based)
**Vocal anatomy and sex differences:**
- [[Zhang_2021_LaryngealSizeSexDifferences]] - This paper documents the supralaryngeal (vocal tract) sex differences while Zhang documents the laryngeal (vocal fold) sex differences. Together they provide the complete anatomical basis for male-female voice differences: Fitch shows the pharyngeal elongation that lowers formant frequencies, Zhang shows the fold length/thickness differences that lower F0 and alter voice quality. Both find that sex differences emerge at puberty and go beyond simple body-size scaling.
- [[Stathopoulos_2011_VoiceAcrossLifespan]] - Stathopoulos provides the acoustic measurements (F0, SPL, SNR) across the full lifespan (ages 4-93) that should correlate with the anatomical growth curves documented here. Fitch's VTL data covers ages 2-25; Stathopoulos extends the acoustic picture through aging and decline.
- [[Iseli_2007_VoiceSourceAgeSexVowel]] - Iseli documents age/sex effects on voice source parameters (H1*-H2*, H1*-A3*) in speakers ages 8-39, providing the source-side complement to this paper's filter-side (vocal tract) age/sex data.

**Formant-body size perception:**
- [[Barreda_2015_FormantSpeakerSize]] - Barreda tests the perceptual consequences of the VTL-body size correlation documented here, finding that listeners use formant frequencies (including F4, F5) for speaker-size judgments but with phoneme-specific biases, suggesting the mapping from VTL to perceived size is not straightforward uniform scaling.
- [[Cartei_2014_VoiceMasculinity]] - Cartei shows that perceived masculinity is mediated by both F0 and formant spacing (deltaF), with F0 as the stronger cue. This paper provides the anatomical explanation: the male pharyngeal elongation produces the lower formant spacing, while the laryngeal changes produce lower F0.
