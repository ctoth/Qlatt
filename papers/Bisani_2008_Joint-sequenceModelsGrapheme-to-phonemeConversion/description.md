This paper gives a complete, self-contained specification of joint-sequence (graphone) models for grapheme-to-phoneme conversion, covering the M-gram model over joint letter/phoneme units, a novel discounted-EM training algorithm that folds Kneser-Ney smoothing into the EM loop, exact best-first decoding, n-best generation and posterior confidence measures. Singular graphones combined with an 8- or 9-gram joint sequence model give the best accuracy on every test set, beating or matching all prior published results, with phoneme error rates of 2.50% on Celex, 5.88% on CMUdict, 0.28% on German LexDb and 1.18% on French Brulex. For a formant synthesizer with a text front-end this is the reference recipe for turning arbitrary orthography into the phoneme sequence the synthesizer needs, including how to carry lexical stress and syllable boundaries through the model and how to use the posterior probability to decide when to trust a generated pronunciation.
