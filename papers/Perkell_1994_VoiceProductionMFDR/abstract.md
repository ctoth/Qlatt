# Abstract

## Full Abstract

Aerodynamic and acoustic voice measurements from Holmberg, Hillman, and Perkell (1988) and Holmberg, Hillman, Perkell, Guiod, and Goldman (1993) studies of normal speakers are compared. Both studies used similar measurement paradigms to extract measures such as sound pressure level, fundamental frequency, maximum airflow declination rate (MFDR), and other parameters from glottal airflow waveforms derived by inverse filtering a high-time-resolution oral airflow signal. Overall group differences and differences for values of several individual parameters between the 1988 and 1993 studies are reported. Some of the variation resulted from improvements in methodology; notably, some lower 1988 values of maximum flow declination rate were due at least in part to excessive low-pass filtering in the 1988 algorithm. The authors concluded that these differences had minimal impact on their conclusions regarding voice disorder research.

## Interpretation

This is a short methodological paper (4 pages) that serves as an erratum/revision for the MFDR values reported in Holmberg et al. 1988. The key finding is that the 1988 inverse-filtering algorithm used excessive low-pass filtering, which attenuated the sharp negative peak of the flow derivative and thus underestimated MFDR. The 1993 study (Holmberg et al. 1995) used a revised algorithm with less aggressive filtering, yielding higher MFDR values. The paper provides corrected group-mean values and demonstrates that the core conclusions about male-female differences and loudness effects remain valid despite the numerical revisions.

For the Qlatt project, this means the MFDR values from Holmberg 1995 (female: 190.8 l/s/s comfortable, 421.4 l/s/s loud) should be preferred over the 1988 values (female normal: 164.0 l/s/s from the 1988 extraction, though note the 1988 paper only reported data for 20 females in normal voice). For males, the 1988 values (normal: 279.6 l/s/s, loud: 481.1 l/s/s) may also be somewhat underestimated due to the same filtering artifact.
