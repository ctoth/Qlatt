# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

Synthetic methods applied to isolated syllables have permitted a systematic exploration of the acoustic cues to the perception of some of the consonant sounds. Methods, results, and working hypotheses are discussed.

---

## Our Interpretation

This paper uses the Haskins Laboratories pattern playback -- a device that converts hand-painted spectrographic patterns into sound -- to systematically test which acoustic features (burst frequency, formant transition direction and extent) allow listeners to identify stop consonants (p, t, k, b, d, g) and nasal consonants (m, n, ng) in synthetic CV syllables. The central finding is that consonant place of articulation is cued by both burst spectral position and second-formant transition direction, with these cues interacting with the following vowel context and functioning as complementary trading relations. This work is foundational for speech synthesis because it establishes the perceptual requirements that any synthesizer must meet to produce identifiable stop and nasal consonants.
