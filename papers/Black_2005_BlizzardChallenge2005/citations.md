## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **[14] Bennett (2005), "Large scale evaluation of corpus-based synthesizers"** — the companion results paper. Black and Tokuda defer every number, scoring procedure, and statistical treatment to it. Highest priority follow-up for anyone building an evaluation methodology.
- **[13] Benoit, Grice & Hazan (1996), "The SUS test," Speech Communication 18:381-392** — the semantically unpredictable sentence method, used verbatim for one of the two intelligibility genres. Required reading to construct a valid SUS set.
- **[12] Logan, Greene & Pisoni (1989), JASA 86(2):566-581** — segmental intelligibility of rule-based (formant) synthesis via DRT/MRT. Doubly relevant: it is the intelligibility tradition Blizzard adapts, and it measures exactly the class of synthesizer this project builds.
- **[3] Kominek & Black (2003), CMU ARCTIC databases, CMU-LTI-03-177** — the corpus design report, including the greedy maximum-diphone-coverage sentence selection run twice to produce balanced A and B sets.
- **[11] Taylor, Black & Caley (2001), Speech Communication 33:153-174** — heterogeneous relation graphs, the Festival utterance structure used to distribute phonetic labels and test-sentence analyses.
