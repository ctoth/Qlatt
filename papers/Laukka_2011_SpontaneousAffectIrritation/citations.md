## Key Citations for Follow-up

**1. Scherer, K.R. (1986, 2003)** — "Vocal affect expression" papers provide comprehensive theoretical framework for linking vocal acoustic features to emotional states and establish the dominant role of voice quality, prosody, and speaking style in emotional expression. Essential reading for understanding which synthesis parameters (F0, formant bandwidth, spectral tilt, speech rate) drive emotional perception.

**2. Laukka, P., Juslin, P.N., & Bresin, R. (2005)** — "A dimensional approach to vocal expression of emotion" provides quantitative dimensional acoustic targets for mapping emotional states to synthesis parameters, directly applicable to implementing emotion presets in Klatt-based TTS systems.

**3. Banse, R., & Scherer, K.R. (1996)** — "Acoustic profiles in vocal emotion expression" presents comprehensive empirical measurements of 29 acoustic parameters across 14 distinct emotions, establishing concrete F0, formant, spectral, and timing targets for emotional speech synthesis including ranges for anger, fear, sadness, and other emotions.

**4. Burkhardt, F., Ajmera, J., et al. (2006)** — "Detecting anger in automated voice portal dialogs" demonstrates practical application of emotion recognition in human-computer interaction and validates that certain emotions (particularly anger) are reliably encoded in specific acoustic patterns that can be systematically synthesized.

**5. Juslin, P.N., & Laukka, P. (2003)** — "Communication of emotions in vocal expression and music performance: different channels, same code?" establishes cross-domain principles for emotional acoustics showing that similar acoustic patterns convey emotions across both speech and music, suggesting that unified emotion synthesis rules could apply across multiple modalities.
