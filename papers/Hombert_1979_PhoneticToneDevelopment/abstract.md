# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

The development of contrastive tone because of the articulatory reinterpretation of segmentally-caused perturbations in intrinsic fundamental frequency is well attested in a number of unrelated languages. Considering the wide-spread character of this process, it is likely that its 'seeds' can be found in the functioning of the human articulatory and/or auditory mechanisms. This paper reviews what the authors consider promising explanations for well-attested tonal sound patterns, e.g. tone originating from the effect of prevocalic stop consonants or postvocalic glottal consonants, and tone rarely or never originating from the influence of postvocalic non-glottal consonants or from vowel height.

---

## Our Interpretation

This paper investigates why tone systems develop in languages that lose voicing distinctions on consonants, providing quantitative acoustic measurements showing that voiceless stops raise F0 of following vowels by approximately 10 Hz relative to voiced stops, and this perturbation persists for 100+ ms in English. The key finding for speech synthesis is that the F0 perturbation magnitude is approximately 8% of baseline F0 for male speakers, with a perceptual detection threshold requiring at least 60 ms of slope duration at 10 Hz magnitude. This empirical data directly informs the F0 microprosody rules in formant synthesizers that model consonant-induced pitch perturbations.
