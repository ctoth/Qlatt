# Female voice frequency in the context of dominance and attractiveness perception

**Authors:** Barbara Borkowska, Boguslaw Pawlowski
**Year:** 2011
**Venue:** Animal Behaviour, Vol. 82, 55-59
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.anbehav.2011.03.024

## One-Sentence Summary
Demonstrates that female F0 predicts dominance linearly (lower = more dominant) but predicts attractiveness nonlinearly (increasing up to ~280 Hz then decreasing), establishing an optimal pitch for female vocal attractiveness.

## Problem Addressed
Previous studies on female voice pitch and attractiveness used narrow F0 ranges or paired comparisons that could not detect nonlinear relationships. This study uses a wider F0 range (184.6-310.3 Hz) with ±20 Hz manipulations to test whether F0-dominance and F0-attractiveness relationships are linear or nonlinear.

## Key Contributions
- F0-dominance relationship is LINEAR and negative: lower F0 = more dominant, across entire tested range
- F0-attractiveness relationship is NONLINEAR: attractiveness increases up to ~280 Hz then decreases
- Women are more sensitive than men to dominance cues in female voices (sex interaction F(9,2943) = 2.55, P < 0.01)
- First study to demonstrate an upper F0 threshold (~282 Hz) beyond which female voice attractiveness decreases
- Voices above ~310 Hz rated as "babyish" or immature-sounding

## Methodology
- 58 female speakers (Polish), aged 19-25 (mean 22.1 ± 1.3)
- Five vowels recorded: A [a], E [ɛ], I [i], O [ɔ], U [u]
- Four voice categories selected from the 58 speakers:
  - Low (L): 184.6 Hz
  - Medium (M): 223.7 Hz
  - High (H): 261.9 Hz
  - Very High (VH): 310.3 Hz
- Each voice raised and lowered by 20 Hz using Praat PSOLA
- Two unnatural-sounding manipulations excluded (raised VH, lowered L)
- 10 stimuli total rated by listeners
- Attractiveness rated by 144 males (aged 19-27, mean 22.6 ± 1.9) on 7-point Likert scale
- Dominance rated by 189 women (aged 19-26, mean 21.9 ± 1.6) and 140 males (aged 19-26, mean 22.7 ± 1.8) on 7-point scale
- Spearman correlation, Wilcoxon signed-ranks, repeated-measures ANOVA

### Key Statistical Results
- Dominance: Spearman correlation with F0 is negative and significant (linear)
- Dominance sex interaction: F(9,2943) = 2.55, P < 0.01, partial eta-squared = 0.008
- Attractiveness: Spearman r_s = -0.19, N = 144, P = 0.6 (NOT significant — no linear relationship)
- Attractiveness peaks around 262-282 Hz, then drops sharply

## Results Summary
1. **Dominance is linear with F0**: Both men and women rate lower-pitched female voices as more dominant. Lowering voice by 20 Hz significantly increases dominance rating; raising by 20 Hz decreases it.
2. **Women more sensitive to dominance cues**: Sex interaction significant but small (eta² = 0.008). Women rate low-F0 female voices as even more dominant than men do.
3. **Attractiveness is nonlinear**: Peaks around 262-282 Hz. Below this, attractiveness increases with pitch. Above ~282 Hz, attractiveness sharply decreases.
4. **Very high voices (>310 Hz) are unattractive**: Rated lowest on attractiveness (2.28/7). Authors attribute this to "babyish" or immature associations.
5. **Optimal female F0 for attractiveness**: ~280 Hz (approximately 282 Hz from the data pattern).

## Limitations
- Polish speakers only — cultural generalizability unknown
- Only F0 manipulated — no formant, intensity, or voice quality control
- Vowels only (no connected speech)
- Attractiveness rated by males only; dominance by both sexes
- 20 Hz manipulation step size — finer resolution would better locate the attractiveness peak
- PSOLA manipulation may introduce artifacts at extreme shifts
- No hormone or menstrual cycle data collected for female raters

## Testable Properties
- F0-dominance relationship must be monotonically negative across 185-310 Hz range
- F0-attractiveness relationship must be nonlinear with a peak around 260-282 Hz
- Voices above ~310 Hz must be rated lower in attractiveness than voices at ~260-280 Hz
- Lowering F0 by 20 Hz must increase dominance rating
- Raising F0 by 20 Hz must decrease dominance rating

## Relevance to Project
For the Qlatt speaker personality system, this paper establishes that female voice dominance and attractiveness require different F0 targets:
- **Dominant female preset**: F0 should be low (~185-200 Hz), well below the female average
- **Attractive female preset**: F0 should target ~260-280 Hz, NOT the lowest possible pitch
- The nonlinear attractiveness function means a simple "lower pitch = more attractive" rule (valid for males per Hodges-Simeon 2010) does NOT apply to female voices
- Combined with Hodges-Simeon 2010 (male voices), this establishes sex-specific voice parameter mappings for social perception
