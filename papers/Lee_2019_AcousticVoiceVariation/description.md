Lee, Keating, and Kreiman (2019) use principal component analysis on 26 acoustic voice quality measures (from a psychoacoustic model) sampled every 5 ms on vowels/approximants across 100 speakers recorded over three days. They find that within-speaker and between-speaker acoustic voice spaces share the same primary structure: the balance between higher-frequency harmonic and inharmonic energy (related to breathiness/brightness) accounts for the most variance, followed by higher formant frequencies and formant dispersion. Beyond these shared dimensions, individual voice variability is largely idiosyncratic, consistent with prototype-based models of voice perception.
