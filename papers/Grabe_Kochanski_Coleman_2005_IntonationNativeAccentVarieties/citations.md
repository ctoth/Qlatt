## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Grabe, Kochanski & Coleman (to appear), "Quantitative Modelling of Intonational Variation," SASRTLM 2003** - the companion in-press paper providing the actual data behind this chapter's claim (Section 4, p.23) that overall F0 pitch level, not tune shape, is the more reliable statement/question cue. Not yet in this collection; would add a pitch-register feature independent of the categorical AM tune tables already extracted here.
- **Grabe, Post, Nolan & Farrar (2000), Journal of Phonetics 28** - the primary IViE truncation/compression paper; still not retrieved for this collection (paywalled, see collection notes).
- **Jenkins, J. (2000), "The Phonology of English as an International Language"** - source of the Lingua Franca Core pedagogical framework this chapter's recommendations extend; worth reading for the fuller list of features considered "core" vs. dispensable for intelligibility.
- **'t Hart, Collier & Cohen (1990), "A Perceptual Study of Intonation"** - source of the "flat hat" terminology and the IPO perceptual approach to intonation, relevant for cross-checking the Cambridge/Newcastle shared wh-question pattern against a different theoretical framework.
- **Ladd, D.R. (1996), "Intonational phonology"** - foundational source for both the prenuclear-accent-collapsing convention used in this paper's counting method and for the broader theoretical treatment of what prenuclear accents contribute to meaning.
