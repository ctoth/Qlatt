# Klofstad, Anderson & Peters (2012) - Voice Pitch and Leadership

**Citation:** Klofstad CA, Anderson RC, Peters S (2012) Sounds like a winner: voice pitch influences perception of leadership capacity in both men and women. Proc. R. Soc. B 279, 2698-2704. doi:10.1098/rspb.2012.0311

## Experimental Design

### Stimuli
- 17 women (age 21-60, mean 31) and 10 men (age 20-55, mean 33)
- Utterance: "I urge you to vote for me this November"
- Recorded in anechoic chamber, Shure SM57, 44.1 kHz
- **Pitch manipulation: +/-0.5 ERB** (~20 Hz perceived shift)
  - ERB used instead of Hz because pitch perception is logarithmic
  - Produces constant perceivable gap regardless of original F0

### Speaker F0 Ranges
- Female voices: 162-207 Hz (mean 187 Hz)
- Male voices: 91-116 Hz (mean 107 Hz)

## Key Results

### Election Experiments: Both Sexes Prefer Lower-Pitched Leaders

**Female candidates:**
- Male listeners voted for lower pitch: t(36)=-3.44, p<.01
- Female listeners voted for lower pitch: t(45)=-2.87, p<.01

**Male candidates:**
- Male listeners voted for lower pitch: t(48)=-2.29, p=.03
- Female listeners voted for lower pitch: t(39)=-3.46, p<.01

### Trait Perception Results (Table 1)

Proportion choosing lower-pitched voice as having the trait:

| Trait | Female voices (M listeners) | Female voices (F listeners) | Male voices (M listeners) | Male voices (F listeners) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Competent** | **0.70**** | **0.72**** | **0.58*** | 0.55 n.s. |
| **Strong** | **0.73**** | **0.69**** | **0.62*** | 0.56 n.s. |
| **Trustworthy** | **0.68**** | **0.73**** | 0.56 n.s. | 0.50 n.s. |

(* p<0.05, ** p<0.01, compared to 0.50 chance)

### Key Asymmetries
1. **Female voice pitch effects are STRONGER than male**: both sexes rate lower-pitched female voices as more competent, stronger, AND more trustworthy
2. **Male voice trustworthiness**: NOT affected by pitch (neither male nor female listeners discriminate)
3. **Female listeners don't discriminate male voices** on competence or strength - possibly because any male voice in normal range is "low enough"
4. **Male listeners DO discriminate male voices** on strength and competence - likely male-male competition context

## Perception Validation
Listeners reliably detected the +/-0.5 ERB pitch difference:
- Female voices: men t(17)=15.29, p<.01; women t(44)=18.21, p<.01
- Male voices: men t(14)=14.09, p<.01; women t(14)=37.04, p<.01

## Implementation Relevance
- For "leader" voice profile: lower pitch by ~0.5 ERB (~20 Hz perceived) from baseline
- Female leader voice requires LOWER pitch than typical female
- This creates a tension: lower-pitched female voices are perceived as more competent/trustworthy but less attractive (per other literature)
- Male voice: pitch lowering increases perceived strength/competence mainly for male listeners
- The +/-0.5 ERB manipulation is a good reference for the minimum "meaningful" pitch shift
