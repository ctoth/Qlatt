# Abstract

This study deals with the influence of preceding and following consonants on the duration of stressed vowels and diphthongs in American English. A set of 1263 CNC words, pronounced in an identical frame by the same speaker, was analyzed spectrographically, and the influences of various classes of consonants on the duration of the nucleus were determined. The residual durational differences are analyzed as intrinsic durational characteristics, associated with each syllable nucleus. The theory is tested with a set of 30 minimal pairs of CNC words, uttered by five different speakers.

## Interpretation

This paper provides the empirical foundation for vowel duration modeling in speech synthesis. The key quantitative result — that vowels are approximately 50% longer before voiced consonants than voiceless ones — became one of the most widely replicated findings in phonetics and a standard rule in TTS duration models (including Klatt 1976). The intrinsic duration table (Table I) gives baseline values for 15 American English vowel types that can serve as default targets in a synthesizer. The detailed segmentation criteria are also valuable for defining measurement protocols when validating synthesized output against natural speech.
