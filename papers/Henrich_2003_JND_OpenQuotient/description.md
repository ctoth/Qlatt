This paper measures Just Noticeable Differences (JNDs) of glottal open quotient and asymmetry coefficient in singing voice synthesis, testing three Oq values (0.4, 0.6, 0.8) and two asymmetry coefficient values (2/3, 0.8) across conditions of vowel, pitch, vibrato, and amplitude with 27 subjects. The key finding is that the relative JND of open quotient follows Weber's law at approximately 14% for untrained and 10% for trained listeners, while the asymmetry coefficient has a smaller relative JND of ~4% meaning listeners are more sensitive to asymmetry changes, and that amplitude coupling is the dominant factor affecting perceptual sensitivity. These perceptual thresholds are directly applicable to voice quality parameter control in formant synthesizers like Klatt, establishing the minimum meaningful resolution for open quotient and spectral tilt parameters in voice quality presets and providing the perceptual grounding needed to determine when parameter differences between synthesis modes (breathy, modal, tense) will actually be perceived by listeners.
