Maeda (1993) investigates the acoustics of vowel nasalization through simulation using a "port" model where velopharyngeal coupling magnitude (NC) controls the degree of nasal tract influence on the vocal tract transfer function. The study identifies four distinct types of spectral modification depending on vowel identity and proposes the N1-N2 spectral peak distance (in Bark) as a vowel-independent perceptual measure of nasalization. The chapter also explains articulatory shifts in French nasal vowels (/a-tilde/, /open-o-tilde/, /epsilon-tilde/) as acoustically motivated gestures that position antiformants near vowel formants to enhance the nasalization percept.
