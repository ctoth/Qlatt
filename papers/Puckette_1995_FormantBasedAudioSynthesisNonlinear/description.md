Puckette defines the PAF (Phase-Aligned Formant) generator, a fixed-cost time-domain oscillator that produces one formant of directly specified center frequency and bandwidth by waveshaping a half-fundamental sine through 1/(1+z²) and multiplying by a crossfaded pair of adjacent harmonic carriers. Each partial's amplitude is exactly exp(-|ω-ω_c|/δ), giving a symmetric triangular spectrum in decibels, and because all formants share one phase generator their spectra superpose additively, unlike bandpass filters, VOSIM, or FOF; the paper also shows the obvious waveshaping formulation amplifies truncation error by 100x and gives the stable reformulation that does not. For a formant speech synthesizer this is the principal time-domain alternative to a parallel filter bank, with pitch and formant fully decoupled, closed-form amplitudes, and a noise post-operator for unvoiced and breathy formants, demonstrated by resynthesizing a spoken word with six PAF formants.
