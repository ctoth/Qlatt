# Acoustic Correlates of Sexual Orientation and Gender-Role Self-Concept in Women's Speech

## One-Sentence Summary

Large-scale study (n=108) finding no significant acoustic differences between German lesbian and straight women in f0, formants, or vowel space, but demonstrating that within-group variability -- particularly the correlation between gender-role self-concept and median f0 -- is the acoustically meaningful dimension.

## Problem Addressed

Prior studies on acoustic correlates of female sexual orientation yielded inconsistent results: some found straight women had higher mean f0 (Moonwomon-Baird 1997, Camp 2011, van Borsel et al. 2013), while others found no difference (Munson et al. 2006, Rendall et al. 2008). Similarly inconsistent findings for vowel space shift and individual formant values. This study attempted to resolve inconsistencies by using larger samples, finer-grained sexual orientation measurement, and including psychological characteristics as potential mediators.

## Key Contributions

- No significant between-group acoustic differences (f0, formants, vowel space, duration) between lesbians and straight women, even with alternative classification strategies (lesbian vs straight; non-straight vs straight)
- Significant within-group variability: more exclusively lesbian speakers had lower median f0 in both spontaneous and read speech (r = -0.59, p = 0.004; study 1)
- Present gender-role self-concept (TMF scale) correlates with median f0 for lesbians: more feminine = higher f0 (r = 0.48, p = 0.011; study 2, not driven by outliers)
- Childhood gender nonconformity (CGNS) also correlates with f0 for lesbians: more gender-conforming childhood = higher f0 (r = -0.45 to -0.48; study 2)
- Intragroup heterogeneity is more acoustically meaningful than between-group differences

## Methodology

### Study 1 (Jena)
- 51 female speakers, ages 20-30 (M=23.37, SD=2.40)
- 22 lesbian (Kinsey 1-3), 22 straight (Kinsey 5-7), 7 bisexual (excluded from main analysis)
- Recording: AKG C1000S condenser mic, M-AUDIO Fast Track Pro, 44.1 kHz, 16-bit
- Male experimenter
- Read speech: sentence list with abbreviations IAA, BII, LUU to elicit long vowel sequences /a: i: u:/
- Spontaneous speech: route description task

### Study 2 (Berlin)
- 57 female speakers, ages 19-30 (M=23.84, SD=2.40)
- 27 lesbian, 27 straight, 3 bisexual (excluded)
- Same recording setup, female experimenter
- Same speech tasks

### Acoustic Analysis
- Praat LPC formant tracking, F1 and F2 measured at vowel midpoint
- Third recording of sentence list used (most familiar)
- Manual correction of formant tracking errors (especially F2 of /i:/ and /u:/)
- Mean F1, Mean F2 computed across /a: i: u:/ per speaker
- Vowel space dispersion: average Euclidean distance from three vowels to balance point of vowel triangle (Bradlow et al. 1996)
- Median f0 from sentence list and spontaneous speech (Praat autocorrelation)
- All acoustic measures converted to ERB scale (Moore and Glasberg 1983)

### Key Within-Group Correlations

**Study 1 - Lesbian group (n=22): Sexual self-identification vs acoustic parameters**
- Median f0 spontaneous: r = -0.59, p = 0.004
- Median f0 read: r = -0.59, p = 0.004
- Mean F2: r = -0.53, p = 0.011
- F2 /a:/: r = -0.51, p = 0.016
- (Mean F2 correlation driven by F2 /a:/ -- partial correlation non-significant after controlling for it)

**Study 2 - Straight group (n=27): Sexual self-identification vs acoustic parameters**
- Median f0 spontaneous: r = 0.43, p = 0.025
- (More exclusively straight = higher f0)

### TMF (Gender-Role Self-Concept) Correlations with Acoustic Parameters

**Study 1 (overall sample, n=44):**
- Mean F1: r = 0.37, p = 0.014
- Mean F2: r = 0.39, p = 0.009
- F1 /i:/: r = 0.31, p = 0.042
- F2 /i:/: r = 0.38, p = 0.011
- (Mean F1/F2 correlations driven by F1 and F2 of /i:/)
- (Lesbian subsample drove the correlations; straight subsample showed no significant effects)
- (Study 1 correlations were driven by one outlier speaker, case #11)

**Study 2 (overall sample, n=54):**
- Median f0 spontaneous: r = 0.38, p = 0.005
- Median f0 read: r = 0.32, p = 0.018
- (Lesbian subsample: r = 0.48, p = 0.011 for spontaneous f0; NOT driven by outliers)
- (Straight subsample: no significant correlations)

### Childhood Gender Nonconformity (CGNS) Correlations (Study 2, Lesbian group, n=27)
- Median f0 spontaneous: r = -0.45, p = 0.019
- Median f0 read: r = -0.48, p = 0.011
- (More gender-nonconforming childhood = lower f0)

### Cross-Study ANOVA
- Only significant between-sample acoustic difference: F2 of /u:/ (Jena > Berlin; F[1,94]=16.09, p<0.001, eta^2=0.15) -- likely dialect difference
- No significant main effect of sexual orientation on any acoustic parameter
- No significant interaction effects

## Key Equations

No novel equations. Standard ERB conversion (Moore and Glasberg 1983), Euclidean vowel space dispersion (Bradlow et al. 1996).

## Implementation Details

### ERB scale conversion
All formant and f0 values reported in ERB (Equivalent Rectangular Bandwidth) scale rather than Hz, using Moore and Glasberg (1983) formula. This is more perceptually relevant than Hz or Bark.

### Vowel space dispersion calculation
Average Euclidean distance from each of three point vowels (/a: i: u:/) to the centroid of the vowel triangle in F1xF2 space (in ERB). Method from Bradlow et al. (1996).

### Sentence design for vowel elicitation
Abbreviations IAA, BII, LUU embedded in carrier sentences to produce long point vowel sequences minimally affected by neighboring consonants. Junctural glottalization between vowels ensured tight interharmonic spacing, improving formant estimation in higher-pitched female voices (Weirich and Simpson 2013).

## Results Summary

1. **No between-group differences**: Lesbians and straight women did not differ significantly on any of 14 acoustic parameters in either study, regardless of classification strategy
2. **Within-group f0 variability**: Sexual orientation exclusivity correlates with median f0 within groups (more exclusively lesbian = lower f0 in study 1; more exclusively straight = higher f0 in study 2)
3. **Gender-role self-concept predicts f0**: TMF (femininity scale) correlates with median f0, driven by lesbian subsample -- more feminine self-concept = higher f0 (replicated across studies, robust in study 2)
4. **Childhood gender nonconformity predicts f0**: CGNS correlates with f0 for lesbians -- more conforming childhood = higher adult f0
5. **Vowel characteristics less reliable**: Formant and vowel space correlations were inconsistent across studies and sometimes driven by single outliers

## Limitations

- German speakers only -- generalizability to other languages unclear
- Laboratory speech (read + route description) -- may not capture natural variation
- Sample sizes (22-27 per group) limited power to detect small/medium effects (only large effects d=0.80 detectable at power 0.74)
- Experimenters knew speakers' sexual orientation -- possible accommodation effects
- Male experimenter in study 1, female in study 2 -- confounds intragroup correlation differences
- Many exploratory correlations (182-224 tests) without correction -- some findings may be chance

## Relevance to Project

### Primary relevance: Speaker personality / voice preset design
This paper provides evidence that **f0 is a reliable acoustic correlate of gender-role self-concept** (at least for women), independent of biological sex differences. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker personality system:

- **f0 should be the primary knob for gender expression**: The TMF-f0 correlation (r=0.38-0.48) is consistent and replicated. Formant correlations are unreliable.
- **Gender identity is not binary**: The continuous TMF scale and its continuous acoustic correlates suggest voice presets should parameterize gender expression continuously rather than offering "male"/"female" categories.
- **Vowel space is not a reliable indicator**: Between-group and within-group formant differences were inconsistent and often driven by outliers. Do not use vowel space shift for gender expression.

### Secondary relevance: ERB-scale acoustic reference
The study provides ERB-scale means for German female speakers' f0 and formant values that could serve as reference points for German voice presets, though the vowels tested (/a: i: u/) are a limited set.
