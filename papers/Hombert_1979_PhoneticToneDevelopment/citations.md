## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Hombert 1975a** - "Towards a theory of tonogenesis" (UC Berkeley dissertation) - Primary source for the English F0 perturbation data referenced throughout this paper. Most detailed measurements of F0 after voiced/voiceless stops.
- **Halle & Stevens 1971** - "A note on laryngeal features" - Proposes the vocal-cord tension hypothesis for F0 perturbation that this paper evaluates. Important for understanding the physiological mechanism behind F0 microprosody.
- **Ohala 1974b** - "A mathematical model of speech aerodynamics" - Provides the aerodynamic simulation data (Fig. 4) showing pressure and airflow differences between voiced and voiceless stops.
- **Hombert & Ladefoged 1976** - "The effect of aspiration on the fundamental frequency of the following vowel" - Directly relevant to understanding the magnitude of F0 perturbation from aspiration, which may account for the difference between our 20% rule and this paper's 8% finding.
- **Lea 1973** - "Segmental and suprasegmental influences on fundamental frequency contours" - Comprehensive F0 perturbation data that could provide additional quantitative targets for F0 microprosody rules.
