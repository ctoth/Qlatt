## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Ref. 1, Flanagan & Landgraf 1968** - the one-mass self-oscillating source that this paper supersedes. Every criticism in Section II is aimed at it, and the conclusion says a one-mass model with the improved intra-glottal pressure distribution is adequate for normal phonation. Read it to understand the cheaper implementation path.
- **Ref. 10, van den Berg, Zantema & Doornenbal 1957** - the plaster-cast larynx flow measurements that supply the 0.37 inlet contraction loss factor this paper adopts and the 0.5 `P_B` outlet recovery it rejects. The single most load-bearing empirical citation.
- **Ref. 7, Ishizaka & Matsudaira, "Acoustic Theory of a Two-Mass Model of the Vocal Cords"** - the underlying theory for the whole formulation, cited as unpublished. Also ref. 6 (published, 6th ICA 1968) covers related ground.
- **Ref. 12, Flanagan, Speech Analysis Synthesis and Perception, 2nd ed. 1972** - source of the tract transmission-line element values, the radiation load for a piston in an infinite baffle, and the mouth-flow-derivative approximation to radiated pressure. Needed to implement the tract half of the system.
- **Ref. 8, Ishizaka & Kaneko 1968** - static tensile stress versus displacement measurements on an excised human larynx, the origin of the nonlinear spring form and the `eta_k` = 50 to 100 range.
- **Ref. 18, Hixon, Mead & Klatt 1971** - the human transglottal-pressure-to-F0 measurements against which the 2.5 Hz/cm H2O slope is validated.
