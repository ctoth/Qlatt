This paper uses overcomplete Gabor filter dictionaries to characterize the optimal time-frequency decomposition for each American English phoneme, parameterized by a single exponent β that controls the quality factor power law, revealing a structured/non-structured dichotomy: vowels and nasals favor time-localized representations (low β, wide bandwidths) while fricatives favor frequency-localized representations (high β, narrow spectral peaks). Key findings include the biphasic nature of stop releases (burst = time-localized, opening = frequency-localized), the dependence of vowel β on formant bandwidths and lip radiation degree, and a decrease in β with sound intensity that parallels nonlinear cochlear filter broadening. For formant synthesis, this work provides independent validation that bandwidth parameters drive perceptual structure in vowels, confirms the acoustic distinctness of burst vs aspiration phases in stops, and establishes that sibilant fricatives require sharper spectral shaping than non-sibilants.
