This paper is a comprehensive review of voice physiology, biomechanics, and the physics of vocal fold vibration and sound generation, establishing cause-effect relationships between laryngeal anatomy, muscle activation, and voice output characteristics (F0, intensity, voice quality) while critically clarifying misconceptions about the Bernoulli effect and convergent-divergent glottal geometry in phonation. The key contributions include an eigenmode synchronization framework for understanding self-sustained vocal fold vibration, detailed mapping of CT and TA muscle activations to voice parameters, identification of medial surface thickness as the primary voice quality controller, and critical assessment of mechanical (lumped-element vs. continuum) and computational models for voice production and synthesis. For Klatt-based speech synthesis, this review provides essential physiological grounding for voice quality presets, explains why source parameters co-vary (challenging independent parameter control), advocates spectral-domain source models over time-domain approaches, and identifies the two-slope spectral structure (OQ controls low-frequency, Ta controls high-frequency) that maps directly to LF model and Klatt TL parameters.
