Birkholz derives the vocal tract target shape of a consonant before any vowel as a weighted average of three measured, acoustically optimized reference shapes for that consonant in /a/, /i/, and /u/ context, with the weights obtained by projecting the context vowel's own articulatory target into the corner-vowel subspace via an SVD pseudo-inverse and clamping the result to the triangle they span. Lip parameters are interpolated separately from tongue parameters because vowels vary in rounding independently of tongue position; synthesized German CV syllables covering seven consonants and eight long vowels reached 82.4% consonant recognition and 95.4% vowel recognition with 20 listeners. The interpolation scheme is linear algebra over arbitrary parameter vectors and so transfers to a formant synthesizer, and the paper supplies a complete measured German dataset of vowel formant targets, per-consonant onset formants and voice onset times, an RMS relative formant error metric, and a critically damped sixth-order transition model with per-consonant time constants.
