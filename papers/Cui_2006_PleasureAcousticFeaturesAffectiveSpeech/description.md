This paper investigates which acoustic features correlate with the pleasure-displeasure dimension of the PAD emotional space in Mandarin Chinese speech, using correlative coefficients, factor analysis, and co-clustering to select 5 features from an initial set of 76 candidates. The key finding is that spectral features (spectral rolloff and spectral low-high ratio) are the primary acoustic carriers of pleasure/valence, while F0 features (average and minimum F0) primarily encode arousal, with a novel F0 Dominant Ratio feature showing theoretical promise but limited practical benefit in speech conversion experiments. For speech synthesis, this work provides supporting evidence that emotional valence must be controlled through spectral tilt and spectral balance parameters rather than F0 alone, though the study is limited to one male Mandarin speaker and provides preliminary conversion results rather than definitive synthesis parameters.
