## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Cahn, J. E. (1989). Generating expression in synthesized speech. Master's thesis, MIT.** The full-length account behind this paper. It should contain the actual transfer functions from the −10..+10 model parameters to DECtalk3 settings, which this paper describes only qualitatively as one-to-many and many-to-one mappings. Highest-priority retrieval for anyone reimplementing the Affect Editor.
- **Williams, C. E. and Stevens, K. N. (1972). Emotions and speech: Some acoustical correlates. JASA 52(4 Pt 2):1238–1250.** Primary acoustic measurements of emotional speech, and the source of Cahn's tremor/jitter observation in fearful utterances. Directly relevant to a glottal-source jitter model.
- **Williams, C. E. and Stevens, K. N. (1981). Vocal correlates of emotional states.** Supplies the sympathetic/parasympathetic physiological account grounding the entire parameter set, including the loudness, rate, and high-frequency-energy predictions.
- **Davitz, J. (1964). The Communication of Emotional Meaning.** Source of the emotion confusion structure that Cahn's Figure 3 reproduces, and of the observation that pitch range, speech rate, loudness, timbre and enunciation covary as affect changes. Needed to justify or reject parameter dependencies in the model.
- **Pierrehumbert, J. B. (1980). The Phonology and Phonetics of English Intonation. PhD thesis, MIT.** The two-tone, primarily local intonation annotation Cahn names as the fix for DECtalk's whole-utterance stress side effects. Relevant to any intonation layer above a formant synthesizer.
