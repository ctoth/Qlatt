## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Titze and Story (2002)** — "Rules for controlling low-dimensional vocal fold models with muscle activation" — Provides explicit rules mapping laryngeal muscle activation levels to lumped-element model parameters, which would be the most direct path to implementing physiologically-grounded voice control in Qlatt.

2. **Kreiman et al. (2015)** — "Measures of the glottal source spectrum" — Zhang advocates spectral-domain source models as superior to time-domain approaches (like Klatt's); this paper presents the methodology for that approach.

3. **Titze (1988a)** — "The physics of small-amplitude oscillation of the vocal folds" — Derives the phonation threshold pressure theory (Eq. 2 in this review) that is fundamental to understanding voice onset modeling.

4. **Story and Titze (1995)** — "Voice simulation with a body-cover model of the vocal folds" — Three-mass model representing body-cover vocal fold structure; intermediate complexity between simple two-mass and full continuum models.

5. **Flanagan and Landgraf (1968)** — "Self-oscillating source for vocal-tract synthesizers" — The original one-mass vocal fold model; foundational for understanding lumped-element phonation models discussed throughout this review.
