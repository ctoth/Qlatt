## Key Citations for Follow-up

### F0 Generation Algorithms
1. **O'Shaughnessy (1976) - F0_Prosody**: Presents comprehensive rule-based system for generating F0 contours in TTS through High Level System (semantic mapping) and Low Level System (actual F0 values with phonetic adjustments). Essential for implementing F0 generation rules.

2. **Pierrehumbert (1980) - EnglishIntonation**: Foundational phonological and phonetic model based on two tones (H/L) combined as pitch accents, phrase accents, and boundary tones. Provides local implementation rules including downstep, upstep, baseline declination.

### Pitch Accent Realization
3. **Grabe et al. (2000) - Pitch accent realization in four varieties of British English**: Empirical study documenting how pitch accents are realized across different English varieties, essential for generating natural accent patterns.

4. **Grice et al. (1996, 2005)**: Multiple papers on German and Italian intonation labeling, consistency in transcription with GToBI, and strategies for intonation labeling across language varieties.

### ToBI and AM Theory Formalization
5. **Hirst & Di Cristo (1998) - Intonation systems: A survey of twenty languages**: Comprehensive cross-linguistic survey establishing standardized frameworks for describing intonation systems across languages.

6. **Veilleux, Brugos & Shattuck-Hufnagel (2007) - The new English ToBI home page**: Reference for complete MAE_ToBI annotation conventions and pitch accent/boundary tone inventory.

### Prosodic Phrasing Rules
7. **Cutler, Dahan & van Donselaar (1997) - Prosody in the comprehension of spoken language**: Literature review documenting how prosodic structure (including phrase boundaries and duration patterns) affects speech perception and requires accurate phrasing.

### Downstep/Declination Models
8. **Liberman (1975) - The intonational system of English**: Foundational thesis on English intonation providing framework for understanding downstep and scaling effects that are essential for natural F0 contour generation across different pitch ranges and emphases.
