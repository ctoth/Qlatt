This paper investigates the historical priority question for the American four-level pitch analysis of English intonation, establishing through publication records and 1989 correspondence with Kenneth Pike that Pike developed the system as early as 1942, predating Wells's 1945 paper and contradicting the standard Trager & Smith attribution. The key contribution is the publication of Pike's own account of developing the four-level system from his tone language fieldwork, including his anticipation of the local-contour vs. global-declination distinction later formalized by Pierrehumbert (1980). For speech synthesis, this paper provides essential intellectual history for the Pike-to-Trager/Smith-to-Pierrehumbert-to-ToBI lineage that underlies modern intonation modeling, and clarifies the theoretical origins of treating pitch accents as locally substitutable contour patterns within a global prosodic frame.
