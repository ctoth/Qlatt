Boersma presents a complete articulatory speech synthesizer in which the whole vocal apparatus from lungs to lips is a chain of straight tubes whose walls are all coupled mass-spring systems, driven by one-dimensional aerodynamic equations with turbulence noise, a smooth zipper collision model, and tube lengths that vary in time. Because every wall can vibrate and the lungs are a finite-capacity volume rather than an ideal pressure source, source-filter interaction, aerodynamic devoicing during stop closure, aspiration, ejectives, implosives, frication, clicks and trills all emerge from the physics rather than from imposed rules, and the paper publishes every equation plus a full parameter set for three model speakers. It is a physical ground truth against which parametric glottal source models, fixed-position frication sources, and rule-driven voicing decisions in a formant synthesizer can be checked.
