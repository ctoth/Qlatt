## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Klatt (1979)** - "Synthesis by rule of segmental durations in English sentences" - The direct predecessor to this work; establishes the segment-level duration rules that Campbell & Isard's syllable-level model complements. Essential for understanding how the two approaches integrate.

2. **Crystal & House (1988)** - "Segmental durations in connected speech signals" - Large-scale empirical study of segment durations in connected speech. Already in our collection. Provides the connected-speech duration data that motivates going beyond isolated-word measurements.

3. **Edwards & Beckman (1988)** - "Articulatory timing and the prosodic interpretation of syllable duration" - Articulatory perspective on syllable-level timing. Reached similar conclusions about phrase and syllable-level timing effects. Could provide physiological grounding for the elasticity model.

4. **Peterson & Lehiste (1960)** - "Duration of syllable nuclei in English" - Classic reference on how vowel nucleus duration varies with consonant context. Foundational for understanding the pre-voicing lengthening effect discussed in Section 5.

5. **Umeda (1988a, 1988b)** - Vowel and consonant duration in American English - Comprehensive duration measurements for American English. Useful for cross-dialect comparison with the British English data in this paper.
