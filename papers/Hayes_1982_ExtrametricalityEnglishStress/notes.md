# Extrametricality and English Stress

## Reading provenance

Direct reading of canonical 150-dpi page images. PDF index 0 is printed p.227; citations below use printed pages. Theory profile. Extraction complete: all canonical page images read.

## Core argument and concepts

- An extrametrical constituent remains in phonological structure but is ignored by stress construction. Aristotle versus anecdotal motivates excluding exceptional final material; -ative demonstrates that delayed sonorant syllabification alone cannot supply the analysis. *(pp.227-228)*
- Rule (4) marks one invariant constituent at a stress-domain edge. Its type can be a segment, rhyme, or suffix; it cannot be an arbitrary variable-length sequence. Right-edge restriction is strongly favored, with possible marked left-edge exceptions acknowledged. *(p.228)*
- Arabic examples distinguish CV, CVV/CVC, and CVVC/CVCC. Excluding the final segment permits a uniform weight-sensitive foot instead of a special final foot template. Arabic feet are unbounded, left-branching, s-w; a right-branching w-s word tree selects the rightmost foot. *(pp.229-230, rules 7-9)*
- Hopi constructs an initial, at-most-binary w-s foot whose weak left member must be light; an s-w word tree makes that foot dominant. Final-syllable extrametricality explains initial stress in disyllables without a separate disyllable-only fronting rule. These are cross-language arguments, not English rules. *(pp.230-233, rules 11-18)*
- Latin's heavy-penult/otherwise-antepenult pattern can use a binary s-w foot after excluding the last rhyme, eliminating a ternary primitive restricted to final position. Extrametricality cannot consume the entire domain: this protects monosyllables. *(pp.234-235, rules 19-20)*
- Stray Syllable Adjunction (21) attaches a stray syllable as a weak member of an adjacent foot after foot construction; its direction between feet is initially unresolved. Excluded segments need no reattachment because they retain their syllable membership. *(p.235)*

## English model: assumptions and initial rules

- English stress is both lexically stored and rule-governed. Preferred maximal feet are not obligatory; lexical exceptions remain possible. The motivation combines historical change, unfamiliar-word judgments reported from Trammell, and cyclic phenomena. This is not evidence of deterministic prediction for every English spelling. *(pp.236-237)*
- Long versus short vowels follows Liberman and Prince's phonological classification; it is not measured acoustic duration. Orthographic examples abbreviate phonological inputs. *(p.237)*
- Consonant Extrametricality (24) excludes the final consonant. English Stress Rule (25) constructs a maximally binary s-w foot at the right edge; its weak right rhyme, if present, must be nonbranching. Thus verbs/unsuffixed adjectives favor final stress with a long vowel or final consonant cluster, otherwise penultimate stress: obey, atone, torment versus astonish, develop. *(p.238)*
- Long Vowel Stressing (30) gives a final long vowel a monosyllabic foot. Some short-vowel finals instead have a lexical foot: insect, parsnip. Final-consonant tendencies are explicitly not exception-free rules. *(p.239)*
- Noun Extrametricality (33) excludes the final rhyme: labyrinth, Arizona, agenda illustrate light versus heavy penults. Adjective Extrametricality (36) excludes a final adjectival suffix, as in magnanimous/reluctant; -ic and -id are named exceptions. *(pp.240-241)*
- Strong Retraction (43) groups remaining unattached rhymes right-to-left into at-most-binary s-w feet without weight restrictions. Unlike LP's formulation, Hayes separates it from the English Stress Rule and identifies weak retraction with that rule itself. Examples: Apalachicola, difficult, usurp. *(pp.242-243)*

## Ordering, retraction, and cyclic preservation

- Ordering graph (45): Long Vowel Stressing precedes Noun/Adjective Extrametricality; all extrametricality rules precede English Stress Rule (ESR); ESR precedes Strong Retraction. Long Vowel Stressing must see final long vowels before exclusion. Strong Retraction starts at the left boundary of the foot already built by ESR, so the resulting secondary-stress parity is domain-dependent, not a word-length alternating mask. *(p.244)*
- ESR may replace metrical structure from an earlier cycle; Strong Retraction may only foot unattached material. Parental/homonymous (46) require ESR to erase earlier foot boundaries. Designate/coruscate (47-49) derive strong retraction from verbal -ate being nonextrametrical; an independently listed retraction class is not required for each suffix. *(pp.244-245)*
- Stalactite/elephantine (50-52): first foot the final long vowel, then apply noun/adjective exclusion and ESR to its left. This derives weight-sensitive weak retraction using ESR itself. Adjectival -ate is weak-retracting, verbal -ate strong-retracting: suffix spelling alone is insufficient. *(pp.246-247)*
- Listed weak retractors (53): adjectival -ane, -ary, -ate, -ative, -atory, -ile, -ine, -oid, -ory, -ose; nominal -ary, -es (long e), -i, -ide, -ine, -ite, -oid, -on, -ory, -ose. These are examples of the analysis, not an instruction to ignore lexical and later-rule qualifications. Historical shifts are interpreted as loss of exceptional extrametricality markings. *(p.247)*
- A single strong/weak/long retraction tag for -ation cannot explain both inherited secondary stress and newly assigned stress. Fraternization (54-56) retains prior foot structure and blocks Strong Retraction on the outer cycle; compurgation (57-58), lacking the internal cycle, permits it. Compensation/condensation (59) further motivate cyclic history; rhythm adjustment is separately invoked in the latter derivation. *(pp.248-250)*
- Ticonderoga permits alternate relative secondary prominence through alternate word-tree branching, while superiority inherits the earlier superior tree. Preserve earlier metrical structure unless an explicit foot-construction rule redraws it; do not reconstruct all secondary stresses from the final primary position. *(pp.250-251, examples 60-62)*

## Destressing and surface ternary feet

- Destressing deletes a foot; syllables survive and are reattached by SSA. Prestress Destressing (63) removes a nonfinal, nonbranching weak foot before another foot. Connecticut (64) and fraternization (65) show removal at the left edge and inside a cyclic derivation. Segment conditions: initial light syllables (with a Latinate-prefix exception) and medial open syllables. The exact segmental detail is delegated to LP, not fully restated here. *(pp.251-253)*
- Sonorant Destressing (67) deletes a nonbranching foot between feet whose rhyme consists of a short vowel and sonorant, provided the target is not already strong. It applies after foot construction but before word-tree construction, preserving strong positions inherited from earlier cycles. Desultory versus elementary, trajectory, infirmary (68) tests these conditions. The -ory/-ary analysis assumes an underlying final glide, syllabified later; surface syllable count cannot be substituted silently. *(pp.253-254)*
- Rule (67) extends to other suffixes (-oid, -ite, -ine) and monomorphemic words. Ampersand/merchandise (71-72) get initial main plus final secondary stress after the intermediate foot is removed. Adirondack/Monadnock (73-74) lack the relevant sonorant configuration and retain penultimate main stress. Exceptions include odontoid, Tridentine; palimpsest, Arbuthnot, anecdote need exceptional noun-extrametricality markings. *(pp.254-256)*
- The metrical Arab rule (75) removes a weak final monosyllabic foot with a short-vowel rhyme following another foot. Hayes conjectures the shared constraint (76): no foot in strong metrical position may be deleted. This is a proposed theoretical generalization, not a measured universal. *(pp.256-257)*
- Nonfinal ternary feet need not be primitives. Abracadabra/Kalamazoo contrast with Saskatchewan/Mamaroneck: the strength of the final foot, rather than its syllable count, controls whether the preceding foot is ternary. *(pp.257-258, examples 77-79)*
- Poststress Destressing (80) deletes a binary foot with an open first syllable immediately after a nonbranching foot; the strong-foot protection still applies. SSA yields abracadabra (81). The same rule yields destressed -ory/-ary/-ative variants, e.g. cursory (82), but not McGrory/canary/creative. This must remain separate from Prestress Destressing: it can delete branching feet. *(pp.258-259)*

## Remaining ternary-foot analyses and peripheral visibility

- Hanover/Hanoverian provides an exception to Poststress but not Prestress Destressing. A four-syllable retraction domain normally creates two binary feet (83); a preceding branching foot blocks rule (80). Closed initial syllables also block it (84): Monongahela, Ticonderoga, carbuncle, cucumber. Galaxy/liberty-type cases instead use noun exclusion plus later sonorant syllabification (85-86); they do not justify weakening the open-syllable condition. *(p.260)*
- Alternative word-tree branching can generate different destressing and vowel reduction, not just relative pitch/timing. Hayes proposes lexical selection of one variant where this difference is large. This remains an explanatory hypothesis. Cyclicly derived subliminality versus monomorphemic Okefenokee (88) supplies a structural preservation control. *(pp.261-262)*
- SSA must preserve the foot type in ambiguous cases: attach weak strays leftward for s-w feet (English), rightward for w-s feet. Attawapiskat flapping and the incorrect derivation (89) motivate directionality. Sonorant Destressing in ampersand also needs leftward adjunction for correct main stress. *(pp.263-264, examples 89-90)*
- Residual strong ternary feet (Manitowoc, catamaran, etc.) require lexical listing or other analyses. Gobbledygook/budgerigar are suggested pseudocompounds; expressive formations may be phrasal and outside lexical stress. Do not manufacture a productive ternary rule merely to cover this residue. *(p.264, example 91)*
- Greek-derived helicograph/heteronym-type forms are analyzed as compounds that can lose internal word brackets after foot construction. Evidence includes independently usable prefixes, conjunction reduction, variation in compound prominence, unusually long unstressed runs, and prefix-final tensing. Final-rhyme exclusion on Greek prefixes and lexically variable bracket erasure are explicit additional machinery. Derived heteronymous/heliography can then undergo later cyclic stress. Microscope/microscopy additionally requires vowel-shortening allomorphy. *(pp.264-265, examples 92-93)*
- Apparent long retraction across two short vowels is narrowed to cases whose first vowel is [i]. Hayes proposes underlying /y/, normal foot construction, then y-to-i vocalization: meteoroid/deteriorate (94-95). Meridional and dialectal espionage support even surface four-syllable feet without a quaternary primitive. *(pp.265-267)*
- Vocalization environments (96): after d/t/r/theta before a vowel; after n across the indicated morpheme boundary; after noncoronal consonants; and after a consonant at a foot boundary. A competing /y/ tendency before final Vn (97) is overridden by these contexts (98). Thus phonological ordering, not unrelated lexical redundancy statements, explains precedence. *(pp.267-268)*
- After short vowel plus l, invariant [i] words are lexically /i/, alternating [i,y] words /y/ with optional vocalization (99-100); Australian and peculiar are exceptions. Auxiliary/beneficiary (101) rely on variable order between vocalization and Poststress Destressing, which bleed one another. These segmental assumptions must not be silently approximated by counting surface vowel tokens. *(pp.268-269)*
- Peripheral marks must cease when an affix moves their owner away from the edge: parental/medicinal (102). Peripherality Condition (103) removes extrametricality when nonempty material follows inside the active domain. Marks belong to the selected constituent, not all descendants: an excluded polysyllabic suffix remains one peripheral unit. *(pp.269-271)*
- Word-tree construction also ignores completely extrametrical feet. Label right nodes strong among visible feet, then adjoin leftover feet weakly: Isidore/Isidora (105-107). This replaces LP's right-strong-if-branching rule and explains noun versus verb/unsuffixed-adjective labeling. Adirondack (108-109) excludes a spurious initial-main-stress tree while still allowing Ticonderoga variants. *(pp.271-273)*
- Late Extrametricality (110), after feet but before word-tree labeling, excludes a peripheral rhyme preceded by a branching foot: difficult (111). Verbal stems such as intersect/comprehend do not regularly undergo it. Verbal -ate/-ize need late exclusion for the cited American disyllabic forms donate/baptize; British patterns differ. Late adjustments add exclusion, rather than systematically undoing it. Exceptional Tennessee/picturesque-type forms can instead lack initial exclusion. *(pp.273-274)*

## Formal contracts for implementation

Rule schemas below are transcriptions of the paper's operations, not a complete executable grammar. R is a rhyme, C a consonant, F a foot, D a stress domain, ex the extrametrical mark. X(x) is a maximum-binary s-w foot whose optional right rhyme must be light; X(X) permits either weight. *(pp.228,238,243)*

$$
X \longrightarrow [+\mathrm{ex}]\;/\;\_\ ]_D
$$
X is one fixed constituent type, not a variable sequence. *(p.228, rule 4)*

$$
[+\mathrm{cons}] \longrightarrow [+\mathrm{ex}]\;/\;\_\ ]_{\mathrm{word}}
$$
Only the final consonant is ignored by Consonant Extrametricality. *(p.238, rule 24)*

$$
R \longrightarrow [+\mathrm{ex}]\;/\;\_\ ]_N
$$
Noun Extrametricality targets a final rhyme. *(p.240, rule 33)*

$$
[X]_{\mathrm{suffix}} \longrightarrow [+\mathrm{ex}]\;/\;\_\ ]_{\mathrm{Adj}}
$$
Adjective Extrametricality targets a suffix constituent, including a multisyllabic suffix. *(pp.241,271, rule 36)*

$$
X[+\mathrm{ex}] \longrightarrow X[-\mathrm{ex}]\;/\;\_\ Y]_D,\qquad Y\ne\varnothing
$$
Remove a mark once its owner is nonperipheral in the active domain. *(p.270, rule 103)*

### Operational ordering

1. Obtain lexical/morphological structure and vowel/rhyme properties; preserve lexically stored feet and declared exceptions. Apply cycles from embedded stem to outer derivation. *(pp.237,248-251)*
2. Reevaluate peripheral visibility and protect a nonempty stress domain. Apply Long Vowel Stressing before noun/adjective exclusion, then the relevant exclusion rules. *(pp.235,244,269-271)*
3. Apply ESR at the current right edge, allowing it to redraw old foot structure in its domain. Attach strays as required. *(pp.238,244-245)*
4. Apply Strong Retraction only to remaining unfooted material, right-to-left. Never redraw existing feet in this operation. *(pp.243-250)*
5. Apply Sonorant Destressing after footing but before word-tree construction; inherited strong feet are protected. Apply the specified late-exclusion adjustments before labeling. *(pp.253,273-274)*
6. Construct the word tree over visible feet with right-strong labeling, preserving inherited structure where not redrawn; adjoin excluded feet weakly. *(pp.251,271-273)*
7. Apply the distinct pre/poststress destressing operations at their specified stages and SSA without deleting segments. Respect interactions with sonorant/glide vocalization and dialectal variants. *(pp.252-269)*

## Load-bearing propositions and regression candidates

- The same syllable count can produce different stress because rhyme weight, part of speech, affix type, and earlier cycles differ. Agenda/labyrinth; obey/develop; adjectival/verbal -ate are separate controls. *(pp.238-247)*
- ESR replacement and Strong Retraction preservation are observably different; test parental and fraternization with explicit intermediate feet. *(pp.244-249)*
- Excluding a suffix does not delete it or mark each of its descendant rhymes independently. A newly internal excluded stem rhyme must become visible. *(pp.270-271)*
- A strong foot cannot be destressed under the proposed condition; infirmary and Saskatchewan are negative controls. *(pp.253,257-259)*
- Secondary stress depends on inherited feet; a fixed alternating pattern from final primary stress is insufficient. *(pp.248-251,261-262)*
- Lexical exceptions, dialect variation, and alternative morphology are necessary inputs rather than errors to hide. *(pp.237,261,264,274)*

## Limitations and relevance to Qlatt

This is a theoretical analysis supported by derivations, lexical patterns, historical evidence and cited experiments, not a tested end-to-end G2P system or an accuracy benchmark. It assumes richer underlying representations than a stressless ARPAbet array, including morphological cycles, lexical exceptions, long/short vowel classes and sometimes glides absent on the surface. The proposed universals and lexical-history explanations retain the author's qualifications. *(pp.236-237,247,257,261-269,274-275)*

For Qlatt #37, the central contribution is an explicit distinction among domain exclusion, foot construction, preservation/replacement, destressing and prominence selection. A direct implementation must represent their inputs and ordering. Using only the heavy-penult rule would be a documented approximation, not an implementation of this paper. A digit serializer and acoustic inventory-target selection are separate engineering layers; the paper does not specify ARPAbet encoding, YAML schemas, or Klatt parameter values. *(Implementation inference from pp.238-274)*

Short source quotation: “No foot in strong metrical position may be deleted.” *(p.257)*
