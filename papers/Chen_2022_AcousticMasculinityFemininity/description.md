This paper uses Extreme Random Forest models and hierarchical clustering to identify which of 23 acoustic measures independently predict perceived masculinity in males and femininity in females, testing 225 Australian speakers rated by 25-30 listeners. The key finding is that F0 mean is the dominant predictor (43.5% importance for males, 23.8% for females), followed by F3/F4/VTL estimators (~13-16%), with voice perturbation measures (jitter, shimmer, HNR) contributing the least, and the study achieves prediction correlations of r=.77 for males and r=.63 for females using 7-second speech segments. For speech synthesis, these importance rankings confirm that F0 is the primary control parameter for perceived vocal gender, formant frequencies (especially higher formants reflecting vocal tract length) are the secondary lever, and perturbation-based voice quality adjustments have minimal impact on gender perception.
