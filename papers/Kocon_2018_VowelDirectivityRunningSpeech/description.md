This paper measures horizontal directivity patterns (0-180 degrees, 15-degree resolution, up to 20 kHz) for five vowels extracted from running speech, providing vowel-specific and long-term averaged third-octave band weighting functions for simulating off-axis listening conditions. The key finding is that vowel identity significantly affects directivity, with /a/ being the most directional (DI = 3.9 dB) and /o/ the least (DI = 2.8 dB), with differences driven by the 1-kHz band and above — particularly a clear separation between /a,e,i/ and /o,u/ in the 4-kHz octave band. For speech synthesis, the complete Table 1 weighting functions enable post-synthesis directivity filtering to model realistic off-axis speech radiation, and the vowel-dependent findings suggest that accurate spatial audio rendering may require phoneme-level directivity modeling rather than a single speech-averaged pattern.
