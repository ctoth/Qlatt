# Perceptual Correlates of Acoustic Measures of Vocal Variability

## One-Sentence Summary

Establishes that pitch sigma (SD of F0 in semitones) and SFF range (in semitones) are strong acoustic correlates of perceived vocal variability (r = .927 and r = .890 respectively), providing validated acoustic measures for quantifying how "monotone" or "expressive" a voice sounds.

## Problem Addressed

Prior to this study, no research had confirmed whether commonly used acoustic measures of vocal variability (pitch sigma and SFF range in semitones) actually correlate with listener perceptions of pitch variability. Researchers assumed these measures reflected perceived variability but had not validated this relationship with modern methods.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrated a strong positive correlation (r = .927, p < .01) between pitch sigma (in semitones) and perceptual judgments of vocal variability during reading
- Demonstrated a strong positive correlation (r = .890, p < .01) between SFF range (in semitones) and perceptual judgments
- Showed that pitch sigma is a slightly better predictor of perceived variability than SFF range
- Found no significant gender differences in pitch sigma (M: 2.18 ST, F: 2.27 ST) or SFF range (M: 11.33 ST, F: 12.05 ST) during normal reading
- Confirmed that semitone-based measures are essential for valid variability measurement (Hz-based measures distort cross-gender comparisons)
- Provided normative SFF data for young adult speakers: males 122.73 Hz, females 215.92 Hz

## Methodology

- **Speakers:** 15 male (19-30 years) and 19 female (20-30 years) speakers recorded reading the Grandfather Passage in normal, monotone, and expressive conditions
- **Acoustic analysis:** KayPENTAX Real Time Pitch (model 5121) with analysis range 50-300 Hz (males), 100-600 Hz (females); pitch smoothing set to medium; outlier elimination via manual analysis range adjustment
- **Listener panel:** 30 graduate students in Communication Sciences and Disorders rated samples on 7-point Likert scale (1 = extreme monotony, 7 = extreme variability)
- **Experimental samples:** 15 male + 15 female samples selected to represent continuum from monotone to expressive (including normal, monotone, and expressive readings)
- **Statistics:** Pearson product-moment correlations, independent samples t-tests, Fisher z-transformations for comparing correlations between genders
- **Reliability:** Intra-rater reliability via 8 repeated samples (~50%), average r = .919; inter-rater reliability via second acoustic examiner with tolerance criteria

## Key Equations

$$
\text{SD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\bar{x} - x_i)^2}
$$
Where: SD = standard deviation (pitch sigma when applied to F0 in semitones), $\bar{x}$ = mean SFF, $x_i$ = individual frequency values, $n$ = number of measurements.

(Baken and Orlikoff, 2000, p. 171)

**Semitone conversion** (implied throughout):
$$
\text{ST} = 12 \cdot \log_2\left(\frac{f}{f_{\text{ref}}}\right)
$$
Where: ST = semitones, $f$ = frequency in Hz, $f_{\text{ref}}$ = reference frequency.

## Implementation Details

### Acoustic Analysis Procedure
1. Record reading passage (Grandfather Passage recommended)
2. Trim to consistent segment (first word to specific endpoint)
3. Set analysis range: 50–300 Hz (males), 100–600 Hz (females)
4. Apply medium pitch smoothing
5. Run preliminary analysis to obtain SFF, pitch sigma, SFF range
6. Inspect pitch tracing for outlier data points
7. Eliminate outliers by adjusting analysis range bounds
8. Re-analyze for final values
9. Verify with second examiner (SFF within 5 Hz, SFF range within 1 ST, pitch sigma within 0.03)

### Perceptual Rating Protocol
1. Present 15-second familiarization montage of all stimuli
2. Rate each sample on 7-point scale (1 = extreme monotony, 4 = average, 7 = extreme variability)
3. Allow 3 seconds between stimuli for rating
4. Include ~50% repeated stimuli for intra-rater reliability
5. Apply reliability criteria: average absolute difference < 1.5, correlation r > .5

### Important Distinctions
- **Individual pitch sigma** (SFF SD): SD of an individual speaker's F0 distribution — measures within-speaker variability
- **Group SFF SD**: SD of mean SFFs across a group — measures between-speaker variability
- These are frequently confused in the literature; both reported as "SFF SD"

## Results Summary

1. **Pitch sigma strongly predicts perceived variability:** r = .927 (both genders combined), significant at p < .01
2. **SFF range also predicts perceived variability:** r = .890, but slightly weaker than pitch sigma
3. **No gender difference in correlation strength:** z-scores comparing male vs female correlations were not significant (pitch sigma: z = -.89, p = .37; SFF range: z = .25, p = .80)
4. **No gender difference in acoustic variability:** males and females showed statistically similar pitch sigma (t[32] = -.357, p = .723) and SFF range (t[32] = -.621, p = .539) during normal reading
5. **Pitch sigma and SFF range are highly correlated with each other:** r = .979 (combined), .990 (males), .964 (females)
6. **Normative values for young adults during reading:** Pitch sigma ~2.2 ST, SFF range ~11.7 ST (both genders)

## Limitations

- Speakers who provided both monotone and expressive samples were repeated in the stimulus set (4 speakers appeared twice)
- Relatively small sample size for normative data (N = 15 males, N = 19 females)
- Only reading task examined (not spontaneous speech)
- Young adult speakers only (ages 19–30)
- The analysis range adjustment procedure, while improving results, introduces some subjectivity
- Likert scale anchors ("extreme monotony" vs "extreme variability") may not be equally spaced perceptually

## Testable Properties

- Pitch sigma (in ST) must correlate positively with perceived vocal variability (r > .85)
- SFF range (in ST) must correlate positively with perceived vocal variability (r > .80)
- Pitch sigma correlation with perceived variability should be >= SFF range correlation
- For normal reading, pitch sigma should be in range [0.5, 5.0] ST for healthy young adults
- For normal reading, SFF range should be in range [4, 22] ST for healthy young adults
- Mean SFF for young adult males during reading: 100–175 Hz
- Mean SFF for young adult females during reading: 185–250 Hz
- Pitch sigma and SFF range must be highly correlated with each other (r > .95)
- No significant sex difference expected in pitch sigma or SFF range during reading when measured in semitones

## Relevance to Project

This study validates two key acoustic measures — pitch sigma and SFF range (both in semitones) — as strong perceptual correlates of vocal expressiveness. For the Qlatt synthesizer's prosody system, this means:

1. **F0 variability targets:** When generating speaker personality profiles, pitch sigma of ~2.2 ST represents "normal" reading expressiveness. Values below ~1.0 ST would sound monotone; values above ~4.0 ST would sound very expressive.
2. **Semitone-based F0 modeling:** All F0 variability in the prosody rules should be computed in semitones, not Hz, to maintain perceptual equivalence across speaker base frequencies.
3. **Gender-neutral variability:** The finding that males and females have similar pitch sigma during reading supports using a single variability model across speaker genders.
4. **Prosody quality metric:** Pitch sigma of synthesized output could serve as a diagnostic measure — if the synthesizer's pitch sigma falls far outside the 0.5–5.0 ST range for reading, the prosody rules need adjustment.
