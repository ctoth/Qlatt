# Abstract

**Paper:** Rothenberg, M., Carlson, R., Granstrom, B., & Lindqvist-Gauffin, J. (1975). A Three-Parameter Voice Source for Speech Synthesis. Speech Communication Seminar, Stockholm, August 1-3, 1974, pp. 235-243.

## Verbatim Abstract

A "black box" model of the voice source has been built. This model has as inputs three physiologically derived variables and an output wave form similar to the actual glottal volume velocity wave. The voice source has been used with the OVE III serial formant synthesizer and this paper reports on some preliminary synthesis experiments using a new rule synthesis system.

## Interpretation

This paper presents an analog circuit-based glottal source model controlled by three neurologically-motivated parameters: F (frequency/pitch), L (loudness/subglottal pressure), and T (tightness/glottal adduction). Unlike purely acoustic models (e.g., Fujimura 1968's aperiodicity model) or fully physiological models (e.g., Ishizaka-Flanagan 1972 two-mass model), this is a "behavioral" or "black box" approach: the inputs are physiologically meaningful, but the internal mechanism (an RC circuit with diodes) has no physiological correspondence. The model was validated by comparison with inverse-filtered glottal airflow waveforms and integrated with the OVE III formant synthesizer at KTH for rule-based synthesis experiments. This is an important precursor to later parametric glottal source models including the LF model (Fant et al. 1985), which replaced analog circuits with mathematical waveform parameterization while retaining the idea of physiologically-motivated control parameters.
