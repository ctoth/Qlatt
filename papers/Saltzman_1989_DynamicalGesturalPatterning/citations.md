## Key Citations from Paper

### Foundational / Heavily Referenced

- Browman, C. P., & Goldstein, L. M. (1986). Towards an articulatory phonology. *Phonology Yearbook*, 3, 219–252.
  - **Why cited**: Articulatory phonology framework that this paper formalizes dynamically

- Browman, C. P., & Goldstein, L. M. (1989). Articulatory gestures as phonological units. *Phonology*, 6, 201–251.
  - **Why cited**: Companion paper defining gestures as phonological primitives

- Fowler, C. A. (1977). *Timing control in speech production*. Bloomington: Indiana University Linguistics Club.
  - **Why cited**: Coproduction theory that this model implements computationally

- Fowler, C. A. (1980). Coarticulation and theories of extrinsic timing. *Journal of Phonetics*, 8, 113–133.
  - **Why cited**: Intrinsic vs. extrinsic timing debate

- Jordan, M. I. (1986). *Serial order: A parallel distributed processing approach* (ICS Report No. 8604). University of California, San Diego.
  - **Why cited**: Connectionist sequential network proposed for serial gesture dynamics

- Kelso, J. A. S., Saltzman, E. L., & Tuller, B. (1986). The dynamical perspective on speech production: Data and theory. *Journal of Phonetics*, 14, 29–59.
  - **Why cited**: Prior work establishing the dynamical systems approach to speech

- Munhall, K. G., Ostry, D. J., & Parush, A. (1985). Characteristics of velocity profiles of speech movements. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance*, 11, 457–474.
  - **Why cited**: Empirical data on articulatory kinematics used for model validation

- Saltzman, E. L. (1986). Task dynamic coordination of the speech articulators: A preliminary model. *Experimental Brain Research Series*, 15, 129–144.
  - **Why cited**: Earlier version of the task-dynamic model

- Saltzman, E. L., & Kelso, J. A. S. (1987). Skilled actions: A task-dynamic approach. *Psychological Review*, 94, 84–106.
  - **Why cited**: General task-dynamic framework applied here to speech

### Articulatory / Acoustic Data

- Perkell, J. S. (1969). *Physiology of speech production*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.
  - **Why cited**: X-ray data used for model articulator geometry

- Perkell, J. S., & Nelson, W. L. (1985). Variability in production of the vowels /i/ and /a/. *JASA*, 77, 1889–1895.
  - **Why cited**: Articulatory variability data

- Öhman, S. E. G. (1966). Coarticulation in VCV utterances: Spectrographic measurements. *JASA*, 39, 151–168.
  - **Why cited**: Classic coarticulation data the model accounts for ⭐ **IN COLLECTION: Ohman_1966_CoarticulationVCV**

### Dynamical Systems Theory

- Haken, H. (1983). *Synergetics*. Berlin: Springer-Verlag.
  - **Why cited**: Theoretical framework for self-organizing dynamical systems

- Kugler, P. N., & Turvey, M. T. (1987). *Information, natural law, and the self-assembly of rhythmic movement*. Hillsdale, NJ: Erlbaum.
  - **Why cited**: Ecological dynamics framework

- Turvey, M. T. (1977). Preliminaries to a theory of action with reference to vision. In R. Shaw & J. Bransford (Eds.), *Perceiving, acting, and knowing*. Hillsdale, NJ: Erlbaum.
  - **Why cited**: Action theory / ecological psychology context

### Speaking Rate and Timing

- Gay, T. (1981). Mechanisms in the control of speech rate. *Phonetica*, 38, 148–158.
  - **Why cited**: Speaking rate effects on articulation

- Stetson, R. H. (1951). *Motor phonetics*. Amsterdam: North-Holland.
  - **Why cited**: Classic data on gestural overlap at different speaking rates (Figure 11)

### Coarticulation Models

- Henke, W. L. (1966). *Dynamic articulatory model of speech production using computer simulation*. Unpublished doctoral dissertation, MIT.
  - **Why cited**: Early computational model of coarticulation (look-ahead)

- Keating, P. A. (1985). Universal phonetics and the organization of grammars. In V. Fromkin (Ed.), *Phonetic linguistics*. Academic Press.
  - **Why cited**: Window model of coarticulation (alternative approach)

- Löfqvist, A. (1990). Speech as audible gestures. In W. J. Hardcastle & A. Marchal (Eds.), *Speech production and speech modelling*. Dordrecht: Kluwer.
  - **Why cited**: Laryngeal gesture timing data
