This paper tests whether female voice fundamental frequency (F0) relates linearly to perceptions of dominance and attractiveness, using four natural female voices (184.6-310.3 Hz) each raised and lowered by 20 Hz and rated by male and female listeners. The key finding is that F0-dominance is linear (lower pitch = more dominant, with women more sensitive than men to this cue) while F0-attractiveness is nonlinear, increasing up to an optimal pitch around 280 Hz then sharply decreasing, with very high-pitched voices (~310 Hz) rated as least attractive. For the Qlatt speaker personality system, this establishes sex-specific voice parameter targets: unlike male voices where lower F0 uniformly increases attractiveness, female voice attractiveness has an inverted-U relationship with pitch, requiring a ~260-280 Hz target rather than simply lowering F0.
