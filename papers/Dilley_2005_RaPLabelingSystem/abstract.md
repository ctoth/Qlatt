# Abstract

This document is a labeling guide/tutorial rather than a journal article, so it does not have a formal abstract. The introduction states:

The RaP (Rhythm and Pitch) system is a method of labeling the rhythm and relative pitch of spoken English. The system permits the capture of both intonational and rhythmic aspects of speech; this and other features distinguish it from other labeling systems. Four labeling tiers are used for annotating speech prosody: the "words" tier (syllabic organization and orthography), the "rhythm" tier (rhythmic structure), the "tones" tier (tonal patterns), and the "misc" tier (parallelism, pitch range, disfluencies).

## Interpretation

RaP is an alternative to ToBI that explicitly represents metrical rhythm as a separate annotation dimension, rather than encoding it implicitly through break indices and tone labels. The key innovation is the independence of rhythm and tonal tiers: a phrase boundary on the rhythm tier does not mandate a boundary tone on the tones tier (as ToBI requires). This independence allows RaP to capture prosodic phenomena that ToBI conflates, such as disjuncture arising from non-intonational sources (e.g., morphosyntactic boundaries without pitch resets). The relative pitch labeling (H/L/E with respect to preceding tone) rather than absolute phonological categories (H*, L+H*, etc.) also provides a more direct link to F0 contour shape, which is useful for synthesis applications.
