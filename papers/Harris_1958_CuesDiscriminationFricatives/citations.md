## Key Citations

### Already in Collection
- **Hughes & Halle 1956** (`Hughes_1956_SpectralPropertiesFricatives`): Confirmed that isolated friction segments of /f/, /s/, and /S/ are identifiable when /theta/ is not a response option. Harris notes this is consistent with her finding that /f/ friction is only confused with /theta/ friction.

### Conceptual Links (not citation-based)
- **Jongman et al. 2000** (`Jongman_2000_FricativeAcoustics`): Comprehensive acoustic analysis of all English fricatives across multiple speakers and vowel contexts; provides the quantitative spectral data that explains why Harris's perceptual results hold -- sibilants have concentrated spectral peaks while non-sibilants have diffuse, low-amplitude spectra.
- **Shadle 1985** (`Shadle_1985_FricativeAcoustics`): Aerodynamic and acoustic modeling of fricative production; the physical basis for why /f/ and /theta/ produce similar weak, diffuse noise spectra.
- **Shadle 2023** (`Shadle_2023_FricativeSpectraHighFreq`): High-frequency spectral analysis of fricatives; extends the acoustic characterization to frequencies above the typical analysis range.
