Edwards & Beckman (1988) demonstrate that different prosodic effects (phrase-final lengthening vs. nuclear stress) have qualitatively different influences on syllable-internal articulatory timing, using mandibular displacement measurements across four prosodic conditions at three speaking rates. Phrase-final lengthening disproportionately extends the closing phase of the vocalic gesture (an edge effect), while nuclear stress produces more uniform lengthening throughout the syllable. The paper argues that prosodic timing effects cannot be adequately modeled by simple millisecond multipliers and proposes sonority profiles as the appropriate representation.
