This paper investigates how prosodic cues in speech signal sincerity versus insincerity when speakers give compliments, using a controlled paradigm with female speakers producing "prosocial lies" (white lies to spare feelings). The key findings reveal that sincere compliments are spoken faster, begin with higher pitch on evidentiality phrases like "I think," and maintain lower amplitude throughout compared to insincere compliments, achieving 87% classification accuracy from acoustic features alone. For speech synthesis, this provides empirically-validated parameters for implementing a "sincerity" dimension in voice quality, particularly the insight that different acoustic features operate at different phrase positions within an utterance.
