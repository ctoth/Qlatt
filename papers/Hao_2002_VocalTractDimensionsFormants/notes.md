# Cross-Racial Studies of Human Vocal Tract Dimensions and Formant Structures

**Authors:** Jianping Hao
**Year:** 2002
**Venue:** Ph.D. Dissertation, Ohio University, School of Hearing, Speech and Language Sciences
**Director:** Steve Xue

## One-Sentence Summary
Provides normative vocal tract dimension measurements (oral/pharyngeal length and volume via acoustic reflection) and F1-F3 formant frequencies for nine vowels across 120 speakers from three racial groups (White American, African American, Chinese), with regression models linking tract dimensions to formant frequencies.

## Problem Addressed
Available normative data for formant frequencies and vocal tract dimensions were based almost exclusively on monolingual Caucasian English speakers. No studies had compared vocal tract configurations of speakers from different races, leaving the causes of cross-racial formant frequency differences largely assumptive.

## Key Contributions
- First study to simultaneously measure vocal tract dimensions (via acoustic reflection) and formant frequencies across three racial groups with controlled demographics
- Comprehensive F1-F3 data for nine vowels in /hVd/ context plus LTAS-derived formants for 120 speakers (balanced by race and gender)
- Demonstration that vocal tract volume (especially oral volume) differs significantly across races even when length parameters do not
- Multiple regression model for predicting average F1 from oral length, pharyngeal length, and oral volume ($R^2_{adj} = .18$)
- Confirmation that F2 and F3 have no significant linear relationship to gross vocal tract dimensions (suggesting non-linear or articulatory-configuration dependencies)

## Methodology
- 120 subjects: 40 White American (20M/20F), 40 African American (20M/20F), 40 Chinese (20M/20F)
- Age range: 20-30 years; controlled for height, weight, hearing, speech, craniofacial norms
- Vocal tract dimensions measured with Eccovision Acoustic Pharyngometer (acoustic reflection technology)
- Six dimensional parameters: oral length (OL), pharyngeal length (PL), total vocal tract length (TL), oral volume (OV), pharyngeal volume (PV), total vocal tract volume (TV)
- Formant frequencies measured using CSL (Computerized Speech Lab) with LPC analysis (12th-order, 25 ms window, 10 kHz sampling)
- Nine vowels in /hVd/ context: /i/, /I/, /a/, /o/, /E/, /ae/, /U/, /u/ plus sustained /a/
- Rainbow Passage reading for LTAS analysis
- Statistical analysis: two-way MANOVA (race x gender), Tukey's HSD post hoc, Pearson's correlation, multiple regression

## Key Equations

### Multiple Regression for Average F1
$$
\hat{F1} = 800.59 - 28.67 \cdot OL - 9.88 \cdot PL + 1.99 \cdot OV - 0.31 \cdot PV
$$

Where:
- $OL$ = oral length (cm), $\beta = -.51$, $p < .001$
- $PL$ = pharyngeal length (cm), $\beta = -.27$, $p = .01$
- $OV$ = oral volume (ml), $\beta = .32$, $p < .001$
- $PV$ = pharyngeal volume (ml), $\beta = -.04$, $p = .65$ (not significant)
- $R^2 = .21$, $R^2_{adj} = .18$, $SE = 49.64$

## Implementation Details
- Acoustic reflection (pharyngometry): non-invasive measurement of cross-sectional area profile. Sound pulse enters mouth, reflections from area changes are recorded. Area-distance function is computed from pressure-time data.
- Oral cavity boundary: incisors to smallest cross-sectional area at back of mouth (oral-pharyngeal juncture, OPJ)
- Pharyngeal cavity boundary: OPJ to smallest cross-sectional area at glottis level
- Formant analysis: LPC at 12th order, 25 ms Hamming window, 10 kHz sampling rate
- Vowels measured in steady-state portion (middle 1/3 of vowel) of second of three repetitions
- LTAS: voiced segments extracted from Rainbow Passage, 4096-point FFT, formant peaks identified from averaged spectrum

## Results Summary

### Gender effects on vocal tract dimensions
- Males have significantly longer OL, PL, TL than females (all p < .01 to p < .001)
- Males have significantly larger PV and TV than females (p < .001)
- OV gender difference was NOT significant (p > .05)

### Race effects on vocal tract dimensions
- OV: Chinese speakers had significantly larger oral volumes than White American (p < .01) and African American (p = .01) speakers
- TV: Chinese speakers had significantly larger total vocal tract volume than African American speakers (p = .01)
- Length parameters (OL, PL, TL): no significant racial main effects

### Gender effects on formant frequencies
- F1: females significantly higher for /i/, /I/, /o/, /a/, /E/, /ae/, /U/, /u/, and LTAS
- F2: females significantly higher for /o/, /a/, /ae/, /U/, and /u/
- F3: females significantly higher for /a/, /i/, /I/, /o/, /a/, /E/, and /u/
- Formants of women approximately 20% higher than men (consistent with Fant 1966)

### Race effects on formant frequencies
- F1: significant race effect for /i/, /I/, /o/, /E/, /ae/, /U/, /u/, and LTAS
- F2: significant race effect for /a/, /i/, /o/, /a/, /E/, /ae/, /U/, /u/, and LTAS
- F3: significant race effect for /E/, /ae/, /U/, and LTAS

### Vocal tract-formant correlations
- F1 significantly negatively correlated with OL (r = -.23), PL (r = -.20), and TL (r = -.36)
- F3 weakly positively correlated with PL (r = .19)
- F2 had NO significant correlations with any tract dimensions
- Multiple regression: OL, PL, and OV significant predictors of F1 ($R^2_{adj} = .18$)
- F2 and F3: NO significant regression models with tract dimensions

## Limitations
- Chinese subjects were Mandarin speakers (English as L2) -- linguistic/articulatory habits may differ from native English speakers
- Acoustic reflection measures tract at rest (neutral breathing), not during phonation
- Only gross dimensions measured (length and volume) -- not cross-sectional area functions at specific locations
- Linear regression may miss non-linear relationships between tract dimensions and F2/F3
- All subjects were young adults (20-30) from one university -- limited generalizability
- $R^2_{adj} = .18$ for the F1 regression model -- tract dimensions explain only 18% of F1 variance

## Testable Properties
- Male vocal tract length > female vocal tract length (average ~17 cm vs ~15.5 cm)
- F1 is inversely correlated with vocal tract length (r = -.36)
- F2 and F3 should not have significant linear relationships with gross tract dimensions
- Female formant frequencies approximately 20% higher than male formant frequencies across vowels
- Oral volume is a significant positive predictor of F1 (longer oral cavity, higher F1 -- counterintuitive, suggests volume captures aperture-related information)
- Pharyngeal volume is NOT a significant predictor of average F1 when other variables are controlled

## Relevance to Project
This study provides normative formant frequency data (F1-F3) for nine American English vowels across gender and racial groups, directly usable as formant targets in the Klatt synthesizer's inventory. The vocal tract dimension data (oral length ~7-8 cm, pharyngeal length ~8-9 cm, total length ~15-17 cm) establishes the physical basis for speaker-dependent formant scaling. The regression model relating tract dimensions to F1 could inform speaker profile systems that link physical parameters to acoustic targets. The finding that F2/F3 are not linearly predictable from gross tract dimensions reinforces that articulatory configuration (tongue/lip position) rather than overall tract size drives higher formant variation.
