Foundational Haskins Laboratories study establishing that each stop consonant has a characteristic second-formant frequency locus corresponding to its place of articulation: 720 Hz for bilabials, 1800 Hz for alveolars, and 3000 Hz for velars (the latter only for vowels with F2 above ~1200 Hz). The paper demonstrates that formant transitions serve as perceptual cues for consonant identity when the transition "points to" but does not begin at the locus, with the first half of the transition interval being silent (closure). A shared first-formant locus near 240 Hz for all voiced stops is shown to be a cue for manner rather than place of articulation.
