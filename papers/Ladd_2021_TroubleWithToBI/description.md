This chapter critically examines the ToBI prosodic annotation system, arguing that its commitment to discrete phonological categories (particularly the L+H* vs. H* pitch accent distinction and the treatment of phrase-final level pitch) prematurely forecloses investigation of gradient phonetic variation in intonation. Ladd draws on Bolinger's concept of "gradience" to show that meaningful phonetic variation in accent peak height, rise extent, and boundary behavior operates on a continuum that ToBI's categorical notation obscures, citing poor inter-transcriber reliability and the theoretical contradictions exposed by "uptalk" transcription. For speech synthesis, this paper cautions against treating ToBI labels as fixed phonological primitives and motivates a system where F0 generation supports continuous variation in accent strength, rise parameters, and boundary tone realization rather than mapping discretely from ToBI categories.
