## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Mann (1980)** — "Influence of preceding liquid on stop-consonant perception" — The original finding of compensation for coarticulation with /l/ and /r/, which this entire paper builds on. Essential background for understanding coarticulation effects on stop perception.

2. **Lotto, Kluender, & Holt (1997)** — "Perceptual compensation for coarticulation by Japanese quail" — The key paper proposing spectral contrast as the mechanism. Understanding this opposing view helps frame what coarticulation models need to account for.

3. **Lotto & Holt (2006)** — "Putting phonetic context effects into context" — Direct rebuttal to Fowler's paper, published in the same issue. Reading both sides is essential for understanding the debate.

4. **Fowler, Brown, & Mann (2000)** — "Contrast effects do not underlie effects of preceding liquids" — Earlier audiovisual experiment from Fowler's lab providing convergent evidence against the contrast account.

5. **Ohman (1966)** — Already in our collection. Fowler's VCV/VCCV paradigm extends Ohman's coarticulation measurements to perception.
