# Abstract

A two-formant synthetic vowel with closely spaced formants (F1 and F2 being fixed) can be made perceptually similar to a single-formant stimulus with F* = (F1 + F2)/2 by adjusting the A2/A1-amplitude ratio of the formants. The critical distance between the formants (delta-z_c) that corresponds to the disappearance of this 'center of gravity' effect was found to be equal to 3.0-3.5 Bark. A close to continuous relation between A2/A1 and F* of a single-formant matching stimulus was found for stimuli with delta-z < delta-z_c. A clear discontinuous relationship between A2/A1 and F* was observed for stimuli with delta-z > delta-z_c. For stimuli with delta-z > delta-z_c, the formant amplitudes appeared to be of minor importance, the vowel quality being determined by the frequency locations of the formant peaks in the vowel spectrum. Formant peaks can be detected even if they are represented by very small spectral irregularities. Possible relations between peak extraction and 'center of gravity' effects are discussed.

## Interpretation

This paper provides the foundational quantitative measurement of the critical Bark distance for spectral integration in vowel perception. The 3.0-3.5 Bark threshold determines whether the auditory system treats two formant peaks as a single integrated percept or as two independent features. For a Klatt synthesizer, this means:

1. **Back vowel formants** (F1-F2 typically within 3.5 Bark) can be treated as a single perceptual unit where A2/A1 ratio smoothly shifts perceived quality
2. **Front vowel formants** (F1-F2 exceeding 3.5 Bark) are perceived independently, making their frequency positions far more important than their relative amplitudes
3. The center-of-gravity effect explains why small errors in individual back-vowel formant frequencies can be compensated by amplitude adjustments, but front-vowel formant frequencies must be more precisely targeted
4. F2-F3 proximity in front vowels creates an analogous integration effect in the higher frequency region
