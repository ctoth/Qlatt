Chistovich & Lublinskaya (1979) establish the critical distance of 3.0-3.5 Bark between formants, below which listeners integrate two formant peaks into a single perceived spectral center of gravity. The paper demonstrates that for closely-spaced formants (as in back vowels), vowel quality is determined by the amplitude-weighted center of gravity rather than individual formant frequencies, while for widely-spaced formants, each peak is perceived independently. This finding has direct implications for formant synthesis, explaining why back vowels can be approximated by single-formant stimuli and why the perceptual effect of formant amplitude ratios depends on formant spacing.
