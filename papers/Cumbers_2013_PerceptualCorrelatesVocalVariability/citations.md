## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Baken & Orlikoff (2000)** — Comprehensive reference defining pitch sigma, SFF, and all variability measures. The authoritative source for clinical voice measurement methodology.

2. **Philhour (1948)** — The only prior study to correlate pitch sigma with perceived pitch variability (r = .88). Foundational despite outdated methodology.

3. **Stoicheff (1981)** — Provides age-decade pitch sigma data for females (ages 20-70+), showing how variability changes with age. Useful for age-based speaker profiles.

4. **Hollien & Jackson (1973)** — Normative pitch sigma data for young adult males (N=157). Large sample, useful for speaker profile baselines.

5. **Nadig & Shaw (2012)** — Examines pitch range in autism context, finding r = .25 between Hz-based SFF range and perceived variability (vs. r = .890 in this study using semitones). Demonstrates importance of semitone measurement.
