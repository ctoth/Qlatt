# Stevens & Blumstein 1978 — Invariant Cues for Place of Articulation in Stop Consonants

## Key Finding

Place of articulation for stop consonants can be identified from the **gross spectral shape** of the short-term spectrum sampled at the onset of the consonantal release, independent of vowel context. This onset spectrum is determined by both the burst spectrum and the initial formant transitions at voicing onset.

## Three Invariant Spectral Templates

These are the perceptually validated counterparts to the acoustic templates in Blumstein & Stevens 1979.

### 1. Alveolar [d] — Diffuse-Rising
- Spectral energy is **spread diffusely** across frequency, with **rising** amplitude toward higher frequencies
- F2, F3, F4 shift **upward** relative to neutral vowel → higher formants increase in amplitude relative to lower formants
- The onset spectrum shows a general rise in amplitude from F2 to F3/F4 region

### 2. Labial [b] — Diffuse-Falling
- Spectral energy is **spread diffusely** across frequency, with **falling** amplitude toward higher frequencies
- F2, F3, F4 shift **downward** relative to neutral vowel → decrease in amplitude of higher formants relative to lower formants
- No single dominant spectral peak; energy distributed but decreasing with frequency

### 3. Velar [g] — Compact (Midfrequency Peak)
- A **prominent spectral peak** in the midfrequency range (1-3 kHz)
- F2 and F3 are close together at onset due to proximity of the constriction to a point where F2 and F3 are roughly equal
- The burst excites resonances near F2/F3, reinforcing this compact peak
- Before back vowels: peak at lower end of 1-3 kHz range
- Before front vowels: peak at upper end of the range

### Burst Parameters
- Burst duration: 5 ms
- Burst onset: 10 ms prior to voicing onset for full-cue stimuli; 15 ms for burst-only stimuli
- For [b] end: burst generated by exciting a resonator centered at F4 of adjacent vowel, with 5-ms white noise, 10 ms prior to voicing
- For [g] end: burst generated by exciting F3 (for [bi gi]) or F2 (for other vowels)
- Burst amplitude: two peaks in burst spectrum, ~10 dB lower for extreme stimulus vs end-point
- Burst spectral peaks roughly continuous with F2 and F4 offsets for intermediate stimuli

### Burst Generation Details
- Alveolar/velar bursts: noise exciting a resonator at burst frequency
- Labial bursts: noise exciting a resonator at a frequency near F4 of adjacent vowel (weak, distributed energy)
- Burst frequency for [g] before back vowels: near F2
- Burst frequency for [g] before [i]: near upper end of F3 range
- The burst is roughly continuous with the second or third formant of the following vowel

## Onset Spectrum Analysis Window
- **26-ms window** with high-frequency preemphasis
- Preemphasis applied by first-difference of waveform (sampled at 10 kHz)
- Smoothed using linear prediction algorithm (all-pole spectra with preemphasis)
- Window begins at consonantal release, encompassing burst and initial 10+ ms of transitions
- This window duration chosen because it gave the most salient gross spectral attributes
- Spectral attributes not strongly dependent on window length

## Implications for Synthesis

1. **Onset spectrum shape matters more than individual cue values**: The gross spectral shape at consonant release — not individual formant frequencies, burst frequencies, or transition directions — is the primary perceptual cue. A synthesizer must get the overall spectral envelope right, not just individual parameter values.

2. **Burst must be spectrally continuous with formant transitions**: In natural speech, the burst spectrum is roughly continuous with the formant onset frequencies. When the burst is isolated from transitions, listeners fail to identify place of articulation. The burst and transition must form a coherent onset spectrum.

3. **The auditory system samples a ~26 ms window at onset**: The relevant spectral information is captured in a short window starting at consonantal release. This suggests that parameters during the first 20-30 ms after release are critical for place perception.

4. **Compact vs diffuse distinction**: The compact/diffuse distinction (from Jakobson, Fant & Halle) maps directly to synthesis parameters:
   - Velar: F2 and F3 close together → prominent midfrequency peak
   - Alveolar: energy rising with frequency → higher formants relatively stronger
   - Labial: energy falling with frequency → lower formants relatively stronger

5. **Context-independent property at onset, context-dependent transitions**: The gross spectral shape at onset is invariant, but the formant transition trajectories that achieve this shape vary with vowel context. This validates the Klatt synthesis approach where formant starting frequencies are context-dependent parameters but the target onset spectrum shape is fixed per place of articulation.

## Relation to Companion Paper (Blumstein & Stevens 1979)

This 1978 paper establishes the **perceptual** validity of invariant onset spectra through synthetic speech experiments. The 1979 paper (Blumstein & Stevens, already in collection) provides the **acoustic production** evidence — showing that the same three spectral templates (diffuse-rising, diffuse-falling, compact) correctly classify ~85% of natural speech tokens across speakers and vowel contexts. Together they establish both the production and perception basis for invariant place-of-articulation cues.
