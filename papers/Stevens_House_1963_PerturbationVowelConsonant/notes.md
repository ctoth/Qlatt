# Stevens & House 1963 — Perturbation of Vowel Articulations by Consonantal Context

## Key Findings for Synthesis

### Core Result: Consonantal Context Systematically Shifts Vowel Formants

Vowel formant frequencies measured in CVC syllables deviate systematically from "null context" (/h-d/ or isolated) values. The shifts depend on:
1. The **vowel identity** (which vowel)
2. The **place of articulation** of the consonant
3. The **manner of articulation** (stop vs fricative)
4. The **voicing** of the consonant

### Experimental Design

- 3 male talkers (JM, AH, KS)
- 8 vowels: /i, I, E, ae, a, A, U, u/ (referred to by IPA symbols in paper)
- 14 consonants in symmetric CVC syllables (same C initial and final)
- Bisyllabic nonsense utterances: /ha/-CVC (iambic, stress on CVC)
- Measurement: analysis-by-synthesis spectrum matching on digital computer
- Three spectral samples midway through vowel, averaged
- Also measured vowels in /h-d/ context and in isolation /#-#/) as "null" baselines

### Effect of Consonant Place on Vowel Formants (Figure 6)

Three consonant place groups analyzed:

1. **Velars** (/k, g/): F2-locus is high (~2000+ cps for palatalized variants)
2. **Postdentals** (/t, d, s, z, sh, zh, ch, j/): F2-locus is intermediate
3. **Labials/Labiodentals** (/p, b, f, v/): F2-locus is low

**F1 effect**: Place of articulation has small effect on F1 (generally small shifts)

**F2 effect**: Place has a large systematic effect on F2:
- For **front vowels** (high F2): consonantal context shifts F2 **downward** from null values; velars cause least shift, labials/postdentals cause more
- For **back vowels** (low F2): consonantal context shifts F2 **upward**; postdentals cause more upward shift than velars or labials
- General rule: **F2 shifts toward a central value (~1500 cps)** from its null-context value

### Effect of Manner and Voicing (Figure 7)

- **Voiced consonants** lower F1 relative to voiceless (downward displacement of larynx in voiced context)
- **Voiceless consonants** raise F2 for front vowels relative to voiced
- **Fricatives** vs **stops**: F2 values tend to be lower for fricatives with front vowels and higher for fricatives with back vowels, relative to stops. Most evident for /I/, /E/, /ae/, /u/
- Vowels approach ideal target configuration more closely in **stop** environments than **fricative** environments (despite shorter vowel durations in stops)

### Undershoot Hypothesis (Key Theoretical Contribution)

The paper proposes that consonantal perturbation of vowel formants is explained by **articulatory undershoot**:

1. Articulatory structures have **mass and inertia** -- they cannot change position instantaneously
2. When moving from consonant configuration to vowel target and back, the articulators may not reach the ideal vowel position
3. The amount of undershoot depends on:
   - **Vowel duration**: shorter vowels show more undershoot (KS has ~20% shorter vowels and consistently larger F2 displacement)
   - **Articulatory distance**: greater distance between consonant and vowel configurations means more undershoot
   - **Articulator dynamics**: lip motion is sluggish compared to tongue, so /u/ (lip-rounded) shows large undershoot in unrounded consonant contexts

4. Two types of displacement co-occur:
   - Shift toward **consonant locus** values (F2 shifts toward F2-locus of adjacent consonant)
   - Shift toward **neutral/schwa** configuration (~500, 1500, 2500 cps) representing rest position

### Neutral (Schwa) Values

The paper identifies a neutral vocal tract configuration corresponding approximately to:
- F1 ~ 500 cps
- F2 ~ 1500 cps
- F3 ~ 2500 cps

(for a typical male talker)

### Implementation Notes for Klatt Synthesizer

1. **Formant targets in consonantal context should differ from isolated vowel targets.** The data in Tables 1-2 provide the empirical basis for these adjustments.

2. **F2 perturbation rule**: F2 shifts toward ~1500 Hz (neutral) from its null value. The magnitude depends on:
   - Consonant place (velars shift least for front vowels, labials/postdentals shift most)
   - Vowel identity (lax vowels shift more than tense vowels)
   - Consonant manner (fricatives cause more undershoot than stops despite longer vowel durations)

3. **F1 perturbation**: Generally small. Voiced consonants lower F1 slightly. Close vowels show slight F1 increase, open vowels show slight F1 decrease in consonantal contexts.

4. **Bandwidth increases**: B1 and B2 should be somewhat wider in consonantal contexts (Table 4 provides values).

5. **Duration interaction**: Speakers with shorter vowel durations show larger formant displacement from target values. This is consistent with Lindblom's (1963) undershoot model.

6. **Formant loci for consonant transitions** should be implemented per place group:
   - Labials: low F2-locus (~700-1000 cps)
   - Postdentals: intermediate F2-locus (~1700-1800 cps)
   - Velars: high F2-locus (~2000+ cps for front vowels, variable for back vowels)
