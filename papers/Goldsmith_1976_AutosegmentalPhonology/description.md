This PhD dissertation introduces autosegmental phonology, a framework where phonological representations consist of multiple parallel tiers of linearly ordered segments connected by association lines, replacing the standard view of phonology as a single string of feature bundles. The key contributions are the Well-formedness Condition (association lines must not cross and must preserve connectedness), the left-to-right tone association algorithm with spreading for mismatch cases, and the distinction between accentual (star-based) and non-accentual (purely categorical) melodic association, demonstrated through detailed analyses of Igbo, Mende, English, and other languages. This work is foundational for our Qlatt TTS system because it establishes the theoretical basis for the Autosegmental-Metrical (AM) framework used in ToBI pitch accent assignment, provides the implementable association algorithm for mapping tonal melodies onto syllable sequences, and introduces the floating tone mechanism that explains downstep and other F0 lowering phenomena in the prosodic pipeline.
