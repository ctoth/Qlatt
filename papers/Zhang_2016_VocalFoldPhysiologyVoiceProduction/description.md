This paper uses a validated 3D continuum phonation model to systematically map cause-effect relationships between vocal fold physiology (subglottal pressure, fold length, body/cover stiffness, medial surface thickness) and voice output (F0, intensity, spectral tilt, voice quality). The key finding is that medial surface thickness is the dominant controller of voice quality (breathy vs pressed), while F0 is primarily controlled by cover stiffness and length, and intensity by subglottal pressure, with a complete table of LF R-parameters (Rd, Ra, Rk, Rg) for six phonation conditions. For Klatt/LF-based speech synthesis, this work provides physiologically grounded voice quality presets and confirms that the three main perceptual dimensions (pitch, loudness, quality) map to largely independent physiological controls.
