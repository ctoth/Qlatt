## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **[3] Grabe & Low (2002), "Durational variability in speech and the rhythm class hypothesis"** - the direct methodological source of the rPVIc/nPVIv formulas used here; essential companion reading for the exact PVI derivation and its original cross-language (including English/Singapore English) values.
- **[8] Deterding (2001), "The measurement of rhythm: a comparison of Singapore and British English"** - closest precedent for measuring rhythm differences *between varieties* of English rather than between unrelated languages; directly relevant to a per-accent duration model spanning multiple English varieties.
- **[6] Ramus, Nespor & Mehler (1999), "Correlates of linguistic rhythm in the speech signal"** - the alternative %V/ΔC rhythm-metric family the authors argue against for within-language dialect work; useful to understand the competing metric design space.
- **[10] Wagner & Dellwo (2004), "Introducing YARD"** - source of the critique that ΔC-standard-deviation measures conflate syllabic complexity with rhythm timing; relevant caveat when choosing which rhythm metric to target in a duration model.
- **[15] Rouas, Farinas & Pellegrino (2003), "Automatic modeling of rhythm and intonation for language identification"** - motivates the paper's choice of raw Dv/Dc duration features as useful for automatic identification tasks, useful if building automated feature extraction for a duration policy.
