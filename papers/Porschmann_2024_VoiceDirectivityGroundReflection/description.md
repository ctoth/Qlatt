This paper systematically measures the vertical radiation directivity of the human voice for 23 phonemes across 13 subjects, revealing a characteristic downward main radiation direction (MRD) of 15-45 degrees below horizontal for frequencies under 800 Hz, with an upward shift to ~15 degrees between 800 Hz and 1.6 kHz attributed to shoulder/torso diffraction. Statistically significant phoneme-class differences are found: unvoiced fricatives [f, s, sh] have weaker downward radiation, nasals show the strongest directivity above 4 kHz, and voiced alveolars [l, r] have the strongest low-frequency downward peak. The ground reflection gain (GRG) analysis shows that for speaker-listener distances > 3 m over a reflective floor, the ground-reflected sound exceeds the direct sound below 800 Hz by up to 4.6 dB. Relevance to Klatt synthesis is indirect — the data concerns post-mouth radiation and room acoustics rather than vocal tract parameters, but could inform future spatial rendering or help explain systematic spectral differences in recordings made at different microphone positions.
