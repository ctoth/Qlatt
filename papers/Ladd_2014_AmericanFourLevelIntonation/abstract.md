# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

The four-level analysis of English intonation prevalent in American structuralist work from the mid-1940s to the late 1960s is variably credited to 1945 publications by both Kenneth Lee Pike (1912-2000) and Rulon S. Wells (1919-2008). However, Pike's work was the basis of published language teaching materials as early as 1942. The author reports a brief correspondence with Pike in 1989, which makes clear that Pike felt that his own contribution was underappreciated in the "intellectual climate" of Bloomfieldian linguistics. Bernard Bloch (1907-1965) and especially George L. Trager (1906-1992) appear to have influenced the reception of Pike's work and to have overstated the credit due to Wells.

---

## Our Interpretation

Ladd resolves a long-standing attribution dispute in American intonation theory by demonstrating that Pike developed the four-level pitch analysis before Wells, using it in English-language teaching materials at the University of Michigan as early as 1942. The paper's primary value is historiographic — it shows how institutional dynamics in Bloomfieldian linguistics shaped credit assignment, and reveals through Pike's own correspondence that his concept of "locally replaceable contour sets" anticipated key ideas in Pierrehumbert's autosegmental-metrical framework by decades. For this project, it clarifies the intellectual genealogy behind the intonation models that Qlatt's prosody rules implement.
