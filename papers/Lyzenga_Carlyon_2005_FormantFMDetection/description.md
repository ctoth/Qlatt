This paper measures detection and direction discrimination thresholds for sinusoidal and linear frequency modulation (FM) of synthetic vowel formants, across variations in F0, spectral slope, frequency region, and harmonic alignment. A hybrid auditory model combining place-of-excitation, AM-depth, and beating cues successfully predicts most thresholds, with place cues dominating for natural-like formants. The paper also demonstrates that frequency-modulation detection interference (FMDI) extends to direction discrimination of formant glides, showing that concurrent formant changes in different frequency regions can disrupt each other's perception. This is relevant to Klatt synthesis because it establishes psychoacoustic limits on formant transition perception and shows that coarticulation cues can be masked by simultaneous formant movements in competing speech.
