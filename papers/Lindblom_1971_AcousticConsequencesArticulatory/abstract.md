# Acoustical Consequences of Lip, Tongue, Jaw, and Larynx Movement

**Authors:** Bjorn E. F. Lindblom and Johan E. F. Sundberg
**Published:** Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 1971, Vol. 50, No. 4 (Part 2), pp. 1166-1179
**DOI:** 10.1121/1.1912750

## Abstract

An articulatory model is presented. It defines a procedure for deriving a set of formant frequencies from information on the state of the lip muscles, the position of the jaw, the shape and position of the tongue body, and larynx height. The acoustic and auditory consequences of varying these parameters individually are reported. The introduction of the jaw as a separate parameter -- a feature not used in previous articulatory models -- makes it possible to explain why "openness" occurs as a universal phonetic feature of vowel production. According to the explanation proposed, the degree of opening of a vowel corresponds to a position of the jaw that is optimized in the sense that it cooperates with the tongue in producing the desired area function. Such cooperation prevents excessive tongue shape deformation. Our results suggest that, in order to reflect this principle of articulatory synergism, "tongue height," although primary with respect to its acoustic consequences, should be represented as a derived feature characteristic of the final vocal-tract configuration.

## Interpretation for Qlatt

This paper establishes the quantitative relationships between individual articulator positions (jaw, tongue body position/shape, lip opening/rounding, larynx height) and their effects on formant frequencies F1-F4. The key findings are directly relevant to formant rule design:

- **Jaw opening** produces large F1 shifts (several hundred Hz) across all tongue shapes; also affects F2 and F3 depending on tongue configuration
- **Tongue body position** (d parameter, palatal to pharyngeal) is the primary controller of F2; F1 and F3 are less sensitive
- **Tongue body shape** (c parameter, degree of constriction) primarily modifies F2; leaves F1 and F3 relatively unaffected
- **Lip rounding/spreading** lowers all formant frequencies; the effect is largest for F3 with palatal constrictions and for F2 with velopharyngeal constrictions
- **Larynx lowering** (10 mm) decreases all formant frequencies by ~5-6% on average; F2 is most affected (~6.3%); F4 shows largest absolute shift (~160 Hz)

The model provides a principled basis for understanding why formant transitions behave as they do during coarticulation -- the acoustic output is a function of the combined articulator states, not any single parameter.
