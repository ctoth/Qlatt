Liberman and Pierrehumbert report two speech production experiments varying pitch range, phrase order, and list length, and derive a quantitative model of English F0 realization in which tonal targets are scaled multiplicatively above a phrase-level reference line that sits above a speaker-invariant baseline. Downstep is a constant-ratio decay on that transformed scale, phrase-final lowering is a separate multiplicative constant, and both are computed locally from adjacent elements, so declination emerges without whole-phrase preplanning; fitted values are a downstep constant of .59 to .68 and a final lowering constant of .59 to .77 across four speakers, with mean absolute errors of 1.5 to 7.8 Hz. For a formant synthesizer this supplies a directly implementable, streaming F0 scaling and declination algorithm with concrete default parameters, plus explicit evidence against the linearly declining baseline and global declination slope that naive prosody modules use.
