Comprehensive empirical study of consonant durations in connected American English speech, based on ~650 spectrograms from one male speaker reading an essay. The paper presents base durations and additive adjustment factors for all measurable consonants across all possible phonological conditions, including word position, stress, clustering, content/function distinction, and boundary effects. Critically argues that a fixed-constant duration model (as proposed by Klatt 1976) cannot predict the complexities of consonant duration, and that different consonants require different adjustment constants.
