This paper defines five corrected acoustic parameters (H1*-H2*, H1*-A1, H1*-A3*, B1, noise ratings) measurable directly from speech spectra that quantify glottal voice quality differences in female speakers, linking them to underlying glottal configurations through theoretical models of chink area, bandwidth, and spectral tilt. The key finding is that 22 female speakers cluster into two groups based on spectral tilt (H1*-A3*): one with abrupt closure and posterior chinks producing shallow tilt (~15 dB), and another with nonsimultaneous fold closure producing steep tilt (>23 dB), with aspiration noise strongly correlated (r=0.86) to spectral tilt. These measures map directly to Klatt synthesizer parameters (TL, B1, AH) and provide quantitative targets for female voice quality presets, making this the essential reference for implementing natural-sounding female voices with correct breathiness and spectral characteristics.
