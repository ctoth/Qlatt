This paper proposes that dynamically defined articulatory gestures -- discrete constriction-forming actions within vocal tract subsystems specified by tract variable targets and stiffness parameters in a task-dynamic model -- serve as the primitive atoms of phonological representation, replacing traditional segments and features. The key contributions include the gestural score notation (a two-dimensional representation of gestural constellations on articulatory tiers over time), the demonstration that connected-speech phenomena like deletion and assimilation emerge from two simple mechanisms (gestural magnitude reduction and inter-gestural overlap increase), and the tube geometry framework that derives a constriction degree hierarchy predicting natural classes and acoustic output types (occlusion, noise, resonance) from the combined effect of concurrent gestures. For speech synthesis, this work provides the theoretical foundation for modeling coarticulation as gestural overlap, deriving formant transition behavior from stiffness parameters, and understanding how the cascade/parallel source selection in Klatt synthesis relates to the constriction degree at the vocal tract level.
