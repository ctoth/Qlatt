# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

This book is an edited volume of multiple chapters, each of which contributes to establishing prosodic typology. It includes descriptions of the intonation and the prosodic structure of thirteen typologically different languages based on the same theoretical framework, the Autosegmental-Metrical (AM) model of intonational phonology. The languages included vary geographically, ranging from European languages (English, German, Greek, Dutch, Serbo-Croatian, Italian, and Swedish), Asian languages (Japanese, Korean, Cantonese, and Mandarin), a Native American Indian language (Chickasaw), and an Australian aboriginal language (Bininj Gun-wok). They also vary in the type and the degree of lexical specifications of prosody. Among these, eleven languages are also described in the prosodic transcription system known as ToBI (Tones and Break Indices). The last chapter of this book compares the eleven ToBI systems described in the book, and discusses the flexibility and the extensions of the ToBI system. This chapter also proposes a model of prosodic typology based on twenty-one languages.

## Our Interpretation

This book is the definitive cross-linguistic reference for the ToBI prosodic annotation framework, providing complete intonation models and transcription conventions for thirteen languages spanning stress-accent, pitch-accent, and tone language types. Its primary value for speech synthesis is the systematic specification of pitch accent inventories, phrase accent and boundary tone systems, and break index scales that map directly to F0 contour generation and prosodic phrasing rules. The concluding typology model (21 languages, classified by Prominence and Rhythmic/Prosodic Unit) provides the theoretical scaffolding for extending English prosody rules to other languages.
