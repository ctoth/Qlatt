This paper presents comprehensive normative measurements of glottal airflow waveform parameters and transglottal air pressure for 25 male and 20 female speakers across soft, normal, and loud voice conditions, using noninvasive inverse-filtered airflow techniques. Key findings establish that loudness changes involve distinct vocal fold adjustment modes rather than continuous variation, with males showing significantly higher maximum airflow declination rates and sharper vocal fold closure than females, while soft voice minimizes sex differences. These quantitative parameter values and their loudness-dependent relationships directly inform voice source modeling in formant synthesizers like Klatt, providing empirical targets for open quotient, spectral tilt, and excitation strength as functions of vocal effort and speaker sex.
