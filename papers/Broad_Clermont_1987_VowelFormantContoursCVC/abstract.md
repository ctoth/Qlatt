# Abstract

Models for the formant-frequency contours of vowels in CVC context are presented and illustrated with an American English database comprising isolated syllables with C, C' = /b,d,g/ and V = /i,I,e,ae,a,backwards-c,u,U,Lambda,schwa/. Both established and reinterpreted regularities among context effects are cast in a series of successively elaborated mathematical models. The first model (model I), in common with all its successors, embodies the notion of the superposition of CV and VC transitions, according to which a formant trajectory F_CVC(n) is modeled as f_CV(n) + T_V + g_VC(n), where n is duration-normalized time, T_V is a vowel target, and f_CV(n) and g_VC(n) are initial- and final-consonant transition functions. The rms errors for F1, F2, and F3 (30, 89, and 111 Hz) against independent test data are near the minimum theoretical values (32, 69, and 100 Hz) predicted from interrepetition variation and its contribution to uncertainty in the model elements. Subsequently, the elements of the additive model are individually characterized by further incorporating per-consonant transition shape similarity (model II), target-locus scaling (model III), exponential transition shapes (model IVa), and exponential duration dependence (model IVb). In model II, the CV and VC trajectories are respectively defined as L + k*f*(n) and L' + k'*g*(n), with contour shape functions f* and g*, scale factors k and k', and consonant loci L and L'. In model III, the scale factors k and k' are proportional to the target-locus distances T - L and T - L'. In model IVa, the shape functions f* and g* are fit to exponentials with asymptotes associated with the vowel targets. Target-locus scaling is a corollary of this vowel target definition. Finally, in model IVb, the exponential model is redefined on a recovered real-time scale. The resulting context effect follows Lindblom's [J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 35, 1773-1781 (1963)] in decaying exponentially with vowel duration.

## Interpretation

This paper provides a complete mathematical framework for modeling how consonant context shapes vowel formant trajectories. The progressive model refinement demonstrates that: (1) CV and VC transitions superpose additively, (2) transition shapes are similar across vowels for a given consonant (the common-contour hypothesis), (3) transition magnitudes scale with target-locus distance, and (4) transition shapes are exponential in real time, naturally producing Lindblom-style vowel undershoot at short durations. For a Klatt synthesizer, this provides a principled way to compute formant trajectories from a small set of parameters (consonant loci, time constants, scale factors) rather than specifying formant values frame by frame.
