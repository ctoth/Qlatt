Hanson and Stevens (2003) challenge the classical three-phase model of voiceless aspirated stop releases by examining whether the "aspiration" phase actually contains frication noise generated at the supraglottal constriction rather than pure glottal aspiration. Using formant-amplitude tracking against an /h/ baseline across four speakers, they find that /t/ releases show frication dominance in the F4-F5 region for all subjects, /k/ releases show F2-F3 frication for some subjects, while /p/ consistently follows the classical aspiration model. This is directly relevant to Klatt-based stop synthesis: the AF (frication amplitude) parameter may need place-dependent extension into the aspiration interval for alveolar and velar stops, rather than switching cleanly to AH (aspiration amplitude) after the burst.
