This paper provides quantitative measurements of fundamental frequency perturbations caused by prevocalic consonant voicing distinctions in English and Yoruba, demonstrating that voiceless stops raise F0 of following vowels by approximately 10 Hz (8%) while voiced stops lower it, with the effect persisting for over 100 ms in non-tonal languages and 40-60 ms in tonal languages. The authors evaluate competing hypotheses (aerodynamic vs. vocal-cord tension) for the perturbation mechanism, conduct perceptual experiments establishing that 10 Hz F0 perturbations become detectable at ramp durations of 60 ms or more, and present cross-linguistic evidence that these phonetic "seeds" drive the historical development of lexical tone systems (tonogenesis). This work is directly relevant to the Qlatt synthesizer's F0 microprosody rules, providing the empirical basis for voiceless-onset F0 raising and establishing quantitative bounds (~8% rather than the currently implemented 20%) on the magnitude and duration of consonant-induced F0 perturbation.
