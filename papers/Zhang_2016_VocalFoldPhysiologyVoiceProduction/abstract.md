# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

The goal of this study was to better understand the cause-effect relationship between vocal fold physiology and the resulting vibratory pattern and voice acoustics. Using a three-dimensional continuum model of phonation, the effects of changes in vocal fold stiffness, resting medial surface thickness, resting glottal angle, and subglottal pressure on the resulting vocal fold vibration and vocal output were investigated. The results show that the medial surface shape has the most significant effect on the vertical phase difference between the upper and lower margins of the medial surface, the open and closed quotients of vibration, and voice production. The medial surface shape, along with subglottal pressure, also affected phonation threshold and voice intensity. Vocal fold stiffness had the most significant effect on the fundamental frequency of vibration. The results are discussed in terms of source-filter interaction and the cause-effect relationship between vocal fold physiology, vocal fold vibration, and the resulting voice production.

---

## Our Interpretation

This study uses a physics-based 3D computational model to figure out which physical properties of the vocal folds control which aspects of the voice. The most important finding is that the shape/thickness of the medial surface of the vocal folds is the primary "knob" for voice quality (breathy to pressed), while stiffness controls pitch and subglottal pressure controls loudness. For speech synthesis, this validates treating pitch, loudness, and voice quality as largely independent control dimensions and provides concrete parameter mappings from physiology to LF glottal source parameters.
