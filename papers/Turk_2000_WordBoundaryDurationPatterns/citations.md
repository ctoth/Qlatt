# Turk & Shattuck-Hufnagel 2000 — Citations

## Full Reference
Turk, A. & Shattuck-Hufnagel, S. (2000). Word-boundary-related duration patterns in English. *Journal of Phonetics*, 28, 397–440. DOI: 10.1006/jpho.2000.0123

## Key Citations (implementation-relevant)

### Duration Modeling
- **Klatt, D.H. (1976).** Linguistic uses of segmental duration in English: Acoustic and perceptual evidence. *JASA*, 59, 1208–1221. — Foundational duration rules for TTS; Turk & Shattuck-Hufnagel's findings refine/correct the word-boundary component.
- **Crystal, T.H. & House, A.S. (1988).** Segmental durations in connected-speech signals: Current results. *JASA*, 83, 1553–1573. — Duration patterns in connected speech; phrase-level effects.
- **van Santen, J.P.H. (1992).** Contextual effects on vowel duration. *Speech Communication*, 11, 513–546. — Vowel duration modeling.
- **Wightman, C.W., Shattuck-Hufnagel, S., Ostendorf, M. & Price, P.J. (1992).** Segmental durations in the vicinity of prosodic phrase boundaries. *JASA*, 91, 1707–1717. — Phrase-boundary lengthening (distinct from word-boundary effects).

### Word-Initial Lengthening
- **Oller, D.K. (1973).** The effect of position in utterance on speech segment duration in English. *JASA*, 54, 1235–1247. — Early evidence for positional duration effects.
- **Fougeron, C. & Keating, P.A. (1997).** Articulatory strengthening at edges of prosodic domains. *JASA*, 101, 3728–3740. — Prosodic-domain-initial strengthening (articulatory, related to acoustic lengthening).
- **Cooper, A.M. (1991).** An articulatory account of aspiration in English. *Ph.D. dissertation*, Yale University. — Articulatory basis for word-initial effects.

### Polysyllabic Shortening
- **Lehiste, I. (1972).** The timing of utterances and linguistic boundaries. *JASA*, 51, 2018–2024. — Early evidence for compression in longer words.
- **Port, R.F. (1981).** Linguistic timing factors in combination. *JASA*, 69, 262–274. — Duration compression effects.
- **Turk, A. & Shattuck-Hufnagel, S. (1994).** Syllable timing: Evidence for the equal spacing tendency. *Proceedings of the 3rd International Conference on Spoken Language Processing*. — Predecessor work on syllable ratio equalization.

### Accentual Lengthening
- **Beckman, M.E. & Edwards, J. (1994).** Articulatory evidence for differentiating stress categories. In P.A. Keating (Ed.), *Phonological Structure and Phonetic Form: Papers in Laboratory Phonology III* (pp. 7–33). Cambridge University Press.
- **Turk, A. & Sawusch, J.R. (1997).** The domain of accentual lengthening in American English. *Journal of Phonetics*, 25, 25–41. — Accentual lengthening extends beyond the accented syllable.

### Prosodic Structure
- **Nespor, M. & Vogel, I. (1986).** *Prosodic Phonology*. Dordrecht: Foris. — Prosodic hierarchy theory.
- **Selkirk, E.O. (1984).** *Phonology and Syntax: The Relation between Sound and Structure*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. — Prosodic word formation, function word cliticization.
- **Beckman, M.E. & Pierrehumbert, J.B. (1986).** Intonational structure in Japanese and English. *Phonology Yearbook*, 3, 255–309. — Prosodic phrasing.
