## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Soulodre & Lavoie (1999), AES 17th International Conference** (Annex 1 ref [4]) - the empirical comparison of large and small impairment assessment that justifies MUSHRA over BS.1116. This is the evidence base for the whole method and the single most useful follow-up if you need to argue why a multi-stimulus design is required.

2. **EBU (2000a), Draft EBU Recommendation B/AIM 022** (Annex 1 ref [2]) - the original MUSHRA specification that ITU-R BS.1534 standardized. Useful for understanding which requirements are original and which were added in the -2 and -3 revisions.

3. **Bech & Zacharov (2007), Perceptual audio evaluation: Theory, method and application** (both lists) - the standard monograph covering assessor selection, experimental design, and analysis for listening tests. The practical companion to this Recommendation.

4. **Maxwell & Delaney (2004), Designing experiments and analyzing data** (Attachment 4 ref [28]) - the repeated-measures / within-subjects framework the entire Attachment 4 analysis path assumes, including the omnibus-versus-post-hoc discrepancy discussion.

5. **Hochberg (1988), A sharper Bonferroni procedure** (Attachment 4 ref [15]) - the specific multiple-comparison correction the Recommendation mandates for post-hoc contrasts. Short and directly implementable.

6. **Oberfeld & Franke (2013), Evaluating the robustness of repeated measures analyses** (Attachment 4 ref [30]) - the source for the claim that increasing sample size does not fix non-normality in repeated-measures designs, which is the key caveat when planning a small listening test.
