# Abstract

There are two durational effects often cited as evidence for very different models of prosodic constituency in English. The first is "final lengthening" or "pre-boundary lengthening," interpreted as a durational correlate of disjuncture that can cause momentary cessation of speech. The second is "stress-timed shortening," a class of effects interpreted as indications of isochronous spacing of prosodically strong syllables. The two effects are similar in that both involve apparent adjustment of syllable durations dependent on constituency, but differ radically in the type of constituency implicit in their interpretations. The paper presents three experiments that attempt to differentiate these effects and locate them at specific levels of the prosodic hierarchy.

## Interpretation

This chapter is foundational for understanding how different prosodic boundary types affect segment duration in speech synthesis. The key takeaway for implementation is that phrase-final lengthening (at intonational phrase boundaries) and word-final lengthening (at lexical word edges) are distinct phenomena with different magnitudes and consistency. The paper uses a metrical grid framework compatible with ToBI annotation, making its findings directly applicable to rule-based duration models that reference prosodic boundary strength.
