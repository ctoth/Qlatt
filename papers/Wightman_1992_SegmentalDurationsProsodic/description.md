Wightman et al. (1992) examine segmental lengthening near prosodic phrase boundaries using a large corpus of radio news speech labeled with break indices. They demonstrate that pre-boundary lengthening is restricted to the rhyme (nucleus and coda) of the syllable immediately before the boundary, and does not extend to earlier segments or across the boundary. Using a z-score normalized duration measure that is invariant to speaking rate, they show that at least four distinct levels of prosodic boundaries can be distinguished by their lengthening patterns.
