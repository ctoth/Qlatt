## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Ref. 31 — Schumacher, "Ab initio calculations of the oscillations of a clarinet," Acustica 48, 72-85 (1981).** The single most relevant follow-up for this project. It extends the model to finite reed mass, replacing the integral system with an integro-differential one still solvable by forward time stepping. That transition is the structural analogue of going from a memoryless glottal valve to a mass-bearing two-mass vocal-fold model, and it also contains the detailed `r(t)` versus `g(t)` efficiency argument and worked reflection functions for bores with tone holes.
- **Ref. 19 — McIntyre and Woodhouse, "On the fundamentals of bowed-string dynamics," Acustica 43, 93-108 (1979).** The source of the hysteresis rule that resolves the Friedlander-Keller ambiguity, and of the multiple-reflection-function algorithm generalized in Appendix B. Needed by anyone implementing a nonlinear characteristic whose maximum slope exceeds the load-line slope, which includes most closing-valve source models.
- **Ref. 23 — Coltman, "Jet-drive mechanisms in edge tones and organ pipes," JASA 60, 725-733 (1976).** The experimental data the flute simulations are validated against, and the equivalent circuit whose excitation portion yields Eq. (8). The reference point for judging whether a jet-driven model is physically right rather than merely plausible.
- **Ref. 30 — Backus, "Small amplitude vibrations of the clarinet," JASA 35, 305-313 (1963).** Supplies the measured, asymmetric nonlinear characteristic that the paper's cubic (24) approximates. Directly relevant to whether a project's glottal characteristic has the asymmetry required for stable low-amplitude oscillation.
- **Ref. 58 — McIntyre and Schumacher, "On the triangular waveforms observed in long organ flue pipes at resonance."** Listed as "to be submitted" in 1983; carries the proof of the universal triangular limiting form and the heat-diffusion analogy for the amplitude limit. Worth checking whether it was ever published, since the universality argument is only asserted here.
