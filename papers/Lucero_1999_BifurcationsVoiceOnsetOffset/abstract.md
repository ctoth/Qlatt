# Abstract

Oscillation hysteresis is a common phenomenon in cases of flow-induced oscillation. In phonation, it is usually observed as different biomechanical configurations of the vocal fold system between voice onset and offset. From the perspective of the theory of nonlinear dynamical systems, the phenomenon may be described as a combination of a subcritical Hopf bifurcation at which an unstable limit cycle is generated from an equilibrium position of the vocal folds, and a saddle-node bifurcation between limit cycles at which the unstable limit cycle and a stable limit cycle coalesce and cancel each other. This description is investigated using a two-mass model of the vocal folds coupled to a two-tube approximation of the vocal tract. The purpose is to simulate the hysteresis in terms of voice output parameters as intensity and fundamental frequency. Bifurcation diagrams are derived to illustrate and discuss the results.
