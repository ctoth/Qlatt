# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

Attempts to add emotion effects to synthesised speech have existed for more than a decade now. Several prototypes and fully operational systems have been built based on different synthesis techniques, and quite a number of smaller studies have been conducted. This paper aims to give an overview of what has been done in this field, pointing out the inherent properties of the various synthesis techniques used, summarising the prosody rules employed, and taking a look at the evaluation paradigms. Finally, an attempt is made to discuss interesting directions for future development.

---

## Our Interpretation

Synthetic speech is intelligible but not natural, and the most conspicuous gap is emotional expressivity. The review's central finding is a trade-off: formant synthesis exposes the voice-source and vocal-tract parameters emotion needs but sounds robotic, unit selection sounds natural but exposes almost nothing controllable, and diphone synthesis sits between with F0 and duration only and no voice-quality control. It is relevant to a formant/source-filter synthesiser because it both justifies that architecture for emotion control and supplies six numeric rule sets (joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, boredom) with measured recognition rates of 44% to 94% against chance levels of 11% to 17%.
