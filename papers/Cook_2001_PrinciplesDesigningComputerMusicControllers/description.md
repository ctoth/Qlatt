Cook states thirteen design principles for real-time gestural controllers of computer-synthesized sound, drawn from fifteen years of building wind, voice, percussion, string, and everyday-object instruments, and argues each from named project successes and failures. The core claims are that sensing bandwidth is not the binding constraint on playability, that controllers must be designed against a specific musical target, that programmable and adaptive instruments frustrate ordinary players, and that an interface should yield attractive output on first contact with subtlety revealed later. For a formant/source-filter speech synthesizer the directly applicable material is SPASM, whose "well over 40 continuously controlled parameters" made the voice "a truly difficult instrument to control" and motivated few-to-many interpolated mappings, and SqueezeVox, which decomposes vocal-model control into breath at the bellows, pitch at the keyboard and a fine linear strip, and vowels and consonants at presets or a continuous pad.
