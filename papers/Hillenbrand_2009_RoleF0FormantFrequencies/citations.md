## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Hillenbrand, Houde & Gayvert (2006)**, JASA 119, 4041-4054 — the full description of the spectral envelope synthesizer (SES) used to generate every stimulus here, plus its intelligibility validation (96.9% for sentences). Required reading to actually implement this paper's synthesis method.
- **Klatt & Klatt (1990)**, JASA 87, 820-857 — the canonical study of male-female voice quality differences: aspiration noise, F1 bandwidth, first-harmonic amplitude, spectral tilt. This is the source for the residual gender cues that f0 and formant scaling alone cannot supply, and it maps directly onto Klatt-synthesizer parameters.
- **Hillenbrand, Getty, Clark & Wheeler (1995)**, JASA 97, 3099-3111 — the H95 vowel database (1,116 /hVd/ tokens, 45 men + 48 women, 12 vowels) underlying the discriminant analysis and Experiment 2 stimuli, and the standard modern reference for American English vowel formants.
- **Peterson & Barney (1952)**, JASA 24, 175-184 — origin of the 1.7041 f0 and 1.168 formant male-to-female scale factors used throughout.
- **Kawahara, Masuda-Kasuse & de Cheveigné (1999)**, Speech Communication 27, 187-207 — STRAIGHT, the competing high-quality source-filter analysis-resynthesis system used by the Smith/Patterson and Assmann studies this paper compares itself against.
- **Traunmüller (1984, 1988)** and **Fant (1975)** — non-uniform, vowel-specific male-female formant scaling. The correct treatment of the uniform-scaling simplification this paper flags as its main methodological limitation.
