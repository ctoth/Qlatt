Titze develops a quantitative theory of nonlinear source–filter interaction in phonation, giving a closed-form wave-reflection expression for glottal flow whose coupling parameter is mean glottal area divided by the parallel combination of subglottal and supraglottal entry areas, which reduces to the epilarynx tube cross-sectional area. Narrowing the epilarynx from 3.0 to 0.2 cm² raises radiated acoustic power and glottal efficiency by about a factor of ten but destabilizes vibration when dominant harmonics cross formants, producing F0 jumps, subharmonics, and chaos; inertive supraglottal reactance combined with compliant subglottal reactance is the ideal load, and harmonic clusters are best placed just below formants rather than tuned to them. For a formant or articulatory synthesizer this supplies a single physically grounded interaction knob that makes flow-pulse skewing, spectral tilt, and source ripple emergent rather than hand-tuned, plus a diagnostic for unwanted bifurcations during pitch glides.
