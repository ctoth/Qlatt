This paper presents an early Swedish text-to-speech system that operates entirely on ordered phonological rules without any word dictionary, arguing that rules are necessary even in dictionary-based systems for handling compounds, affixes, and novel words. The key contributions are a flexible rule notation for phonological transformations (similar to Sound Pattern of English but extended for continuous variables), a segment duration formula, and validation that rule-generated errors are acceptable when they match errors native speakers would make. This work is relevant as an early demonstration of rule-based TTS architecture, though the Swedish-specific stress rules and OVE III synthesizer differ substantially from English Klatt-based systems.
