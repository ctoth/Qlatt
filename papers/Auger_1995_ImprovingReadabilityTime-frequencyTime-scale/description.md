Auger and Flandrin generalize the reassignment method from the spectrogram to any bilinear time-frequency or time-scale distribution, moving each computed value from its grid point to the local center of gravity of the surrounding energy so that smoothing can suppress cross-terms without blurring the signal components. Their central practical result is that the reassignment operators reduce to ratios of two or three cheap extra transforms computed with the modified windows t·h(t) and h'(t), avoiding the numerical differentiation and phase unwrapping that had kept the original 1976 method unused, and the resulting representations are perfectly localized for chirps and impulses while the reassigned spectrogram and scalogram stay nonnegative. For a formant and source-filter synthesizer this supplies a directly implementable front end that collapses formant transitions onto their instantaneous frequency law and glottal closure instants onto their true excitation time, at roughly three times the cost of a spectrogram already being computed.
