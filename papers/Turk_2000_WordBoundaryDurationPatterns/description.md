Systematic evaluation of five proposed word-level duration adjustment mechanisms (word-final lengthening, word-initial lengthening, polysyllabic shortening, accentual lengthening, syllable ratio equalization) using segmentally matched English triads where word boundary location varies while phonemic content stays constant. Support was found for word-initial lengthening, polysyllabic shortening, accentual lengthening, and syllable ratio equalization, but not for word-final lengthening as a separate mechanism. Function word boundaries produce weaker duration effects than content word boundaries, consistent with function words cliticizing to adjacent content words.
