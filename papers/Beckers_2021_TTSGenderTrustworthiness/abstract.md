# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

When text is presented through a computer-generated voice, Text-To-Speech (TTS) technology is being used. This technology has already served people with blindness and visual impairment for several decades. The increased voice quality makes it now particularly useful for the reading of long-form content, such as the increasing amount of news articles that media companies are offering their audience to listen to. These types of long-form content require a suitable voice for a pleasurable listening experience during a longer period. Previous work has found that individuals use voice attributes as cues to apply interpersonal social rules and expectations to computers, also known as the Computers As Social Actors (CASA) paradigm. With regards to TTS, this means that when the computer 'talks' through the TTS voice, people respond based on the psychological rules and expectation of interpersonal interaction. The current study aimed to determine whether a difference in TTS voice gender (male versus female) and accent (national vs international) affects the listening experience and trustworthiness of informational TTS news content and the mediating role of interpersonal concepts similarity-attraction and content-accent congruence. The results showed that a higher similarity of TTS voice accent evoked a more positive listening experience. Results regarding the trustworthiness of the TTS voice showed that when an accent is perceived as more similar, it is also perceived as more trustworthy and that male voices were generally perceived as more trustworthy than female voices. Based on the results of the experiment and interviews, the current study concluded that a difference in TTS voice accent (national vs foreign) and TTS voice gender (male vs female) evoke a difference in the listening experience and trustworthiness when reading informational news content.

---

## Our Interpretation

This study shows that TTS voice gender and accent affect how listeners evaluate news content: male voices are perceived as more trustworthy (a ~1 point difference on a 7-point scale), and voices matching the listener's own accent produce better listening experiences through similarity-attraction. For Qlatt voice persona design, this means the social identity projected by a synthetic voice has measurable effects on listener engagement and trust, independent of synthesis quality.
