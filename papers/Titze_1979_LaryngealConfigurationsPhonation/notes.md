# Titze & Talkin 1979 - Implementation Notes

**Paper:** A theoretical study of the effects of various laryngeal configurations on the acoustics of phonation
**Authors:** Ingo R. Titze, David T. Talkin
**Journal:** J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 66(1), 60-74, July 1979
**DOI:** 10.1121/1.382973

## Overview

Finite-difference simulation of vocal fold vibration in a three-dimensional viscoelastic tissue model with layered structure (mucosa, ligament, muscle). The model maps physiological vocal fold parameters to acoustic outputs: F0, volume velocity, oral acoustic power, and vocal efficiency.

## Key Contribution for Synthesis

This paper provides the foundational body-cover theory quantification: how control parameters of the layered vocal fold structure (geometrical shape, tissue elasticity, viscosity, subglottal pressure) independently affect F0, airflow, power, and efficiency. Directly relevant to understanding how glottal source parameters map to voice quality in a Klatt-type synthesizer.

## Model Parameters (Nominal Configuration for Male Voice)

### Geometrical Parameters (Section II, Fig. 1)

| Parameter | Symbol | Nominal Value | Description |
|-----------|--------|---------------|-------------|
| Length | L | 1.4 cm | Vocal processes to anterior commissure |
| Thickness | T | 0.5 cm | Including mucosal layer, ligament, conus elastica |
| Depth | D | varies (max 1.0 cm at processes, min 0.5 cm) | Depth decreases according to f = 1 - 5y^5/L^5 |
| Superior-medial deviation | w | 0.0 rad | Angular deviation of superior-medial edge |
| Shaping factor | s | 0.05 rad | Controls medial surface contour (quadratic) |
| Inferior surface angle | q | 0.7 rad (~40 deg) | Angular deviation from horizontal plane |

### Glottal Width Function (Eq. 1)

```
g(y,z) = 2w(L - y) + 2[-s(T - z) + (T - z)^2] / f
```

At z = T (superior surface): width increases linearly from zero at anterior to 2wL at processes.

### Tissue Layer Properties (Section V, Eq. 25)

Three-layer system with 10:4:2 ratio for transverse shear moduli (muscle:ligament:mucosa):

| Property | Symbol | Mucosa | Ligament | Muscle |
|----------|--------|--------|----------|--------|
| Transverse shear elasticity | mu | 0.2 mu_o | 0.4 mu_o | 1.0 mu_o |
| Transverse shear viscosity | eta | 0.5 eta_o | -- | 1.0 eta_o |
| Longitudinal shear elasticity | mu' | 0.5 mu_o' | 1.0 mu_o' | 1.0 mu_o' |
| Longitudinal shear viscosity | eta' | n_o' | n_o' | n_o' |

Where:
- mu_o = transverse shear elasticity of relaxed muscular tissue; nominal 20,000 dyn/cm^2
- mu_o' = longitudinal shear elasticity of relaxed muscular tissue (stress due to elongation); nominal 50,000 dyn/cm^2
- eta_o = transverse shear viscosity of relaxed muscular tissue; nominal 6 P (poise)
- eta_o' = longitudinal shear viscosity of relaxed muscular tissue; nominal 10 P
- P_s = subglottal pressure; nominal 8 cm H2O

### Aerodynamic Model

- Glottis divided into M adjacent ducts in y direction
- Each duct formed by N elements vertically
- N time-varying areas A(j, k, n) computed
- Glottal resistances and inductances from Ishizaka and Flanagan (1972) applied per duct
- Turbulent losses at entry/exit included
- Pressure profile generated by solving first-order equation (Eq. 23)

### Glottal Area Computation (Eq. 24)

```
A(j, k, n) = 1/4 [g(j-1, k, n) + 4g(j, k, n) + g(j+1, k, n)] * delta_y
```
(Simpson's rule integration, delayed by one sample for flow computation)

## Key Results: Parameter Sensitivity (Section VII, Fig. 5)

### F0 Control (Fig. 5a)

1. **F0 vs. Length (L):** Inverse relationship. 30% decrease in length (L=1.4 to L=0.98) causes 28% increase in F0. Consistent with vibrating string analogy.
2. **F0 vs. Ligament stress (mu_L'):** Strong positive correlation. Ligament tension is the primary F0 raising mechanism.
3. **F0 vs. Vocalis stress (mu_o'):** Moderate positive correlation. Muscular tension also raises F0 but less effectively.
4. **F0 vs. Thickness (T):** Minimal effect. Changes in thickness produce small F0 changes.
5. **F0 vs. Subglottal pressure (P_s):** Small effect under linear stress-strain assumptions. Noticeable only in the region of small subglottal pressure where the opening quotient varies substantially.

### F0 Estimation Formula (Eq. 31)

For a vibrating string model of length L with mu' representing combined tissue stress:

```
F0 = (1 / 2L) * (mu' / rho)^(1/2)
```

where rho is tissue density. A 30% decrease in length raises F0 by 43%. Nominal F0 = 101 Hz when mu' is taken as average of mu_o' and mu_L' (i.e., ~75,000 dyn/cm^2).

### Volume Velocity (Fig. 5b)

- Most significant factors: L (length), P_s (subglottal pressure), vocal fold abduction, ligament stress
- Vocal fold length simply increases glottis longitudinally
- Vocal fold abduction increases the glottis laterally
- mu_o' and P_s change the size of the glottis laterally by changing vibrational amplitude
- Stiffer vocal folds generally reduce amplitude; increased P_s has opposite effect
- Thickness and depth have slight negative correlation (effective reduction of glottal width)

### Acoustic Power Output (Fig. 5c)

- Most significant: L, P_s
- Local maxima exist: acoustic power can be maximized by tuning configurational parameters
- Optimal ratio of ligament-to-muscle stress approximately 1:1 (mu_L'/mu_o' ~ 0.5 of nominal)
- Reduction in mu_L' from 100,000 to 50,000 dyn/cm^2 produces optimal power and efficiency

### Vocal Efficiency (Fig. 5d)

- Defined as ratio of oral acoustic power output to aerodynamic input power
- Efficiency maximized when muscular layers are somewhat more tense than nonmuscular layers (mu_o' ~ 0.5 * mu_L')
- Maximum efficiency ~16% in the model
- Viscosity eta_o reduces efficiency: increase from 5 to 8 P reduces efficiency from ~15% to ~9%
- Shaping parameters (s, q) have limited effect; w (adduction parameter) is critical -- phonation only in narrow range near nominal

## Body-Cover Theory Quantification

The paper provides quantitative verification of Hirano's body-cover theory:
- **Cover** = epithelium (mucous membrane) + superficial layer (mucosa in this model)
- **Body** = muscle (vocalis)
- **Ligament** = intermediate layer between body and cover

Key finding: F0 can be raised by:
1. Increasing longitudinal tension of nonmuscular (mucosal and ligamental) tissue layers relative to muscular layers
2. The most effective F0 control mechanism is ligament stress (mu_L')
3. Muscular stress (mu_o') has a secondary effect through transverse coupling

The "optimum ratio" for power and efficiency: reduction in mu_L' equivalent to achieving approximately 1:1 ratio of mu_L'/mu_o' (both at ~50,000 dyn/cm^2).

## Control Parameter Reduction (Eq. 32)

The paper proposes that a four or five parameter model could capture the essential behavior:

```
c_i = c_i(T_c, T_v, T_l, P_s)
```

Where:
- T_c = tension in cricothyroid muscle (controls L and mu_L')
- T_v = tension in vocalis muscle (controls mu_o')
- T_l = tension in arytenoid adductors
- P_s = subglottal pressure

## Relevance to Klatt Synthesis

1. **F0 control:** The body-cover theory explains why F0 is primarily controlled by CT (cricothyroid) tension in speech, with subglottal pressure playing a minor role. This justifies treating F0 as an independent parameter in the Klatt model.

2. **Voice quality mapping:** The tissue layer properties (stiffness ratios) affect not just F0 but also the shape of the glottal waveform (open quotient, speed quotient), which maps to voice quality parameters. Breathy voice correlates with lax cover; pressed voice with tense cover.

3. **Source amplitude:** The finding that acoustic power has local maxima as parameters vary explains why AV (voicing amplitude) in the Klatt model cannot simply be derived from F0 and P_s -- it depends on the laryngeal configuration.

4. **Efficiency:** The ~16% maximum vocal efficiency figure provides a reference for source amplitude calibration.

## Limitations

- Linear stress-strain model (no nonlinear tissue mechanics)
- No nonlinear coupling between parameters (each varied independently)
- No vocal tract coupling in the acoustic output
- Subglottal system treated as simple pressure source
- Mucosal wave not explicitly tracked (combined mucosal layers)
