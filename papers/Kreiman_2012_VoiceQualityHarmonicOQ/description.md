This paper empirically tests the assumed relationship between glottal open quotient (OQ), the spectral measure H1*-H2*, and perceived voice quality (breathiness/pressedness) using synchronous high-speed laryngeal video and acoustic recordings from six speakers. The key finding is that while H1*-H2* is predictable (57-93% variance explained per speaker), the predictive model is speaker-dependent: some speakers control H1*-H2* primarily through OQ, others through pulse skewness (asymmetry coefficient), and others through F0, challenging the simple OQ-breathiness mapping assumed by KLGLOTT88. For formant synthesis voice quality modeling, this means that realistic breathy-to-pressed variation requires co-varying multiple glottal source parameters (OQ, pulse asymmetry, and potentially F0-dependent adjustments) rather than relying on a single OQ control.
