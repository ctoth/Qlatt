# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

This chapter has no formal printed abstract (it is a book chapter, not a journal article). The paper's introduction serves the equivalent function:

"The production of prosody is subject to a multitude of factors. Prosodic patterns are affected by dialect, speaking style, discourse structure, syntax, the words spoken, the segmental phonetic structure of the utterance, speaker habit, gender, age and social background. In addition, these factors interact. Nevertheless, variation has not been a major concern of prosodists... In this paper, I present evidence for intonational variation in dialects of English spoken in the British Isles. I will discuss (1) variation conditioned by dialect and (2) variation across speakers of a single dialect."

## Our Interpretation

The paper argues that intonational-phonology research has wrongly treated dialect and speaker variation as noise to be averaged away, and uses the IViE corpus (9 British Isles urban dialects, 108 adolescent speakers, 36 hours of speech, 5 speaking styles) to show the opposite: dialect systematically shapes both the phonetic realization of pitch accents (truncation vs. compression) and their phonological identity (which nuclear tune is chosen for a given sentence type). It is a synthesis/discussion chapter over the IViE project's findings (particularly Grabe, Post, Nolan & Farrar 2000 for the truncation/compression result), extended with a new cross-linguistic comparison to Haan's (2002) Dutch question-marker/final-rise trade-off. Relevant to any project building dialect-specific rather than single-standard-variety intonation rules.
