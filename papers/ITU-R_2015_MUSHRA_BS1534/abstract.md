# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

This Recommendation has no conventional abstract. The text below is the verbatim "Scope" section from p.1, which serves that function.

**Scope**

This Recommendation describes a method for the subjective assessment of intermediate audio quality. This method mirrors many aspects of Recommendation ITU-R BS.1116 and uses the same grading scale as is used for the evaluation of image quality (i.e. Recommendation ITU-R BT.500). The method, called "MUlti Stimulus test with Hidden Reference and Anchor (MUSHRA)", has been successfully tested. These tests have demonstrated that the MUSHRA method is suitable for evaluation of intermediate audio quality and gives accurate and reliable results.

**Keywords**

Listening test, artifacts, intermediate audio quality, audio coding, subjective assessment, audio quality

---

## Our Interpretation

Existing subjective-quality standards could not measure systems in the intermediate quality band: ITU-R BS.1116 discriminates poorly at the bottom of its scale, ITU-R BS.1284 offers no absolute scoring there, and the ITU-T P.800 family is confined to telephone-band speech. MUSHRA fills that gap with a double-blind multi-stimulus test on a 0-100 continuous quality scale, in which a hidden full-bandwidth reference and two mandatory low-pass anchors at 3.5 kHz and 7 kHz sit alongside the systems under test and anchor the scale. The document is fully normative and prescriptive: it fixes stimulus length, loop and fade mechanics, listening level, assessor screening thresholds, outlier rules, and the statistical treatment, which makes it directly usable as an implementation specification for a listening-test harness evaluating synthesized speech against natural reference recordings.
