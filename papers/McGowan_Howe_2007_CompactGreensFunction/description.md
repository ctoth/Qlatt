This paper extends the classical acoustic theory of speech production by introducing compact Green's functions that decompose the source-to-listener relationship into a far-field transfer function component and a source-region coupling component that depends on the detailed 3D geometry of the obstruction where aerodynamic noise is generated. The key finding is that sharp edges like incisors are far more effective at converting vortex-flow energy into acoustic radiation than rounded surfaces like the false vocal folds, providing a physical explanation for why sibilant fricatives are 10-15 dB louder than aspiration noise despite comparable flow velocities. For Klatt-based speech synthesis, this work provides theoretical grounding for the separate AF/AH noise source architecture and informs the amplitude ratios, spectral tilt differences, and frequency-dependent effectiveness of frication versus aspiration sources.
