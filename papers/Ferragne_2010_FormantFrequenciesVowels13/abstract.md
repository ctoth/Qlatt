# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

This study is a formant-based investigation of the vowels of male speakers in 13 accents of the British Isles. It provides F1/F2 graphs (obtained with a semi-automatic method) which could be used as starting points for more thorough analyses. The article focuses on both phonetic realization and systemic phenomena, and it also provides detailed information on automatic formant measurements. The aim is to obtain an up-to-date picture of within- and between-accent vowel variation in the British Isles. F1/F2 graphs plot z-scored Bark-transformed formant frequencies, and values in Hertz are also provided. Along with the findings, a number of methodological issues are addressed.

---

## Our Interpretation

The paper asks whether existing (often decades-old) descriptions of British Isles vowel systems still hold, and answers with a single consistent acoustic methodology applied to 13 regional accents from the ABI corpus. The key finding is that both between-accent systemic differences (mergers/splits of FOOT-STRUT, NURSE-SQUARE, FOOT-GOOSE, etc.) and within-accent/within-speaker variability are substantial, and that averaging formants without first classifying phonetic variants (monophthong vs. diphthong, split vs. merged) can be misleading. It is relevant here as a source of concrete, accent-differentiated vowel target data (median Hz values, diphthong trajectory shapes) for building or validating per-accent synthetic vowel inventories.
