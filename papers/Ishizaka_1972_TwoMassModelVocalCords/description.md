Derives the two-mass self-oscillating vocal-cord model, in which each cord is split in depth into two lateral mass-spring-damper oscillators joined by a coupling stiffness, driven by quasi-steady Bernoulli flow and coupled to a four-section vocal-tract transmission line, with the whole system reduced to explicit difference equations and simulated on a DDP-516. The model reproduces a 55-degree cord-edge phase difference, a duty ratio near 0.6 falling toward 0.5 with rising lung pressure, a fundamental frequency that rises 2 to 3 Hz per cm H2O of subglottal pressure independently of vowel, a chest/falsetto analogue under coupling-stiffness control, and a hard rest-area ceiling near 0.25 cm squared beyond which phonation stops; unlike the earlier one-mass model it sustains oscillation when the tract driving-point reactance is capacitive, and it reproduces the frequency jumps measured on a human phonating into a motor-driven sliding pipe. For this project it is the canonical physically-parameterized glottal source, replacing ad-hoc waveform knobs with subglottal pressure, cord tension, rest area, and tract shape, and supplying the momentum-based glottal outlet pressure recovery that governs how strongly the tract perturbs the flow pulse.
