# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

This study investigates the perceptual contributions of formant transitions and nasal murmurs to the identification of the unreleased Catalan nasal consonants [n], [ɲ], [ŋ] (alveolar, palatal, velar, respectively) after [a] in absolute final position. Transition and murmur patterns were synthesized from synthetic stimuli as [n], [ɲ], and [ŋ]. The main findings were: (1) Although transitions provided more effective cues for place of articulation than murmurs, the murmurs did make a significant contribution to the [ŋ]–[n] distinction. (2) The cue value of the transitions ([ɲ] > [n], [ŋ]) was inversely related to that of the murmurs ([ŋ], [n] > [ɲ]). It is concluded that static and dynamic place cues for nasals in an [aC#] context are perceptually integrated with reference to the typical pattern of production of these consonants.

---

## Our Interpretation

The paper asks: when listeners hear a nasal consonant at the end of a syllable, what acoustic information tells them *where* in the mouth it was made? The answer is that both the changing formant frequencies leading into the nasal (transitions) and the steady spectral shape of the nasal itself (murmur) matter, but their relative importance flips depending on which nasal — transitions dominate for palatal [ɲ], while murmur spectrum matters more for velar [ŋ]. For speech synthesis, this means we need both accurate formant transitions AND distinct murmur spectra to produce nasals that listeners can reliably tell apart.
