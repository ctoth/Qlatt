Perceptual study demonstrating that spectral balance (energy distribution across frequency bands) is a strong cue for the perception of linguistic stress, while overall intensity is only marginally effective. Through two experiments manipulating duration, intensity level, and spectral balance of reiterant nonsense words, the authors show that duration is the primary stress cue (68-93% variance) and spectral balance is the second most important (up to 90% of intensity-related variance), while overall intensity level contributes approximately 1%. The findings are particularly relevant for speech synthesis, where stress is traditionally implemented via intensity and duration changes alone, neglecting the spectral tilt modifications that listeners actually rely on.
