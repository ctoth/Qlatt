Nordstrom (1977) investigates how male vocal tract area functions (from Fant 1960) can be perturbed to simulate female and infant vocal tracts, using both length-only and combined length-plus-volume scaling of mouth and pharynx cavities independently. The study finds that non-uniform length scaling alone has surprisingly little effect on formant patterns compared to uniform scaling, but volume scaling (reducing cross-sectional areas along with length) produces a systematic F1 increase averaging 11.8%. The key conclusion is that anatomical size differences only partially explain observed male-female formant differences, suggesting that vocal tract shape (form) also varies between speaker categories.
