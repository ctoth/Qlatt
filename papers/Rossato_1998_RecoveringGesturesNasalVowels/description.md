This paper uses Maeda's articulatory model to generate synthetic databases of nasal vowel transfer functions with varying velar port areas and area ratios, then applies Bayesian probabilistic inversion to estimate velum position from cepstral coefficients. Key findings show that velar port area is reliably estimated for small openings (< 0.8 cm^2) while the area ratio d = An/(An + Aoral) is a more robust parameter across the full range of nasality, and that nasality projects onto a spectral axis nearly perpendicular to the oral vowel subspace. For formant synthesis, the area ratio parameterization and the pharyngo-nasal spectral target (~300/800 Hz) offer insights into how nasal coupling modifies vowel spectra, though the paper's inversion focus is the reverse of the synthesis direction.
