This paper investigates whether prosody alone can convey communicative intentions (speech acts) without lexical or contextual support, using acoustic analysis and perceptual experiments with German single-word stimuli spoken with six different intentions. The key finding is that distinct prosodic configurations exist for different speech acts, with pitch rise (offset-onset F0) being the primary discriminating feature, followed by mean F0 and intensity, achieving 92% acoustic classification accuracy. For Qlatt, this provides empirically-grounded prosodic profiles that could inform intention-aware prosody generation, particularly for mapping F0 contour shapes to pragmatic categories like warning, suggestion, or criticism.
