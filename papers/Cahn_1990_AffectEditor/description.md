Cahn presents the Affect Editor, a program that models the effects of emotion on speech as seventeen independently varying acoustical parameters spanning pitch, timing, voice quality and articulation, each quantified on a −10 to +10 scale with zero as neutral affect, and maps those values onto DECtalk3 formant-synthesizer instructions. It publishes complete parameter settings for angry, disgusted, glad, sad, scared and surprised, and a 28-subject forced-choice experiment recognized the intended affect in 53.5 percent of 834 presentations against a 17 percent chance baseline, rising to 78.7 percent when the dominant confusion is allowed and reaching 91 percent for sadness. For a formant or source-filter synthesizer aiming at emotion control by rule this is the canonical reusable emotion parameter table, an architecture that separates synthesizer-independent affect description from per-synthesizer interpretation, and a catalogue of DECtalk limitations that reads directly as requirements for a modern back end.
