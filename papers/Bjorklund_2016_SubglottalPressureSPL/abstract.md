# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

Subglottal pressure (Ps) shows a strong correlation with sound pressure level (SPL) and is easy to measure by means of commonly available equipment. The Ps to SPL relationship was determined at four pitches produced by 16 female and 15 male healthy voices and analyzed by means of regression analysis. The subjects produced sequences of the syllable [pae], and Ps was measured as the oral pressure during the [p] occlusion. The effect of pitch was insignificant. The average correlation between Ps and SPL was 0.83 and did not differ significantly between the female and male subjects. In female and male subjects, Ps = 10 cm H2O produced 78.1 dB and 80.0 dB SPL at 0.3 m, and a doubling of Ps generated 11.1 dB and 9.3 dB increase of SPL. Both these gender differences were statistically significant. In other words, male subjects produce slightly higher SPL for a given pressure than female subjects but gain less for a doubling of Ps. This relationship between SPL and Ps can be reliably established from series of repetitions of the syllable [pae] and reflects the SPL/Ps ratio. This ratio is strongly dependent on the efficiency of the phonatory apparatus and should be of great relevance to clinical practice as a tool for documenting phonatory dysfunction.

---

## Our Interpretation

This paper quantifies how efficiently the voice converts subglottal air pressure into acoustic output, finding a reliable log-linear relationship with r = 0.83 and distinct gender-specific slopes (females gain more SPL per pressure doubling than males). For our speaker personality system, this provides the normative Ps-to-SPL transfer function needed to model how changes in vocal effort translate to changes in output level, with the gender difference in phonatory efficiency being a key parameter for distinguishing male and female voice profiles.
