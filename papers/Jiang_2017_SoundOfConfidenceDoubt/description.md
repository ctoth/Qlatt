This paper investigates the perceptual-acoustic correlates of speaker confidence and doubt, establishing comprehensive prosodic profiles for confident, close-to-confident, unconfident, and neutral speech through perception experiments and detailed acoustic analysis. Key findings reveal that confidence is marked by higher amplitude, greater F0 and amplitude variation, faster speaking rate, and lower harmonics-to-noise ratio (suggesting relaxed phonation), while unconfident speech shows higher mean F0, rising final intonation, slower rate with more pauses, and higher HNR (tense phonation). For speech synthesis, this work provides actionable acoustic targets for generating graded confidence expressions through manipulation of F0 contours, intensity patterns, speaking rate, and voice quality parameters.
