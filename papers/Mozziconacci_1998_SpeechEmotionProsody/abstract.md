# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

The thesis is divided into two major parts. The first part addresses perceptually based optimal values for pitch level, pitch range, and speech rate in emotional speech synthesis. Through a series of controlled experiments (Experiments 1-7), optimal values for each of seven emotions are established by systematically manipulating these prosodic parameters and measuring listener perception of emotion category and naturalness. A framework for evaluating which intonation contour types suit which emotions is developed, with findings that emotion recognition can be achieved through rule-based generation of F0 contours achieving approximately 63% accuracy. Voice quality is identified as essential for expressing certain emotions like sadness and fear, where pitch variation alone proves insufficient.

The second part focuses on fundamental frequency (F0) fluctuations and pitch variations in emotional speech. A global analysis of pitch level and pitch range is conducted, followed by refined analysis of intonation patterns at refined temporal resolution. A two-component intonation model is proposed to describe pitch value variations and their perceptual significance. The work establishes that pitch contour type and pitch implementation contribute independently to emotion perception, with complex F0 dynamics being approximable through simpler rule-based approaches for synthesis applications.

---

## Our Interpretation

Mozziconacci's thesis quantifies the prosodic acoustic parameters required to synthesize recognizable emotions in speech, establishing that pitch (F0), pitch range, and speech rate can be systematically manipulated to convey distinct emotional states. The research demonstrates that while F0 contours are important, voice quality variations are equally critical for certain emotions—a finding essential for formant synthesizers like Klatt that can control both spectral parameters and F0 trajectories. By showing that complex intonation patterns can be approximated with simpler rule-based models (achieving ~63% emotion recognition), the work directly enables practical emotion synthesis in text-to-speech systems through explicit parameter rules rather than requiring natural speech recordings or neural networks.
