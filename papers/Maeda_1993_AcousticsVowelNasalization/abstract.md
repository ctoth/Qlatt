# Abstract

When the velum is sufficiently lowered during the production of a vowel, the nasal tract becomes acoustically coupled to the main vocal tract, and the resulting sound is perceived by listeners as nasalized or nasal. The chapter investigates vowel nasalization by means of simulation, employing area functions of the vocal tract appropriate for each vowel and for the nasal tract, examining the acoustical structure of vowels as a function of the degree of coupling between the two tracts. Four distinct types of spectral modification due to nasal coupling are identified, depending on the distribution of nasal critical resonances and vowel formant frequencies. A spectral measure for perceived nasalization (N1-N2 distance in Bark) is proposed and validated against perceptual data. Articulatory shifts observed in French nasal vowels are explained as acoustically motivated adjustments that enhance the nasalization percept by positioning antiformants near vowel formants.

## Interpretation

This chapter provides a detailed acoustic model of how nasal coupling modifies vowel spectra, with direct implications for formant synthesizers. The key finding for synthesis is that nasalization effects are highly vowel-dependent: high vowels require only small coupling for perceptible nasalization (antiformant near F1), while low vowels like [a] require much larger coupling or compensatory articulatory shifts. The proposed N1-N2 distance measure could serve as an objective quality metric for evaluating synthetic nasalized vowels. The articulatory shift analysis suggests that French nasal vowels are not simply oral vowels with added nasalization but involve coordinated labial and lingual gestures, which a synthesizer should model as combined parameter changes (formant shifts + nasal pole/zero).
