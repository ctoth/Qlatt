Introduces Combilex, an accent-independent pronunciation lexicon that uses a superset "metaphone" symbol set for base-form transcriptions and automatically derives accent-specific surface-form lexica (RP, GAM, or individual speakers), extending explicit expert phone-to-grapheme alignment through that derivation. Shows empirically that training letter-to-sound rules on Combilex's manual alignment beats automatic alignment (86.50% vs 84.49% words correct) and that Combilex outperforms the older OALD lexicon. Complements the Unisyn keyword-lexicon paper as a second concrete case study of one lexicon plus per-accent derivation rules for a declarative TTS frontend.
