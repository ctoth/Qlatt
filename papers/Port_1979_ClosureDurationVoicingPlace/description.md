Port (1979) investigates how speaking tempo affects the perception of stop consonant voicing via closure duration, using synthesized continua embedded in natural fast and slow carrier sentences. The study demonstrates that the voiced/voiceless perceptual boundary shifts by about 10 ms toward shorter closure values in fast speech, establishing that closure duration as a voicing cue must be defined relative to speaking rate rather than as an absolute value. The paper also examines place-of-articulation effects, finding that closure duration alone is insufficient to distinguish place, with formant transitions providing the dominant cue.
