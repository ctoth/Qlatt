
## Zhang_2021_LaryngealSizeSexDifferences
This paper uses 216,000 computational simulations of a three-dimensional body-cover vocal fold model to systematically isolate how vocal fold length, thickness, and depth independently contribute to sex- and age-related differences in voice production across adult males, adult females, and children. The key finding is that length and thickness operate on orthogonal voice dimensions: length dominates F0, flow rate, and SPL differences (explaining why males have lower pitch and louder voices), while thickness dominates closed quotient, maximum flow declination rate, and spectral tilt H1-H2 (explaining voice quality differences), with depth having minimal effect. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker profile system, this provides the physical justification for parameterizing male-female-child voice presets along two independent axes (pitch/amplitude via a size parameter, and voice quality/spectral slope via a thickness parameter), and establishes that females require higher vocal fold contact pressure to achieve equivalent SPL, informing effort-dependent parameter curves.

## Stathopoulos_2011_VoiceAcrossLifespan
This paper presents a comprehensive cross-sectional study of fundamental frequency (F0), sound pressure level (SPL), and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) across the full human life span (ages 4-93) using 192 speakers, revealing nonlinear age trajectories that differ by sex. Key findings include that male F0 follows a U-shaped curve with minimum around age 50, female F0 declines more gradually, SNR shows sex-specific patterns linked to hormonal changes at menopause, and acoustic variability is elevated at both ends of the life span. For the Qlatt synthesizer, this work provides the empirical foundation for age-dependent voice parameter profiles, particularly F0 defaults, noise characteristics, and variability settings across child, adult, and elderly speaker categories.

## Karthikeyan_2023_ArticulatoryStatusAttractiveness
This paper investigates how men's articulatory clarity of word-final /t/ (aspirated vs unreleased) influences women's mating attractiveness evaluations and men's status perceptions, using speech samples from 80 male speakers rated by 91 participants. The key findings show that aspirated /t/ speakers are rated as more attractive for long-term relationships and higher in prestige (but not physical dominance), with jitter as the only acoustic parameter differentiating the two speaker groups, while F0 and formant frequencies do not differ. For speech synthesis voice profiles, this establishes that stop release parameters (VOT, burst amplitude, aspiration) contribute to perceived social status and attractiveness, suggesting that articulation precision should be a controllable dimension in voice preset design.


## Iseli_2007_VoiceSourceAgeSexVowel
This paper introduces and validates a spectral magnitude correction formula that removes the influence of vocal tract formant frequencies and bandwidths from harmonic amplitude measurements, enabling reliable estimation of voice source parameters H1*-H2* (open quotient) and H1*-A3* (spectral tilt) from speech spectra. Applying the correction to 3145 utterances from 335 speakers ages 8-39, the authors show that age has the largest effect on all three source measures (F0, H1*-H2*, H1*-A3*), with males showing a 4 dB drop in H1*-H2* and 10 dB drop in H1*-A3* from childhood to adulthood, while vowel dependencies are stronger for females suggesting greater vocal tract-source interaction. For the Qlatt synthesizer, this work provides both the correction formula needed to validate synthesized voice source characteristics against natural speech, and quantitative age/sex/vowel-dependent targets for open quotient and spectral tilt parameters across speaker profiles.

## Keating_2016_AcousticSimilarityFemaleVoices
This ASA poster presents a multi-dimensional scaling and linear discriminant analysis study of 50 female voices (ages 18-29) examining which of 26 acoustic parameters best distinguish individual speakers, using perceptual similarity ratings and VoiceSauce measurements. The key finding is that F0, F3, F4, CPP (cepstral peak prominence), SHR (subharmonic-to-harmonic ratio), and energy do most of the work of speaker discrimination, while traditional voice source spectral measures like H1*-H2* are surprisingly unimportant for distinguishing individual voices. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker personality system, this work establishes that speaker profile design should prioritize F0, higher formants (F3/F4), and periodicity measures over source spectral tilt parameters to create perceptually distinct voice profiles.

## Krumpholz_2022_PitchManipulationFemaleRatings
This paper investigates how increasing female voice pitch by approximately 20 Hz affects male listeners' ratings of attractiveness, femininity, health, and age in both audio-only and audiovisual conditions, using 20 speakers and 104 male raters in a within-subject design with naturalistic German sentences. The key finding is that increased voice pitch significantly boosts perceived femininity and reduces perceived age by ~2 years, but does not significantly affect perceived attractiveness or health, contradicting prior studies that used less naturalistic stimuli. For speech synthesis voice profiles, this establishes that modest F0 adjustments primarily affect femininity and age perception rather than attractiveness, and that cross-modal effects of voice pitch on face perception are limited to age judgments only.

## XuLee_VocalAttractivenessMandarinListeners
This conference paper tests whether Mandarin native listeners rate vocal attractiveness similarly to Western listeners, using VocalTractLab-synthesized stimuli that manipulate voice quality (breathy, modal, creaky, pressed), fundamental frequency, formant dispersion, and pitch range across 108 conditions per gender. The key findings show that breathy and modal voices are preferred over pressed voices, low f0 and narrow formant spacing increase male voice attractiveness, and narrow pitch range decreases attractiveness for both sexes, with creaky voice offering no attractiveness benefit to Mandarin listeners unlike its social cachet in American English. For speech synthesis voice profiles, this provides cross-cultural validation that voice quality preferences (breathy > modal > creaky/pressed) and formant spacing effects are partly universal, while pitch range preferences may be language-specific, and supplies concrete spectral tilt measurements (H1-H2*, H1-A1*, H1-A3*) for each voice quality type.

## Scherer_TaskLoadStressAcoustics
This conference paper reports a study of 100 speakers across three language groups distinguishing the acoustic effects of cognitive load from psychological stress using a computer-aided dual-task induction paradigm, finding that load primarily increases speech rate and energy attack/decay gradients while stress primarily raises F0 and shifts spectral energy upward from below 500 Hz to the 500-1600 Hz range. The key insight is that mean acoustic results hide large individual differences in stress responsivity, with only about 55% of speakers showing the predicted F0 increase under stress, and self-report stress correlating only with syllable duration. For speech synthesis, this establishes that load and stress should be modeled as separate voice modification dimensions with different parameter profiles, and provides approximate effect sizes for Klatt parameter adjustments.


## Cartei_2014_VoiceMasculinity
This paper uses two-level path analysis on 37 male speakers and 20 female listeners to determine how physiological characteristics (testosterone, height) relate to perceived vocal masculinity through acoustic mediators (F0 and formant spacing deltaF). The key finding is that testosterone's effect on perceived masculinity is entirely mediated by F0, while height's effect is only partially mediated by both F0 and deltaF, with F0 being the stronger cue (rho=-.53 vs rho=-.33) and the two acoustic dimensions being independent as predicted by source-filter theory. For the Qlatt speaker personality system, this establishes that F0 is the primary parameter for controlling perceived masculinity in voice presets, with formant spacing as a secondary independent control, and that these can be manipulated orthogonally without acoustic interaction.

## Beckers_2021_TTSGenderTrustworthiness
This master's thesis investigates how TTS voice gender (male vs female) and accent (national Dutch vs foreign Belgian) affect perceived trustworthiness and listening experience of news content, using a 2x2 between-subjects design with 326 Dutch participants and Google WaveNet voices. The key finding is that male TTS voices are rated significantly more trustworthy than female voices (M=5.09 vs M=4.00, p<.001) for news reading, while national accent voices produce a better listening experience mediated by perceived similarity-attraction, with qualitative interviews revealing that listeners associate male voices with "seriousness" and "expertise" despite acknowledging the gender bias. For speech synthesis voice persona design, this establishes that voice gender and regional accent are significant social variables affecting listener trust and engagement, though the thesis provides no acoustic parameter data as it uses commercial black-box TTS.


## HodgesSimeon_2010_DominanceAttractiveness
This paper examines how five vocal parameters (mean F0, F0 variation, formant dispersion, intensity, and duration) independently predict perceptions of physical dominance, social dominance, and attractiveness in 111 male speakers' natural speech from a mock dating-game paradigm, controlling for semantic content. The key finding is that different parameters predict different social outcomes: physical dominance is predicted by low F0 variation (beta=-.16), attractiveness by low mean F0 (beta=-.21 to -.27), high intensity (beta=.20-.27), and low formant dispersion (beta=-.17 to -.19, significant only for fertile-phase women), while social dominance is predicted solely by verbal content. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker personality system, this establishes that dominance and attractiveness are separable voice quality dimensions requiring different parameter adjustments, with F0 variation (prosodic dynamism) being a key predictor of perceived authority that is distinct from mean pitch level.

## Borkowska_2011_F0DominanceAttractiveness
This paper tests whether female voice fundamental frequency (F0) relates linearly to perceptions of dominance and attractiveness, using four natural female voices (184.6-310.3 Hz) each raised and lowered by 20 Hz and rated by male and female listeners. The key finding is that F0-dominance is linear (lower pitch = more dominant, with women more sensitive than men to this cue) while F0-attractiveness is nonlinear, increasing up to an optimal pitch around 280 Hz then sharply decreasing, with very high-pitched voices (~310 Hz) rated as least attractive. For the Qlatt speaker personality system, this establishes sex-specific voice parameter targets: unlike male voices where lower F0 uniformly increases attractiveness, female voice attractiveness has an inverted-U relationship with pitch, requiring a ~260-280 Hz target rather than simply lowering F0.

## Miller_1998_PronunciationModelingSpeechSynthesis
This dissertation addresses the gap between canonical dictionary pronunciations and natural surface speech by training neural networks to learn postlexical phonological transformations (flapping, deletion, glottalization, vowel reduction) directly from labeled speech data for individual speakers. The research demonstrates that allophonic variation is largely predictable from phonetic context alone, with the network achieving 87.8% exact match and 98% acceptable pronunciation accuracy, while revealing that /t/ and schwa have the highest entropy (most variable realizations). This work is critical for speech synthesis because it shows how to bridge the lexicon-phonetics gap and identify the most impactful allophony rules (/t/ deletion, flapping, glottalization and vowel fronting) that native speakers expect to hear in natural-sounding speech.

## Weiss_2020_VoiceAttractiveness
This edited volume (17 chapters, 366 pages) consolidates interdisciplinary research on voice attractiveness, social likeability, and vocal charisma, organized into five parts covering general considerations, voice quality, prosody, databases, and technological applications including robot voice design and uncanny valley effects. Key chapters address prosodic correlates of attractive voices, systematic reviews of vocal preferences, voice quality norms, F0 entrainment in conversations, and computational modeling of attractiveness from audio signals, with authors including Kreiman, Babel, Quene, and Burkhardt whose individual works are already in this collection. For Qlatt voice persona design, this volume serves as a comprehensive reference connecting acoustic parameters (F0, formant dispersion, spectral tilt, voice quality) to social perception dimensions, though only the table of contents and metadata were extracted in this pass due to the book's length.


## Larrouy-Maestri_2024_EmotionalProsody
This paper is a comprehensive 30-year literature review of emotional prosody research that synthesizes findings on the acoustic features—including pitch, duration, loudness, spectral quality, and dynamics—associated with different emotional states in speech. The authors identify key acoustic factors that distinguish emotions (e.g., high F0 and intensity for anger, low pitch and slow rate for sadness) while highlighting the persistent lack of consensus in the field due to variability in speech materials, measurement choices, cultural differences, and emotion granularity. For speech synthesis, this work provides concrete parameter profiles and implementation guidance for modulating formant synthesizers like Klatt (F0 contours, formant frequencies, spectral tilt, voice quality) to generate emotionally expressive synthetic speech.


## Henrich_2001_SpectralOqAsymmetry
This paper derives analytical spectral formulas for three glottal flow models (LF, R++, KLGLOTT88) and demonstrates that the commonly used spectral measure H1*-H2* cannot uniquely determine the glottal open quotient because it depends jointly on both open quotient and asymmetry coefficient in 5-parameter models, with only the simpler 4-parameter KLGLOTT88 model providing a monotonic Oq-to-H1*-H2* relationship. Experimental measurements on speech and singing signals reveal that many H1*-H2* values fall outside the domain predicted by any glottal flow model, suggesting either model inadequacy or significant source-filter interaction that invalidates inverse filtering. For the Qlatt synthesizer, this paper is a critical reference establishing that voice quality presets must set both Oq and asymmetry coefficient (not just Oq based on H1*-H2* literature values), and that H1*-H2* alone is insufficient for validating synthesized voice source parameters against natural speech.

## Lucero_2005_VocalFoldBifurcations
This paper presents a complete bifurcation analysis of the Titze (1988) mucosal wave vocal fold oscillator model, deriving the Lyapunov number that determines whether phonation onset is soft (supercritical Hopf) or hard (subcritical Hopf with hysteresis). The key finding is that for physiologically typical parameters the bifurcation is always supercritical, but an extended model with quartic nonlinear damping can produce the oscillation hysteresis observed experimentally between phonation onset and offset pressures. This is relevant to the Qlatt synthesizer for physically motivated modeling of glottal source onset/offset behavior, phonation threshold pressure, and register transitions.

## Nygren_2016_TransVoiceF0Testosterone
Longitudinal study tracking fundamental frequency (F0) changes in 50 trans men over 3-24 months of testosterone treatment, with measurements of speech range profiles and voice range profiles at six timepoints. Mean F0 decreased continuously from 192 Hz (female-typical) to 125 Hz (male-typical) by 12 months, with large interindividual variation (89-170 Hz range) and no correlation between F0 and androgen levels, suggesting AR-mediated saturation. Provides direct empirical evidence that vocal fold properties vary continuously rather than as a sex binary, supporting the use of continuous anatomical parameters (rather than categorical presets) in speaker personality modeling for formant synthesis.


## Fitch_1999_VocalTractMorphology
This paper uses MRI scans of 129 subjects ages 2-25 to establish normative data on vocal tract length and segment proportions, providing regression equations relating VTL to body size (height: r=0.926; log weight: r=0.941) and documenting that VTL scales allometrically as approximately the cube root of body mass. The key finding is that male-female vocal tract length differences arise at puberty through disproportionate pharyngeal elongation in males (pharynx accounts for ~6.8 mm of the 12.9 mm total sex difference), while oral cavity segments grow roughly uniformly and show no significant sex differences. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker profile system, this provides the quantitative basis for deriving vocal tract length from speaker body size parameters, establishes that oral and pharyngeal sections must be scaled independently (not uniformly) when modeling different speaker types, and demonstrates that child-to-adult voice changes require segment-specific growth curves rather than simple uniform VTL scaling.

## Titze_1989_MaleFemaleVoices
This paper derives two physiologic scale factors -- alpha (1.2, general larynx size) and beta (1.6, membranous vocal fold length) -- from cadaveric data and uses them to predict male-female differences in fundamental frequency, mean airflow, sound power, glottal efficiency, and glottographic waveform shape through a vibrating string model and computer simulation of vocal fold contact area. Key findings include that F0 differences are almost entirely explained by membranous length (F0 = 1700/L_m), that vocal fold thickness scales with the smaller alpha factor rather than beta, that male vocal folds exhibit medial surface bulging producing distinct contact area waveform features, and that females are predicted to be ~25% more glottally efficient than males due to higher F0. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker personality system, this work provides the physiological foundation for parameterizing male-female voice differences using orthogonal scale factors for size and length, principled scaling laws for airflow and power, glottal shape quotients (Q_a, Q_s, Q_b, Q_p) that could drive the LF source model, and stress-strain relationships that connect F0 range to tissue mechanics.

## Titze_1992_VocalIntensity
Titze and Sundberg derive analytical expressions for radiated acoustic power from the mouth as a function of glottal source waveform parameters, lung pressure, F0, and vocal tract transfer characteristics, combining radiation impedance theory with empirical glottal flow measurements from professional tenors and nonsingers. The key finding is that SPL increases at 8-9 dB per doubling of *excess pressure over threshold* (lung pressure minus phonation threshold pressure), with professional singers achieving 10-12 dB greater intensity than nonsingers at equivalent lung pressures due to 3-4x greater peak airflow derivatives. This paper is directly relevant to Qlatt's intensity control system, providing physics-based relationships between lung pressure, F0, spectral slope, and SPL that can drive loudness modeling, speaker personality differentiation (singer vs nonsinger profiles), and voicing onset thresholds via the phonation threshold pressure concept.

## Herzel_1994_VocalDisordersNonlinearDynamics
Applies nonlinear dynamics analysis techniques (phase space embedding, bifurcation classification, attractor visualization) to sustained vowel recordings from 95 dysphonic patients, demonstrating that approximately 25% exhibit deterministic nonlinear phenomena including period-doubling, subharmonics, tori, and chaos. The paper provides a unified terminology mapping clinical voice disorder descriptions (diplophonia, biphonation, vocal fry, creaky voice) to attractor types from dynamical systems theory, and identifies four desynchronization mechanisms (left-right vocal fold, horizontal-vertical modes, ventricular fold interaction, acoustic coupling) as likely physiological origins. Directly relevant to voice quality modeling in a formant synthesizer, as it provides a taxonomy of roughness/disorder types with distinct acoustic signatures (amplitude modulation frequencies, subharmonic patterns) that can guide parameter modulation strategies for simulating pathological and non-modal voice qualities.

## Isshiki_1964_VoiceIntensityRegulation
This paper simultaneously measures voice intensity (SPL), subglottic pressure, airflow rate, and glottal resistance during sustained vowel phonation across low, medium, and high pitch registers, establishing that voice intensity is approximately proportional to the 3.3 power of subglottic pressure regardless of pitch. The central finding is that the dominant mechanism for controlling voice intensity shifts from laryngeal (glottal resistance/adduction) at low pitch to respiratory (airflow rate) at high pitch/falsetto, with glottal resistance varying from 20-100 dyne-sec/cm^5 at low pitch to a near-constant 150 dyne-sec/cm^5 at falsetto. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker personality and effort modeling, this provides quantitative constraints for pitch-register-dependent intensity control: at low F0 the synthesizer should vary glottal closure parameters while keeping flow roughly constant, and at high F0 it should increase flow-related parameters while keeping glottal configuration fixed, with the power law I proportional to P^3.3 governing the overall SPL-to-effort mapping.


## Bjorklund_2016_SubglottalPressureSPL
Bjorklund and Sundberg (2016) establish normative regression data relating subglottal pressure (Ps) to sound pressure level (SPL) in 31 untrained healthy speakers, finding an average correlation of r = 0.83 and quantifying that a doubling of Ps produces 11.1 dB (female) or 9.3 dB (male) increase in SPL. The study demonstrates gender differences in phonatory efficiency: males produce ~2 dB higher SPL at equivalent pressure but show a shallower slope, and pitch does not significantly affect the Ps-SPL relationship. For speaker personality modeling, this provides the quantitative mapping from respiratory effort to acoustic output level, with gender-specific parameters that should be reflected in any physiologically-grounded voice synthesis system.

## Titze_1991_NeurologicAperiodicity
This paper develops a quantitative model of how stochastic motor neuron firing in thyroarytenoid muscle creates fundamental frequency perturbation (jitter) through twitch summation, predicting F0 perturbation magnitudes of 0.2-1.2% as a function of four parameters: number of motor units, mean firing rate, coefficient of variation of twitch amplitude, and coefficient of variation of interspike intervals. The key finding is that neurologic jitter accounts for a significant portion (0.2-0.3%) of total jitter measured in normal voices, with perturbation decreasing as motor unit count and firing rate increase, and increasing with variability in twitch amplitude and firing timing. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker personality system, this provides a physiologically grounded jitter model whose four parameters map directly to speaker characteristics (age, pathology, effort level), enabling more realistic F0 micro-perturbation than simple random noise, with quasi-periodic spectral components at motor unit firing frequencies (0-50 Hz) that contribute to natural voice quality.
