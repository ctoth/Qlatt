# Description

Ohala (1984) proposes the "frequency code" hypothesis: a biologically motivated principle explaining why high F0 universally conveys smallness, submission, and non-threat, while low F0 conveys largeness, dominance, and threat -- across human languages, cultures, and animal species. The paper unifies seven apparently disparate phenomena (question intonation, vocal affect expression, sound symbolism, cross-species vocalizations, facial expressions, and sexual dimorphism of the vocal tract) under a single ethological framework rooted in the relationship between body size and acoustic frequency. This foundational work provides the theoretical basis for understanding why F0 range and contour choices in speech synthesis carry specific attitudinal and pragmatic meanings beyond pure linguistic function.
