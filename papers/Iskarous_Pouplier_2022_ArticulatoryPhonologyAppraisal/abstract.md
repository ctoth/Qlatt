# Iskarous & Pouplier (2022) — Abstract

## Verbatim Abstract

Articulatory Phonology (AP) is a theoretical framework for studying the abstract units of speech and how they are organized both in the mind and in time. In this paper, we focus on recent developments in AP and on the associated computational model Task Dynamics (TD), as a way to review work within the theory in the 21st century. We organize the review around three broad topics. First, we discuss contrast dynamics: how articulatory targets and the dynamical parameters that determine how a target is reached vary to create different contrasts and contextual variants. Second, we discuss prosodic dynamics: how gestures are organized with respect to each other and how prosodic structure impacts the unfolding of gestures in time. Third, we discuss planning dynamics: how models of gestural planning and selection are combined with the TD model. Fourth, we raise a question about the completeness of the dynamical system underlying speech. We consider whether the theory, which currently focuses on modeling articulation, should expand its scope to include modeling the acoustic consequences of articulatory events.

## Interpretation for Qlatt

This review paper synthesizes the theoretical framework that underlies gestural models of speech production. While it does not provide new acoustic measurements or synthesis parameters directly, it establishes the mathematical and conceptual foundations for understanding:

1. **Why formant transitions have specific shapes** — they follow critically damped (or near-critically damped) dynamical approach curves, not linear interpolation
2. **How coarticulation emerges** — from overlapping gestures with weighted blending, not from ad-hoc smoothing rules
3. **Why phrase boundaries cause lengthening** — prosodic "time-warping" gestures slow the articulatory clock
4. **How syllable structure constrains timing** — coupled oscillators enforce phase relationships between onset consonants and vowels

The paper's explicit call for "Acoustic Phonology" — extending AP to model articulation-acoustics mappings — validates the importance of projects like Qlatt that bridge articulatory descriptions and acoustic synthesis parameters.
