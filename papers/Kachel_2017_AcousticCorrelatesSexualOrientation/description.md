This paper investigates acoustic correlates of sexual orientation and gender-role self-concept in German-speaking women (n=108 across two studies), analyzing median f0, vowel formant frequencies (F1/F2 of /a:/, /i:/, /u:/), vowel space dispersion, and vowel durations. The key finding is that no significant between-group differences exist between lesbians and straight women on any acoustic measure after Bonferroni correction. However, significant within-group variability was found: more exclusively lesbian speakers had lower median f0 (r=-0.59, study 1), and present gender-role self-concept (TMF scale) correlated with median f0 for lesbians (r=0.48, study 2) -- more feminine self-concept corresponded to higher f0. For speech synthesis, this paper's primary relevance is to speaker personality/voice preset design: it provides evidence that f0 varies meaningfully with gender-role self-concept independently of biological sex, suggesting that synthesizer voice presets aiming to convey different social personae should modulate f0 range rather than formant targets. All acoustic measures were reported in ERB scale (Moore and Glasberg 1983).
