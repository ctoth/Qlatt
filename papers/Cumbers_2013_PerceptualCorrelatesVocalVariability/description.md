This master's thesis investigates whether acoustic measures of vocal variability (pitch sigma and SFF range in semitones) correlate with listener perceptions of pitch variability during reading, using 30 speech samples rated by 30 trained listeners on a 7-point scale. The key finding is a strong positive correlation between pitch sigma and perceived variability (r = .927, p < .01) and between SFF range and perceived variability (r = .890, p < .01), with no significant gender differences in either acoustic measures or correlation strength, and normative data showing young adults average ~2.2 ST pitch sigma and ~11.7 ST SFF range during reading. For speech synthesis prosody systems, this validates pitch sigma as a reliable acoustic proxy for perceived expressiveness and establishes that F0 variability should be modeled in semitones (not Hz) to maintain perceptual equivalence across speaker base frequencies.
