This paper critiques traditional voice quality rating scales (GRBAS, CAPE-V) as theoretically unfounded and unreliable at the individual listener level, despite 2000 years of use. The authors identify four factors causing listener disagreement and demonstrate 96% agreement using synthesis-based method-of-adjustment tasks, proposing a psychoacoustic model where acoustic parameters are validated by their perceptual salience rather than correlations with subjective ratings. For speech synthesis, this work validates the parameterization approach while cautioning that commonly used measures like jitter/shimmer may have limited perceptual importance, and suggests that synthesis itself can serve as the validation tool for voice quality models.
