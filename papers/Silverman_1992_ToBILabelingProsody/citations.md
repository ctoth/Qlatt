## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **[3] Pierrehumbert & Hirschberg (1990)** - "The Meaning of Intonation Contours" -- Defines the pragmatic/semantic interpretation of pitch accent and boundary tone combinations. Essential for understanding what each ToBI label *means* communicatively, which informs how a TTS system should select prosodic patterns based on discourse context.

2. **[5] Price, Ostendorf, Shattuck-Hufnagel & Fong (1991)** - "The Use of Prosody in Syntactic Disambiguation" (JASA) -- Origin of the break indices concept. Important for understanding how break index levels correlate with acoustic-phonetic properties (duration, pause, F0 reset) that must be realized in synthesis.

3. **[4] Price, Wightman, Ostendorf & Bear (1990)** - Use of relative duration for disambiguation -- Relevant for understanding how durational patterns relate to prosodic structure, which affects the timing module of a TTS frontend.

4. **Pierrehumbert (1980)** - PhD thesis (not explicitly in reference list but heavily cited in text as foundational [3]) -- "The phonology and phonetics of English intonation" -- The theoretical foundation for the entire ToBI tonal tier. Defines the autosegmental-metrical framework with H and L tone targets.

5. **[6] Silverman, Blaauw, Spitz & Pitrelli (1992)** - Prosodic comparison of spontaneous vs read speech -- Relevant for understanding how prosodic patterns differ across speaking styles, which affects TTS naturalness.
