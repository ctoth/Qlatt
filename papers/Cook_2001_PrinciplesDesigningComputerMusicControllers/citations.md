## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **[5] Cook (1991), "Identification of Control Parameters in an Articulatory Vocal Tract Model, With Applications to the Synthesis of Singing," Stanford PhD.** The most directly relevant reference for this project: it is precisely the problem of reducing a large articulatory parameter set to a controllable one.
- **[6] Cook (1992), "SPASM," Computer Music Journal 17:1, pp 30-44.** The system whose "well over 40 continuously controlled parameters" grounds the paper's claim that the voice is hard to control; Figures 3 and 4 of the present paper come from it.
- **[7] Cook (1993), "New Control Strategies for the Singer Articulatory Voice Synthesis System," SMAC.** Explicitly about the few-to-many mapping problem for a voice model; the algorithmic detail absent from the NIME paper should be here.
- **[15] Cook and Leider (2000), "SqueezeVox: A New Controller for Vocal Synthesis Models," ICMC.** Full description of the breath/pitch/articulation control decomposition that the NIME paper only summarizes.
- **[4] Cook (1992), "A Meta-Wind-Instrument Physical Model, and a Meta-Controller for Real Time Performance Control," ICMC.** The HIRN/WhirlWind co-design case that supplies the paper's central negative result about control bandwidth.
