This paper analyzes the GEMEP corpus of 12 acted emotions systematically varying in arousal, valence, and potency/control to determine which acoustic parameters encode each emotional dimension, using PCA-derived composite scores in stepwise logistic regression. The key finding is that while arousal dominates vocal expression through F0 and intensity (R²=0.82), valence is specifically reflected in spectral slope and intensity variability (R²=0.22-0.49), and potency/control is reflected in F0 level, spectral noise (HNR), and low-frequency spectral dominance (R²=0.23-0.38), with these effects being arousal-dependent. For speech synthesis, this establishes that emotional voice presets must control spectral tilt and voice quality parameters (not just F0 and intensity) to convey valence and potency dimensions, and that emotion implementation should follow a hierarchical approach: set arousal level first, then adjust spectral parameters for the target valence and potency.
