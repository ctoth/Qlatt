Blumstein, Stevens, and Nigro use selective adaptation experiments to investigate how the auditory system processes burst and formant transition cues for stop consonant place of articulation. They find that full-cue stimuli (burst + transitions) produce stronger adaptation effects than partial-cue stimuli (transitions only), while conflicting-cue stimuli (mismatched burst and transitions) produce minimal adaptation, supporting an integrated property detector model. The results establish that burst and transition information are combined into a single integrated acoustic property at consonantal release, rather than being processed independently as simple cues.
