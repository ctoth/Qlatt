This paper tests whether the V-shaped relationship between valence and arousal, widely observed for visual stimuli, holds for human vocal bursts, using two studies with 1,008 nonlinguistic vocalizations rated by both a small deep-phenotyping cohort and a large online sample under core affect and perceived affective quality conditions. The key finding is that quadratic (V-shaped) models consistently outperform linear models in explaining the valence-arousal relationship for vocal bursts, and that stimulus ambiguity (high between-participant variability in valence) predicts violations of the V-shape and elevated arousal ratings. For speech synthesis, these findings constrain how emotional dimensions should be mapped to acoustic parameters in vocal burst generation: extreme positive and negative valence states both require high-arousal acoustic features, and the distinction between subjective experience and semantic attribution ratings should inform evaluation of synthetic emotional vocalizations.
