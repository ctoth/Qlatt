This paper systematically analyzes acoustic correlates of politeness in English requests, measuring F0 (mean, range, contour shape), duration, and voice quality (HNR) across three linguistic structures (direct, please-type, indirect requests). The key finding is that polite speech consistently exhibits higher pitch, wider pitch range, rising (or less falling) contours, faster speech rate, and clearer voice quality compared to rude speech, with effects manifesting both globally and on specific semantic constituents. For Qlatt, this provides concrete parameter targets for implementing prosodic variation based on pragmatic intent, directly informing how to modulate F0, duration, and potentially spectral tilt to convey speaker attitude.
