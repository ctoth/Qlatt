# Effects on Voice Fundamental Frequency and Satisfaction with Voice in Trans Men during Testosterone Treatment -- A Longitudinal Study

**Authors:** Ulrika Nygren, Agneta Nordenskjold, Stefan Arver, Maria Sodersten
**Year:** 2016
**Venue:** Journal of Voice, Vol. 30, No. 6, pp. 766.e23-766.e34
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jvoice.2015.10.016

## One-Sentence Summary
Provides longitudinal F0 tracking data across 50 trans men over 3-24 months of testosterone treatment, demonstrating continuous decline in fundamental frequency from female to male reference ranges with large interindividual variation.

## Problem Addressed
No prior longitudinal study had examined voice changes during testosterone treatment in a large group of trans men. Existing data came from case reports and small studies with insufficient follow-up to characterize the time course and extent of F0 change.

## Key Contributions
- First large-sample (N=50) longitudinal study of voice F0 changes during testosterone HRT
- Demonstrates continuous F0 decline over 12 months reaching male reference values
- Quantifies interindividual variation in voice masculinization rate and extent
- Shows 24% of participants developed voice problems requiring therapy
- Correlates voice satisfaction with F0 values (lower F0 = higher satisfaction)
- Notes that F0 does not correlate with androgen levels (suggesting AR-mediated saturation)

## Methodology
- **Participants:** 50 trans men, ages 18-64 (mean 27), diagnosed with transsexualism (ICD-10 F64.0)
- **Treatment regimens:** Intramuscular injection (n=29), transdermal (n=4), transdermal/IM switch (n=8), adolescent IM (n=9)
- **Measurement timepoints:** Before treatment, 3 months (2-5 mo), 6 months (5-10 mo), 12 months (11-15 mo), 18 months (16-21 mo), 24 months (22-36 mo)
- **Total recordings:** 211 out of a possible 300
- **Acoustic measures:** Speech Range Profile (SRP) and Voice Range Profile (VRP) recorded in sound-treated booth
- **Variables:** Mean F0 (MF0), F0-mode, Min F0, Max F0, SPL (Leq), Max SPL, area (ST x dB)
- **Software:** Soundswell and Phog (Neovius Data och Signalsystem AB, Sweden)
- **Statistics:** Linear mixed model with Bonferroni corrections; Spearman rank-order correlations

## Key Equations

No novel equations. Standard acoustic analysis using:
- $12 \times \ln(x/110) / \ln(2)$ -- conversion from Hz to semitones (reference 110 Hz)

## Implementation Details

### Time Course of F0 Change
- Significant effect of time: F(5,159) = 140, P < 0.001 for MF0; F(5,159) = 113, P < 0.001 for F0-mode
- MF0 decreased significantly in first 3 months AND between 3-6 months AND between 6-12 months
- No further significant lowering after 12 months
- For IM injection subgroup (n=29): significant F0 lowering occurred within first 6 months, no further change after 6 months
- Six participants still had MF0 between 143-170 Hz at latest recording (F0-mode 128-164 Hz)

### Reference Comparisons
- Vocally healthy Swedish females: MF0 = 188 Hz
- Vocally healthy Australian females: MF0 = 183 Hz
- Vocally healthy Swedish males: MF0 = 123 Hz
- Vocally healthy Australian males: MF0 = 110 Hz
- Trans men after 12 months: MF0 = 125 Hz (95% CI 119-132 Hz) -- congruent with male references

### Satisfaction Correlations
- MF0 vs "satisfied with voice": r_s = -0.68 (lower F0 = more satisfied)
- F0-mode vs "satisfied with voice": r_s = -0.70
- Min F0 vs "satisfied with voice": r_s = -0.66
- MF0 vs "perceived as male on phone": r_s = -0.65
- F0-mode vs "perceived as male on phone": r_s = -0.63
- Min F0 vs "perceived as male on phone": r_s = -0.52

### Voice Therapy
- 24% (12 of 50) received voice therapy
- Reasons: vocal fatigue (9/12), vocal instability (5/12), strained voice quality (5/12), voice too young (2/12), insufficient F0 lowering (6/12), hoarseness (6/12), difficulty projecting (5/12)

## Results Summary
- F0 decreased continuously from ~192 Hz to ~125 Hz over 12 months
- F0-mode decreased from ~178 Hz to ~117 Hz
- Min F0 decreased from ~130 Hz to ~79 Hz
- Max F0 decreased from ~941 Hz to ~746 Hz (loss of upper range)
- SPL did not change significantly
- Voice range area (ST x dB) did not change significantly
- F0 did NOT correlate with androgen levels -- suggests AR-mediated saturation mechanism
- Voice changes were not dose-dependent at 12 months
- VTL (vocal tract length) is noted as affecting formant frequencies and gender perception, but formants were not measured in this study

## Limitations
- Not all participants came to visits on schedule (211 of 300 possible recordings)
- Formant frequencies were not measured (noted by authors as important for gender perception via VTL)
- No laryngoscopic or imaging data of vocal fold changes
- Mixed treatment regimens (IM, transdermal, adolescent dosing)
- Swedish-language recordings (may differ from English)

## Testable Properties
- MF0 must decrease monotonically as a group mean across 0-12 month timepoints
- MF0 at 12 months should be in range 89-170 Hz (observed interindividual range)
- Group mean MF0 at 12 months: 125 Hz (95% CI 119-132 Hz)
- SPL (Leq) should remain stable (~70.5-70.8 dB) across treatment
- Min F0 at 12 months should be lower than Min F0 before treatment
- Max F0 at 12 months should be lower than Max F0 before treatment
- Satisfaction ratings should correlate negatively with F0 (r_s approx -0.65 to -0.70)

## Relevance to Project
This paper provides direct empirical evidence that vocal fold properties (mass, length, tension) vary continuously along a spectrum rather than falling into binary sex categories. The F0 trajectory data -- showing gradual, continuous lowering from female-typical (~192 Hz) through an androgynous zone to male-typical (~125 Hz) over 12 months -- demonstrates that any speaker personality model must parameterize vocal fold properties as continuous variables, not as a sex binary. The large interindividual variation (some participants plateau at 170 Hz, others reach 89 Hz) further supports the need for individual anatomical parameters rather than category-based defaults.

Key implication: the Qlatt speaker profile system should model F0 range, vocal fold mass, and vocal fold length as independent continuous parameters that can take any value in the observed human range, rather than switching between "male" and "female" presets.

The paper does NOT provide formant data, which would be needed to parameterize vocal tract length independently. The authors note that VTL affects formant frequencies and gender perception. For formant data during voice transition, see Hodges-Simeon et al. 2021 (Scientific Reports) which measured estimated VTL from formants.

## Open Questions
- [ ] What is the mechanism by which T affects vocal fold mass vs length? (authors note this is unknown)
- [ ] How does F0 change relate to vocal fold thickness changes measurable by laryngoscopy?
- [ ] Why do some participants not achieve male-typical F0 despite adequate T levels? (AR sensitivity, CAG repeat length?)
- [ ] How would formant frequencies change during the same treatment course?
