# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

A parametric model for the voice source is described which includes the acoustic interaction between the glottal source and the subglottal and supraglottal acoustic systems.

---

## Our Interpretation

The paper addresses the fundamental limitation of treating the voice source as independent from the vocal tract in speech production models. Rothenberg demonstrates that subglottal and supraglottal inertance at frequencies below the first formant causes the glottal airflow waveform to differ dramatically from the glottal area waveform, transforming a roughly symmetric triangular pulse into an asymmetric sawtooth with a sharp discontinuity at closure that generates higher frequency energy in voiced speech. For speech synthesis, this explains why the voice source must be modeled as interacting with the vocal tract rather than as a simple independent excitation, and provides the physical basis for parameters controlling spectral tilt, voice quality, and excitation strength.
