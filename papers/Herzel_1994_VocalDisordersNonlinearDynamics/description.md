Applies nonlinear dynamics analysis techniques (phase space embedding, bifurcation classification, attractor visualization) to sustained vowel recordings from 95 dysphonic patients, demonstrating that approximately 25% exhibit deterministic nonlinear phenomena including period-doubling, subharmonics, tori, and chaos. The paper provides a unified terminology mapping clinical voice disorder descriptions (diplophonia, biphonation, vocal fry, creaky voice) to attractor types from dynamical systems theory, and identifies four desynchronization mechanisms (left-right vocal fold, horizontal-vertical modes, ventricular fold interaction, acoustic coupling) as likely physiological origins. Directly relevant to voice quality modeling in a formant synthesizer, as it provides a taxonomy of roughness/disorder types with distinct acoustic signatures (amplitude modulation frequencies, subharmonic patterns) that can guide parameter modulation strategies for simulating pathological and non-modal voice qualities.
