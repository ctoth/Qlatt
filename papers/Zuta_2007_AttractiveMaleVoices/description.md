This ICPhS 2007 paper investigates which acoustic parameters of German male voices correlate with female attractiveness judgments, analyzing F0, formant frequencies, LTAS, pause structure, and hesitation patterns across six speakers divided into "more attractive" and "less attractive" groups. The key findings are that F0 mean alone does not predict attractiveness (disproving the "deep voices are attractive" assumption), but F0 modulation (σ = 24 Hz attractive vs 18 Hz unattractive), absence of nasality (no LTAS dip at 2.8 kHz), fewer/shorter hesitations, and moderate pause structure all correlate with higher attractiveness ratings. For speech synthesis, this provides empirical targets for prosody rule parameters (F0 variation ranges), voice quality checks (nasality detection via LTAS), and the concept that voices closest to a neutral "0-Voice" standard are perceived as most attractive.
