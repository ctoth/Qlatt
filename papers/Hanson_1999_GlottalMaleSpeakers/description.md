This paper presents acoustic measurements of glottal source characteristics for 21 male speakers, quantifying five parameters (H1*-H2*, H1*-A1, H1*-A3*, aspiration noise rating, and first-formant bandwidth) across three nonhigh vowels and comparing them with predictions from theoretical models of posterior glottal chink effects. The key finding is that spectral tilt (H1*-A3*) shows the largest gender difference at approximately 9.6 dB lower for males than females, with males clustering more tightly around their means, while open quotient (H1*-H2*) and first-formant prominence (H1*-A1) each differ by about 3 dB between genders. These quantitative voice-source parameter ranges and gender-differentiating acoustic correlates are directly applicable to setting voice quality parameters in the Klatt synthesizer for natural-sounding male and female voice presets, and the Table II data linking glottal chink area to bandwidth and spectral tilt provides concrete targets for LF source model parameterization.
