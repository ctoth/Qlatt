# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

It is argued that reliable acoustic profiles of speech under stress can only be found if different types of stress are clearly distinguished and experimentally induced. We report first results of a study with 100 speakers from three language groups, using a computer-based induction procedure that allows distinguishing cognitive load due to task engagement from psychological stress. Findings show significant effects of load, and partly of stress, for speech rate, energy contour, F0, and spectral parameters. It is further suggested that for the complete sample of speakers the mean results do not reflect the amplitude of stress effects on the voice. Future research should isolate and focus on speakers for whom the psychological stress induction has been successful.

---

## Our Interpretation

This study separates the vocal effects of cognitive load (increased speech rate, steeper energy contours) from psychological stress (raised F0, upward spectral shift) using 100 speakers and a dual-task paradigm. The critical finding is that individual differences are enormous -- only about half the speakers show the predicted stress effects -- meaning population-level averages underestimate the real effect size for responsive speakers. For Qlatt voice presets, this means load and stress require different parameter adjustments and should be treated as separate modification dimensions.
