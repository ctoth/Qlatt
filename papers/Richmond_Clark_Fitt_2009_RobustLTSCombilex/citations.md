## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **[3] Fitt, "Unisyn multi-accent lexicon"** — The companion multi-accent lexicon project (see [[Fitt_Isard_1999_SynthesisRegionalEnglishKeywordLexicon]]), explicitly used as the comparison point for Combilex's own accent-independence design.
- **[4] Fitt & Richmond (2006), "Redundancy and productivity in the speech technology lexicon"** — Referenced directly (p.1) as the fuller discussion of Combilex's advanced design features, only briefly summarized in this paper.
- **[11] Black, Lenzo & Pagel (1998), "Issues in building general letter to sound rules"** — Source of the automatic epsilon-scattering alignment method used for all baseline (B1-B4) models in this paper's evaluation; essential for replicating the baseline comparison.
- **[6] Pagel, Lenzo & Black (1998), "Letter-to-sound rules for accented lexicon compression"** — Source of the OALD comparison figures (78.13% words correct / 93.97% phones correct) used throughout Tables 3-4.
- **[12] Bisani & Ney (2008), "Joint-sequence models for grapheme-to-phoneme conversion"** — Named as a stronger candidate LTS method the authors want to test with Combilex's expert alignment in future work.
