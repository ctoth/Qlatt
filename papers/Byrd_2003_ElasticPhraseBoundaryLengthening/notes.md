# The Elastic Phrase: Modeling the Dynamics of Boundary-Adjacent Lengthening

**Authors:** Dani Byrd, Elliot Saltzman
**Year:** 2003
**Venue:** Journal of Phonetics, 31, 149-180
**DOI:** 10.1016/S0095-4470(02)00085-2

## One-Sentence Summary

This paper proposes that phrase boundary lengthening is generated by prosodic gestures (pi-gestures) that locally slow a central clock controlling the temporal unfolding of an utterance, providing a unified dynamical account of duration increase, reduced gestural overlap, and spatial magnitude changes near boundaries.

## Problem Addressed

Existing models of boundary-adjacent lengthening either used ad-hoc duration multipliers with no principled mechanism, or used stiffness modulation which only affected duration without capturing the full range of observed boundary phenomena (reduced overlap, spatial strengthening). The paper seeks a single dynamical mechanism that can account for all temporal and spatial consequences of prosodic boundaries.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the concept of **pi-gestures** (prosodic gestures): abstract gestures on a prosodic tier that modulate the local "clock rate" of an utterance near phrase boundaries
- Demonstrates that clock-slowing (time-warping) via pi-gestures captures boundary lengthening, reduced intergestural overlap, and spatial magnitude changes under a single mechanism
- Shows that stiffness modulation alone is inadequate -- it only affects duration without changing intergestural timing
- Provides a computational simulation framework comparing stiffness modulation vs. clock-slowing approaches
- Proposes that boundary strength differences (intonational phrase vs. intermediate phrase) correspond to differences in pi-gesture activation level, not type

## Methodology

Computational simulations of two overlapping articulatory gestures (modeled as damped mass-spring systems) spanning a phrase boundary, comparing three approaches:
1. No pi-gesture (baseline)
2. Stiffness-modulating pi-gesture (reduces gestural stiffness proportionally)
3. Clock-slowing pi-gesture (warps local time proportionally)

## Key Equations

### Equation 1: Tract-variable dynamics

$$\ddot{x} = \ddot{x}_c + \ddot{x}_d$$

where:

$$\ddot{x}_c = -a_c(t) b_c \dot{x} - a_c(t) k_c [x - x_{o,c}] \tag{1b}$$

$$\ddot{x}_d = (1 - a_c(t))[-b_d \dot{x} - k_d(x - x_{o,d})] \tag{1c}$$

Where:
- $x$, $\dot{x}$, $\ddot{x}$: tract variable position, velocity, acceleration
- $a_c(t)$: activation of constriction gesture (0 to 1)
- $b_c$, $k_c$: damping and stiffness of constriction gesture
- $x_{o,c}$: target position of constriction gesture
- $b_d$, $k_d$: damping and stiffness of neutral (default) gesture
- $x_{o,d}$: target position of neutral gesture

### Equation 2: Activation function (half-cosine rise/fall)

$$a(t) = \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if } t < t_{on,1} \\ 0.5\{1 - \cos(\omega_r[t - t_{on,1}])\}, & \text{if } t_{on,1} \leq t < t_{on,2} \\ 1, & \text{if } t_{on,2} \leq t < t_{off,1} \\ 0.5\{1 + \cos(\omega_f[t - t_{off,1}])\}, & \text{if } t_{off,1} \leq t < t_{off,2} \\ 0, & \text{if } t \geq t_{off,2} \end{cases}$$

Where: $\omega_r$ and $\omega_f$ control rise/fall times; $T_R$ = rise duration, $T_H$ = hold duration, $T_F$ = fall duration.

### Equation 3: Stiffness modulation

$$k^* = (1 - \alpha \cdot a_\pi) \cdot k \tag{3}$$

Where:
- $\alpha$: boundary or prosodic strength (0 to 1)
- $a_\pi$: activation of the pi-gesture (0 to 1)
- $k$: underlying stiffness value
- $k^*$: effective (modulated) stiffness

### Equation 4: Clock-slowing (the core model)

$$\dot{\tau} = \frac{d}{dt}\tau = (1 - \alpha a_\pi) \tag{4}$$

Where:
- $t$: unscaled "standard" time (rate of change = 1.0)
- $\tau$: scaled clock time
- $\dot{\tau}$: rate of change of clock time
- $\alpha$: prosodic boundary strength (0 to 1)
- $a_\pi$: pi-gesture activation (0 to 1)

When the pi-gesture is inactive ($a_\pi = 0$), $\dot{\tau} = 1$ (normal speed). When active, clock slows in proportion to $\alpha \cdot a_\pi$. At maximum ($\alpha = 1, a_\pi = 1$), clock stops entirely.

## Implementation Details

### Simulation setup
- Two constriction gestures on different or same tract variables
- Neutral (default) gestures return articulators to rest when no active constriction
- Pi-gesture defined on a prosodic tier, separate from constriction tiers
- Pi-gesture centered at the phrase boundary, symmetric rise/fall (half-cosine shape)
- Activation trajectories are half-cosine (Eq. 2), NOT step functions
- Multiplicative gating: effective stiffness = $a_c(t) \cdot k_c$ (activation gates stiffness)

### Clock-slowing mechanism
1. All gestural activations $a(\tau)$ become functions of scaled time $\tau$, not standard time $t$
2. The pi-gesture modulates the rate at which $\tau$ evolves (Eq. 4)
3. As the boundary is approached and $a_\pi$ rises, $\tau$ slows relative to $t$
4. This stretches ALL concurrent activation trajectories (constriction AND neutral gestures)
5. Gestures closer to the boundary are stretched more than remote ones

### Key distinction: stiffness modulation vs. clock slowing
- **Stiffness modulation**: only affects intragestural duration; does NOT change intergestural timing or overlap
- **Clock slowing**: affects both intragestural AND intergestural timing; produces duration increase, reduced overlap, and spatial magnitude changes simultaneously

### Practical consequences for duration modeling
- Clock slowing is equivalent to locally warping the time axis near a boundary
- The slowing is **gradient**, not uniform: maximum at the boundary, decaying away from it
- Final consonants lengthen more than preceding vowels (consistent with empirical data)
- Both phrase-final and phrase-initial gestures are affected

|---------|----------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Lowering duration (s) | 0.0730 | 0.0920 | 0.0816 |

| Kinematic overlap (C2 in C1) | 0.9452 | 0.9675 | 0.8946 |
| Kinematic overlap (C1 in C2) | 1.0952 | 1.3515 | 0.9865 |

Clock slowing **reduces** overlap (0.89 vs baseline 0.95), matching empirical findings. Stiffness modulation **increases** overlap (0.97), contrary to data.

Spatial magnitude of the closing gesture increases ("initial strengthening" effect), naturally arising from reduced overlap and longer gestural duration.

## Limitations

- Simulations use only two gestures; real utterances have many overlapping gestures
- Pi-gesture shape is assumed symmetric (half-cosine); asymmetric shapes not fully explored
- Coordination of pi-gestures with intonational gestures not addressed
- Pausing behavior not modeled
- The model is articulatory, not acoustic; mapping to acoustic duration multipliers is indirect
- Whether boundary strength is gradient or categorical remains an open question

## Testable Properties

- Lengthening must be greatest at the boundary edge and decrease with distance from it
- Clock slowing must affect both phrase-final and phrase-initial gestures
- Stronger boundaries (higher alpha) must produce more lengthening
- Different boundary strengths produce differences in degree, not type, of effect
- Clock slowing must reduce intergestural overlap (unlike stiffness modulation which increases it)
- Duration increase in phrase-final position should be greater than phrase-initial (asymmetric effect)
- Final consonants within the phrase-final syllable should lengthen more than preceding vowels

## Relevance to Project

The pi-gesture clock-slowing model provides a principled theoretical foundation for Qlatt's boundary lengthening rules. Rather than applying ad-hoc duration multipliers, the model suggests that boundary lengthening should be modeled as a local time-warping effect where:
1. The amount of slowing is controlled by boundary strength (corresponding to prosodic hierarchy level)
2. Slowing is maximal at the boundary and decays with distance
3. Both pre-boundary and post-boundary segments are affected (asymmetrically)

For Qlatt's duration.yaml rules, this means:
- Duration multipliers should be largest for segments immediately adjacent to the boundary
- The multiplier should decay for more remote segments
- Phrase-final lengthening should be greater than phrase-initial
- The same mechanism should apply to all boundary types (IP, ip, word) with different strength values
