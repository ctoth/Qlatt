# Abstract

A two-mass model of vocal-fold vibrations is analyzed with methods from nonlinear dynamics. Bifurcations are located in parameter planes of physiological interest (subglottal pressure, stiffness of the folds). It is shown that a sufficiently large tension imbalance of the left and right vocal fold induces bifurcations to subharmonic regimes, toroidal oscillations, and chaos. The corresponding attractors are characterized by phase portraits, spectra, and next-maximum maps. The relevance of these simulations for voice disorders such as laryngeal paralysis is discussed.

PACS numbers: 43.70.Aj, 43.70.Dn, 43.70.Gr, 05.45.+b

## Interpretation

This paper bridges nonlinear dynamics theory with clinical voice pathology. The key insight for synthesis is that irregular voice quality (jitter, shimmer, subharmonics, chaos) emerges naturally from the mechanical asymmetry of vocal fold tension — not from stochastic noise injection. The two-parameter bifurcation diagrams (Q vs P_s) provide a principled map for controlling voice quality transitions in a synthesizer: small Q deviations from 1.0 produce natural-sounding jitter, moderate deviations produce diplophonia/period doubling, and large deviations produce chaotic vibration. The critical subglottal pressure formula (Eq. 24) links phonation threshold to mass, stiffness, and glottal geometry parameters.
