## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Low, E.L., Grabe, E. & Nolan, F. (2000), "Quantitative characterisations of speech rhythm: 'syllable-timing' in Singapore English," Language and Speech** - the direct predecessor whose method (nPVI applied to vowel durations, British/Singapore English comparison) this paper extends to 18 languages and generalizes with the new raw rPVI. Not yet independently retrieved into this collection (paywalled, no open-access copy found).
- **Ramus, F., Nespor, M. & Mehler, J. (1999), "Correlates of linguistic rhythm in the speech signal," Cognition 72** - the competing %V/ΔC/ΔV rhythm-metric family, directly compared against this paper's PVI measures in Table 3 and Figures 3-4.
- **Dauer, R. (1983), "Stress-timing and syllable-timing re-analysed," Journal of Phonetics 11** - source of the continuous "languages are more or less stress-based" reanalysis this paper's own conclusions echo, and of the empirical failure of classical interstress-interval isochrony.
- **Deterding, D. (1994), "The rhythm of Singapore English"** - source of the local-pair normalization procedure this paper adopts for nPVI (over a global-average alternative), and an independent spontaneous-speech replication of the British/Singapore English durational-variability contrast.
- **Wenk, B. & Wioland, F. (1982), "Is French really syllable-timed?" Journal of Phonetics 10** - together with Brakel (1985), the source (cited in endnote 1) of the specific mechanistic claim that vowel reduction is what produces near-isochronous foot durations in stress-timed languages, the paper's clearest statement on vowel reduction as the rhythm-class driver.
