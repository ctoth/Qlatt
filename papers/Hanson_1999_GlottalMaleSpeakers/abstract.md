# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

Acoustic measurements believed to reflect glottal characteristics were made on recordings collected from 21 male speakers. The waveforms and spectra of three nonhigh vowels (/ae, ^, epsilon/) were analyzed to obtain acoustic parameters related to first-formant bandwidth, open quotient, spectral tilt, and aspiration noise. Comparisons were made with previous results obtained for 22 female speakers [H. M. Hanson, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. **101**, 466-481 (1997)]. While there is considerable overlap across gender, the male data show lower average values and less interspeaker variation for all measures. In particular, the amplitude of the first harmonic relative to that of the third formant is 9.6 dB lower for the male speakers than for the female speakers, suggesting that spectral tilt is an especially significant parameter for differentiating male and female speech. These findings are consistent with fiberscopic studies which have shown that males tend to have a more complete glottal closure, leading to less energy loss at the glottis and less spectral tilt. Observations of the speech waveforms and spectra suggest the presence of a second glottal excitation within a glottal period for some of the male speakers. Possible causes and acoustic consequences of these second excitations are discussed.

---

## Our Interpretation

This paper quantifies how male and female voices differ at the glottal source level, using five acoustic measures on 21 male speakers compared to 22 females from a prior study. The main finding is that spectral tilt (measured as H1*-A3*) is the single strongest differentiator between male and female voices, with males showing nearly 10 dB less tilt due to more complete glottal closure. For our synthesizer, this means spectral tilt control is the most important parameter to get right when switching between male and female voice presets, and the paper provides concrete numerical targets for each acoustic measure.
