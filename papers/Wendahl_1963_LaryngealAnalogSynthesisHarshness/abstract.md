# Abstract

An electrical laryngeal analog (LADIC) was used to generate stimuli which varied in the magnitude of frequency differences between successive cycles. For each of two median fundamental frequencies, a 100 and a 200 cps condition, stimuli were generated to have frequency variations on successive cycles of +/-10, 8, 6, 4, 2, and 1 cps. 535 listeners judged the stimuli on the basis of which sounded the most rough. The results show that even very slight frequency variations, as little as +/-1 cps around a median fundamental frequency of 100 cps, sounded rough. The data also indicate that the magnitude of the roughness judgement was directly related to the frequency differences between successive cycles. The preceding statement held true for a given median frequency value. Thus, for the two median frequency conditions used in this study, a 100 cps and a 200 cps median frequency, the degree of perceived roughness was directly related to the frequency differences between successive cycles for a single condition; the 200 cycle condition, however, was always judged to be less rough when the same frequency variations were programmed.

## Interpretation

This paper provides the first systematic, synthesizer-based evidence that cycle-to-cycle F0 perturbation (jitter) is the primary acoustic parameter driving roughness/harshness perception. The use of synthesized stimuli (rather than natural voice recordings) isolates jitter from confounding factors like shimmer, noise, or spectral irregularity. The finding that identical absolute jitter produces less roughness at higher F0 implies that jitter should be parameterized as a proportion of the fundamental period rather than in absolute Hz. The Thurstone scale values provide a quantitative roughness function that could calibrate jitter parameters in a formant synthesizer's voice quality model.
