## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **Nolan 1983** — Used to interpret the 2.8 kHz LTAS dip as nasality. The nasality-attractiveness link is one of the paper's strongest findings; Nolan's speaker recognition work provides the spectral basis.
- **Hollien 1971** — Source of the "0-Voice" concept (neutral baseline voice). Relevant for defining default/neutral voice presets in synthesis.
- **Bruckert et al. 2006** — Larger study on female assessment of male voice parameters; found pleasantness based on intonation and preference for rising pitch. Directly related to voice attractiveness research.
- **Ní Chasaide & Gobl 1997** — Voice source variation handbook chapter; already partially in collection via Gobl_2003. Relevant for voice quality parameter space.
