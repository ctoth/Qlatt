# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

This paper will present observations on the design, artistic, and human factors of creating digital music controllers. Specific projects will be presented, and a set of design principles will be supported from those examples.

**Keywords:** Musical control, artistic interfaces.

---

## Our Interpretation

Cook distills fifteen years of building musical controllers into thirteen numbered design principles covering human/artistic factors, technology choices, and the mutual generation of algorithms and controllers. Each principle is argued from named projects, including two explicit failures: a stateful loop-triggering scheme for the Cook/Morrill trumpet, and the HIRN "super instrument" built with high sensor bandwidth but no target composition. For a formant/source-filter speech synthesizer the paper is directly relevant through SPASM, whose "well over 40 continuously controlled parameters" made the voice "a truly difficult instrument to control," and SqueezeVox, which maps breath to bellows, pitch to keyboard and linear strip, and vowels/consonants to presets or a continuous pad.
