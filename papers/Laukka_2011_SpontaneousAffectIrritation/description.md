This paper analyzes the acoustic correlates of authentic irritation and resignation in spontaneous call-center speech, using 200 utterances from 64 speakers with within-speaker comparisons against neutral baselines across 73 acoustic measures covering F0, intensity, formants, voice source, and duration. Key findings show that irritation is marked by higher F0, higher intensity (especially IntQ5), wider F2 bandwidth, and creakier voice quality (lower H1MA3), while resignation shows lower F0, reduced F0 variability, lower intensity, and both states exhibit slower speech rate — with all effects going in the same direction as posed anger/sadness but with substantially smaller magnitudes. For speech synthesis, this work provides realistic (non-exaggerated) acoustic targets for mild affective states that map directly to Klatt parameters, with the critical insight that intensity cues outweigh pitch cues for distinguishing spontaneous affect from neutral speech.
