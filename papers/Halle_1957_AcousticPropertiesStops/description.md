Foundational study analyzing the two major acoustic cues for stop consonant identification: burst spectra and formant transitions in adjacent vowels. Halle, Hughes, and Radley measured energy density spectra of stop bursts from multiple speakers and established a three-class spectral classification (acute vs grave, with further subdivision) that correctly identifies place of articulation in 95% of cases using intensity in the 700-10,000 and 2700-10,000 cps bands. The paper also documents formant transition patterns for all six English stops across multiple vowel contexts and reports perceptual tests showing that natural speech transitions and bursts are each independently sufficient cues for stop identification.
