This paper presents a parametric model of the voice source that incorporates acoustic interaction between the glottal source and the subglottal/supraglottal vocal tract systems, introducing the normalized vocal tract inertance parameter that explains how symmetric glottal area pulses transform into asymmetric sawtooth airflow waveforms with sharp slope discontinuities at closure. The key contributions include identifying the critical inertance range (0.2-1.0) where this transition occurs, a complete glottal conductance parameterization (T0, Ay, S1, S2, B1, B2, B3) with significance ordering, and demonstrating that f1/f0 interaction affects all formant amplitudes rather than just f1. This work is foundational for voice source modeling in formant synthesizers like Klatt, providing the physical mechanism underlying spectral tilt and excitation strength that later models (LF, CALM) formalize, and directly informing our aerodynamic model and voice quality control parameters.
