# Turk & Shattuck-Hufnagel 2000 — Abstract

## Verbatim Abstract

In this paper, we examine the effects of word boundaries on the durations of neighboring segments in English. Results of an experiment in which segmental content is held constant across word-boundary conditions suggest that there is no need to posit an independent word-level, domain-final (or word-final) lengthening mechanism. Instead, three other proposed duration-adjustment mechanisms - word-initial lengthening, polysyllabic shortening, and a tendency toward syllable-ratio equalization within a word - can account for the observed patterns. The data also show that word boundaries adjacent to function words have a reduced effect on neighboring segment durations, compared to boundaries between content words; this difference is consistent with an account in which function words are grouped with a neighboring content word to form a single prosodic word.

## Interpretation

This paper directly challenges the common assumption in TTS duration models that word-final segments should be lengthened. The controlled experimental design — using triads where the same phoneme sequence spans different word boundary configurations — provides strong evidence that what looks like word-final lengthening is actually the combined effect of word-initial lengthening on the following word, polysyllabic shortening differences between words of different lengths, and vowel nucleus equalization within words. For Qlatt's duration rules, this means: (1) do not implement word-final lengthening as a duration rule, (2) do implement word-initial lengthening on onset consonants and following nuclei, (3) polysyllabic shortening should be symmetric across all syllables in a word, and (4) function word boundaries should receive attenuated lengthening effects compared to content word boundaries. Note that phrase-final and utterance-final lengthening (driven by prosodic phrase boundaries) are separate phenomena not addressed here.
