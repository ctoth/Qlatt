## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Delattre, Liberman, and Cooper (1955)** - "Acoustic Loci and Transitional Cues for Consonants" JASA 27, 769 - The original "locus hypothesis" paper that this study tests and modifies. Essential companion for understanding formant transition targets.
2. **Stevens, Kasowski, and Fant (1953)** - "An Electrical Analog of the Vocal Tract" JASA 25, 734 - Describes the 35-section vocal tract analog instrument used in this study and the Stevens & House (1955) vowel study.
3. **Peterson and Barney (1952)** - Canonical vowel formant frequency data (already in our collection) used to map articulatory configurations to vowel regions.
4. **House and Stevens (1955)** - JASA 27, 882 - Vowel production study using the same analog, provides the r0=0.4 cm baseline for vowel configurations.
5. **Liberman et al. (1954)** - Psychol. Monogr. 68 - Perceptual experiments with painted spectrograms showing formant transitions as consonant cues. Foundation for the locus hypothesis.
