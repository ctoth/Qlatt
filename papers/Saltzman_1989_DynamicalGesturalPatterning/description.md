This paper presents the task-dynamic model of speech production, where speech gestures are formalized as critically damped point attractors in tract-variable space that are temporally overlapped according to a gestural score, with coarticulation emerging naturally from the blending of context-independent gestural primitives. The mathematical framework includes parameter blending equations for simultaneous gestures, a gated Jacobian pseudoinverse for mapping tract-variable dynamics to articulator movements, and a neutral attractor that returns articulators to rest when undriven. For speech synthesis, this model provides a principled alternative to ad-hoc coarticulation rules by generating smooth, context-dependent parameter trajectories from fixed phonological targets, with the gestural score concept mapping naturally to Klatt track structures and the blending equations informing formant transition interpolation strategies.
