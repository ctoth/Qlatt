Fowler (1980) critiques the class of "extrinsic timing" theories of speech production, which treat phonological segments as timeless feature bundles executed by a separate articulatory mechanism, arguing they cannot explain observed coarticulation patterns. She proposes an alternative "intrinsic timing" framework in which time is an inherent dimension of phonological segments, realized through coordinative structures (functional muscle organizations) that naturally produce overlapping, coproduced gestures. The paper introduces the concept of vowels and consonants as products of distinct but nested coordinative structures, with coarticulation emerging as a natural consequence of coproduction rather than feature spreading.
