This meta-analysis synthesizes 41 studies examining nine paraverbal speech behaviors (pitch, speech rate, message duration, pauses, response latency, speech errors, repetitions) associated with deception, finding that only pitch (r=.101) and response latency (r=.106) reliably increase during lying in weighted analyses, with all effect sizes being very small. The key contribution is demonstrating that moderator variables — particularly motivation, preparation time, and lie sanctioning — dramatically affect which paraverbal cues emerge, with high-motivation unprepared lies producing the strongest pitch elevation (r=.529). For speech synthesis, the findings provide quantitative bounds on how much pitch, rate, and timing parameters shift under psychological stress or deception, though the effect sizes are far smaller than those observed for emotional speech, making this primarily relevant as background for voice quality modeling rather than direct synthesis implementation.
