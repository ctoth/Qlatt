Hertz and Huffman present a nucleus-based timing model for rule-based formant speech synthesis, where the "acoustic nucleus" (the voiced core of a syllable including sonorants and voiced transitions) serves as the fundamental unit of duration assignment rather than individual segments. The model enables efficient development of multi-dialect synthesis rules by separating timing into language-universal, dialect-universal, and dialect-specific components, with duration distributed among phones within the nucleus via a trading relationship. The paper demonstrates the approach using the Delta System for General American English and four other American English dialects, achieving above 80% intelligibility on Modified Rhyme Tests.
