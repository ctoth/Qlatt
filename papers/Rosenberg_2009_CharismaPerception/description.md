Parallel experiments on charisma perception from spoken and transcribed American political speech (2004 Democratic primary candidates). Finds acoustic-prosodic correlates of charisma: higher mean F0 (r=0.252), greater F0 variability, faster speaking rate, fewer disfluencies, more H* pitch accents, fewer rising boundaries. Compares speech vs. text judgments to separate "what is said" from "how it is said." Establishes that charisma correlated with enthusiasm, charm, persuasiveness, and convincingness.
