This book presents the definitive exposition of the Autosegmental-Metrical (AM) theory of intonation, demonstrating that pitch patterns in speech derive from sequences of categorical H and L tonal targets associated with metrically prominent syllables and prosodic boundaries, with comprehensive cross-linguistic evidence from approximately 30 languages. Key contributions include the complete MAE_ToBI pitch accent and boundary tone inventory with phonetic realization rules, a three-layer pitch scaling model (intrinsic tonal identity, extrinsic paralinguistic modulation, metrical downstep/declination), the segmental anchoring hypothesis for tonal alignment, and the relationship between focus, metrical structure, and accent placement. This work provides the theoretical foundation for implementing F0 generation in TTS systems, directly complementing Pierrehumbert (1980) and the ToBI framework with explicit parameters (downstep ratio k~0.6, pitch range normalization formula, calling contour interval ~3 semitones) and rules for accent placement, phrasing, and contour interpolation.
