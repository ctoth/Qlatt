Gay (1978) investigates how speaking rate affects vowel formant frequencies and CV transition movements in CVC and CVCVC utterances produced by four speakers at slow and fast rates. The key finding is that midpoint formant frequencies (acoustic vowel targets) remain invariant across speaking rates; instead, the CV transition onset frequency shifts closer to the target for fast speech while transition velocity stays constant, indicating articulatory movement simply begins earlier. The paper also demonstrates that speaking rate and lexical stress are controlled by two independent mechanisms: rate primarily compresses duration, while destressing reduces amplitude, F0, and vowel color even at matched durations.
