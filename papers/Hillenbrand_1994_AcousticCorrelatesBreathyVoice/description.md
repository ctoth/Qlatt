Hillenbrand, Cleveland, & Erickson (1994) evaluate six acoustic measures as predictors of perceived breathiness in sustained vowels produced by 15 normal talkers at three breathiness levels. They introduce Cepstral Peak Prominence (CPP) and autocorrelation-based RPK as automated periodicity measures, finding these account for ~80% of variance in breathiness ratings, far exceeding H1 amplitude (r=0.66) and spectral tilt measures (r~0.41-0.51). The study confirms Klatt & Klatt's (1990) conclusion that aspiration noise is the primary acoustic correlate of breathiness, with H1 amplitude playing a secondary role.
