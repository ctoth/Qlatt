# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

The purpose of the present study was to determine the contributions of fundamental frequency (*f*<sub>0</sub>) and formants in cuing the distinction between men's and women's voices. A source-filter synthesizer was used to create four versions of 25 sentences spoken by men: (1) unmodified synthesis, (2) *f*<sub>0</sub> only shifted up toward values typical of women, (3) formants only shifted up toward values typical of women, and (4) both *f*<sub>0</sub> and formants shifted up. Identical methods were used to generate four corresponding versions of 25 sentences spoken by women, but with downward shifts. Listening tests showed that (1) shifting both *f*<sub>0</sub> and formants was *usually* effective (~82%) in changing the perceived sex of the utterance, and (2) shifting either *f*<sub>0</sub> or formants alone was usually *ineffective* in changing the perceived sex. Both *f*<sub>0</sub> and formants are apparently needed to specify speaker sex, though even together these cues are not entirely effective. Results also suggested that *f*<sub>0</sub> is somewhat more important than formants. A second experiment used the same methods, but isolated /hVd/ syllables were used as test signals. Results were broadly similar, with the important exception that, on average, the syllables were more likely to shift perceived talker sex with shifts in *f*<sub>0</sub> and/or formants.

*(p.1150)*

## Our Interpretation

The problem is that decades of work had established both f0 and formant frequencies as speaker-sex cues without settling their relative weight, and every prior synthesis study rested on one to four original talkers. Using 25 talkers per sex and a spectral-envelope resynthesizer that scales f0 and the whole envelope independently, the authors find that even shifting both cues together leaves ~18% of utterances sounding like the original sex, and that the two cues combine far under-additively, which pins a hard ceiling on frequency scaling as a voice-conversion method. For a source-filter synthesizer project this supplies the exact male-to-female scale factors (1.7041 for f0, 1.168 for formants), a validated resynthesis architecture with a published 96.9% intelligibility benchmark, and a strong argument that convincing gendered voices need the Klatt-family voice-quality parameters (aspiration noise, spectral tilt, F1 bandwidth) and not just frequency scaling.
