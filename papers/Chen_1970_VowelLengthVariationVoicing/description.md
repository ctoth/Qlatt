Cross-linguistic study of vowel duration variation as a function of the voicing of the following consonant, examining English, French, Russian, and Korean. Establishes that vowels are universally longer before voiced consonants than voiceless consonants, with the magnitude being language-specific (English ratio ~0.61, French ~0.87). Evaluates multiple articulatory theories and proposes that differential closure transition speed is the primary mechanism, with English showing an enlarged effect due to the perceptual/phonological function of vowel length.
