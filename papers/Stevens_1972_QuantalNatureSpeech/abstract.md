# Abstract

When a parameter specifying the configuration or state of an articulatory structure is manipulated through a range of values, some acoustic parameter describing the resulting sound often changes in a non-monotonic fashion. In particular, there appear to be ranges of the articulatory parameter for which there is very little change in the acoustic parameter and other ranges where the acoustic parameter is maximally sensitive to changes in the articulatory parameter. These relations are quantal in the sense that the acoustic pattern shows a change from one state to another as the articulatory parameter is varied through a range of values. Similar kinds of relations are observed between parameters that give a measure of the auditory response to a sound and certain acoustic or articulatory parameters that describe acoustic dimensions of the sound. In this paper a number of examples of such relations are examined, and their implications for speech production and speech perception are discussed. It is suggested that this tendency for quantal relations among acoustic, auditory, and articulatory parameters is a principal factor shaping the inventory of acoustic and articulatory attributes that are used to signal distinctions in language.

## Interpretation

This paper establishes the theoretical foundation for understanding why phonological categories exist as discrete units rather than continuous dimensions. For a Klatt formant synthesizer, the key insight is that parameter targets for vowels and consonants should be placed in the acoustically stable (plateau) regions identified by the coupled-resonator analysis. The theory also explains why certain formant transitions (the quantal-jump regions) must be precisely controlled while steady-state targets tolerate imprecision. The noise source analysis provides a physical basis for the abrupt onset of frication parameters (AF), and the voicing analysis justifies the relatively step-like behavior of the AV parameter.
