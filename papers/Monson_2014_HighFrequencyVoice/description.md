This review paper synthesizes the acoustical analysis and perceptual significance of high-frequency energy (HFE, 5.7-22 kHz) in human speech and singing, demonstrating that frequencies above 5 kHz affect quality, naturalness, localization, intelligibility, and talker recognition despite being historically neglected in speech research. Key findings include that HFE is 15-17 dB below overall speech level, that fricatives /f/, /theta/, and /s/ have primary spectral peaks in the 7-9 kHz range, that the critical perceptual region for naturalness is 7-10.9 kHz, and that female voices exhibit 2-6 dB more HFE than male voices. This work provides essential empirical constraints for setting high-frequency formant amplitudes (A7-A10) in Klatt parallel-branch synthesis, establishing which phonemes carry significant energy above 5 kHz and what perceptual impact that energy has on speech quality and intelligibility.
