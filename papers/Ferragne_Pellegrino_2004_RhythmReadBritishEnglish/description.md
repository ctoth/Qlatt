This 2004 Interspeech paper by Ferragne & Pellegrino applies the Pairwise Variability Index (PVI) method, fully automatically segmented with no hand labeling, to 14 British English dialects plus French from the ABI and Eurom corpora.
Consonant-duration measures (Dc, rPVIc) reliably separate French from English but fail to discriminate among English dialects, while vowel-duration measures (Dv, nPVIv) show greater between-dialect variability, and no crisp Northern/Southern dialect clustering emerges - only a continuum.
It is directly relevant to a per-accent duration policy: it argues vowel-duration variability, not consonant-duration variability, carries within-language accent rhythm identity, and flags that duration alone is insufficient without pitch/intensity cues.
