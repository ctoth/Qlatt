# Are There Differences in Vocal Parameters Across Various Ethnic Groups?

**Authors:** Mason P. Meek
**Year:** 2018
**Venue:** Cleveland State University, Master's Thesis (MA in Speech-Language Pathology and Audiology), ETD Archive 1096
**DOI/URL:** https://engagedscholarship.csuohio.edu/etdarchive/1096

## One-Sentence Summary
This thesis presents a small-sample descriptive comparison of F0, jitter (RAP), shimmer, NHR, VTI, and glottal closure patterns across African-American, Asian, Caucasian, and Hispanic speakers, finding no statistically significant acoustic differences but noting that posterior glottal chink was present in all non-Caucasian males and correlated with higher F0.

## Problem Addressed
Established vocal parameter norms (F0 ranges, perturbation thresholds) were derived primarily from Caucasian individuals, raising the question of whether these norms generalize across ethnic groups. Prior studies had examined acoustic or perceptual differences in isolation, but none combined acoustic, physiological (videostroboscopy), and perceptual data to investigate ethnic vocal characteristics together.

## Key Contributions
- Combined acoustic (Visi-Pitch MDVP), physiological (videostroboscopy), and perceptual (listener judgment) data across four ethnic groups
- Observed that all non-Caucasian male participants exhibited posterior glottal chink during phonation, while Caucasian males showed complete closure
- Found that males with posterior chink had higher mean F0 (176 Hz) compared to males without (133 Hz), a 43 Hz difference
- Perceptual listeners achieved ~83% accuracy identifying speaker ethnicity from 1-second sustained vowel samples
- All acoustic parameters fell within normal clinical ranges regardless of ethnicity

## Methodology
- 15 participants aged 18-35 from Cleveland State University: 1 AAM, 3 AAF, 2 ASM, 1 ASF, 3 CM, 2 CF, 1 HM, 2 HF
- Acoustic measures via Visi-Pitch IV (KayPentax Model 3950) with MDVP: F0, RAP (jitter), shimmer, VTI, NHR
- Task: sustained vowel /a/ for 4 seconds, recorded twice
- Videostroboscopy via KayPentax Rhino-Laryngeal Stroboscope RLS 9100B for physiological VF examination
- Perceptual task: two inexperienced listeners judged ethnicity from paired 1-second vowel excerpts
- Descriptive analysis only (no inferential statistics due to small sample)

## Key Equations
None. This is a descriptive clinical study with no mathematical models.

## Results Summary
- No statistically significant differences in acoustic parameters across ethnic groups (descriptive analysis only, sample too small for inferential tests)
- All acoustic parameters within normal clinical ranges for all groups
- Males: HM had highest F0 (222 Hz), CM had lowest (133.33 Hz)
- Females: CF had highest F0 (275.5 Hz), ASF had lowest (193.9 Hz)
- Posterior glottal chink observed in all males except the Caucasian group
- All females across all groups exhibited posterior chink
- Males with posterior chink: F0 43 Hz higher than those without
- Perceptual listeners ~83% accurate at identifying ethnicity from 1-second vowel samples

## Limitations
- Very small sample size (n=15, only 1 participant in some groups)
- No inferential statistics possible
- No controls for BMI, height, weight, or dialect
- No control for accented speech (non-native English speakers)
- Rigid endoscope placement at base of tongue limits natural vowel production
- Some participants eliminated due to poor stroboscopic image quality
- Only two perceptual listeners, both male and inexperienced

## Testable Properties
- Male F0 with posterior chink > male F0 without posterior chink (observed: 176 vs 133 Hz)
- Female F0 generally > male F0 across all ethnic groups (consistent with all data)
- All measured acoustic parameters (F0, RAP, shimmer, NHR, VTI) should fall within normal clinical ranges regardless of ethnicity
- Perceptual identification of ethnicity from sustained vowels should exceed chance (50%) levels

## Relevance to Project
This thesis provides limited but interesting descriptive data on F0 variation across ethnic groups for sustained /a/ production. The small sample sizes (n=1-3 per group) make the absolute values unreliable for parameterizing a synthesizer. However, the finding that posterior glottal chink correlates with higher F0 in males is noteworthy for voice source modeling: incomplete glottal closure (as modeled by the OQ/aspiration parameters in Klatt synthesis) may need to be correlated with F0 defaults when building speaker profiles. The normative F0 ranges referenced (128-150 Hz male, 225-250 Hz female) from Williamson 2006 are more useful as baseline values.

### Conceptual Links (not citation-based)

**Male-female voice source differences:**
- [[Titze_1989_MaleFemaleVoices]] — Titze derives physiological scale factors (alpha=1.2, beta=1.6) that predict male-female F0 differences from membranous VF length. Meek's observation that posterior glottal chink correlates with 43 Hz higher F0 in males suggests an additional mechanism beyond VF length: incomplete closure may independently contribute to F0 elevation, which Titze's model does not account for.
- [[Childers_Lee_1991_VoiceQualityFactors]] — Childers & Lee characterize voice qualities (modal, breathy, etc.) through open quotient and turbulent noise parameters. Meek's finding that posterior chink males had higher NHR (.131 vs .126) and VTI (.048 vs .044) aligns with the prediction that incomplete glottal closure increases turbulent noise, consistent with the breathy voice source model.

**Voice source parameters across speaker populations:**
- [[Iseli_2007_VoiceSourceAgeSexVowel]] — Iseli provides quantitative age/sex targets for voice source parameters (H1*-H2*, spectral tilt) across 335 speakers. Meek's acoustic measures (jitter, shimmer, NHR, VTI) are complementary voice quality metrics; both studies suggest that voice source characteristics vary systematically with speaker demographics beyond simple F0 differences.
- [[Babel_2014_VocalAttractiveness]] — Babel measures the same acoustic parameters Meek uses (jitter, shimmer, HNR) plus spectral tilt across 60 speakers, finding that these voice quality parameters are perceptually salient for listener judgments. Meek's finding that listeners identified ethnicity at 83% from 1-second vowel samples suggests these perturbation differences carry perceptual weight for ethnic identification as well.
