# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

This paper does not contain a formal abstract. It is a chapter in an edited volume (*Prosodic Theory and Practice*, Barnes & Shattuck-Hufnagel eds., MIT Press). The opening paragraph serves as an introduction:

"ToBI came into existence about 1990. Its original stated purpose was to serve as an agreed standard for the prosodic annotation of English speech databases. The primary aim of such annotation was to render databases easily searchable -- to make it possible to look for all the phrase-final rises, or all the low accented syllables, or all the sequences of downstepped accents."

---

## Our Interpretation

Ladd argues that while the autosegmental-metrical (AM) approach to intonation is fundamentally sound, its institutionalization in ToBI has locked in premature theoretical commitments -- particularly the assumption that pitch accent categories like L+H* and H* represent discrete phonological contrasts rather than gradient phonetic variation. The paper uses the concepts of cross-dialect variation, "gradience" (Bolinger 1961), and the practical difficulties of transcribing "uptalk" to demonstrate that ToBI's categorical notation obscures important continuous dimensions of intonational meaning. This is relevant to Qlatt because it motivates designing prosody rules that treat F0 targets as continuously variable rather than discretely mapped from ToBI labels.
