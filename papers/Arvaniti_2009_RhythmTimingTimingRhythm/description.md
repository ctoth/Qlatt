Reviews the rhythm-metric literature (%V, ΔC, nPVI, rPVI, VarcoV, VarcoC) and shows with new cross-linguistic data from six languages that metric scores are driven by speaker, elicitation method and material design rather than by language, so they cannot support the stress-timed/syllable-timed division. Argues that timing and rhythm are distinct, that equating them is circular, that syllable-timing is psychologically implausible given the 200 ms fusion threshold and subjective rhythmization, and proposes that rhythm in every language arises from grouping and prominence, with interstress intervals of 380-493 ms falling near the 0.5-0.6 s preferred tempo. For a formant synthesizer this argues against tuning duration rules to metric targets and for an explicit inventory of duration effects, prosodically gated reduction, prominence spacing counted in syllables, and language-specific multi-parameter prominence cues including spectral tilt.
