This is the canonical Grabe & Low (2002) paper that introduces the raw and normalized Pairwise Variability Index (rPVI, nPVI) and applies both to vowel and intervocalic interval durations across 18 languages, seven of them previously rhythmically unclassified.
Vocalic nPVI cleanly separates prototypical stress-timed languages (English, Dutch, German) from syllable-timed languages (French, Spanish) while unclassified languages overlap the boundary, supporting only a weak categorical (gradient) rhythm-class distinction rather than a strict discrete one, and the paper explicitly attributes the stress-timed pattern to vowel reduction producing near-isochronous feet.
It is the direct source of the PVI formulas used by Low, Grabe & Nolan (2000) and by Ferragne & Pellegrino (2004), and supplies an 18-language reference table of target vocalic-nPVI and intervocalic-rPVI values directly usable for calibrating a per-accent duration policy.
