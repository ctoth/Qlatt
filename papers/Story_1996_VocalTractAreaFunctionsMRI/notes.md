# Story, Titze, Hoffman 1996 — Vocal Tract Area Functions from MRI

## Implementation-Relevant Notes

### Overview

MRI-derived vocal tract area functions for 18 phoneme configurations (12 vowels, 3 nasals, 3 plosives) from one male subject (BS). Cross-sectional areas measured perpendicular to the airway centerline, from glottis to lips. This is the most comprehensive speaker-specific area function dataset available for a single subject.

### Key Data: Table III — Equal-Interval Area Functions

Area functions discretized at 0.396825 cm intervals. Section 1 = glottal end, last section = mouth termination. "n.c." row = nasal coupling area (zero for all except nasals).

### Vocal Tract Lengths

- Range: 15.88 cm (/E/) to 18.25 cm (/U/, /l/)
- Most vowels: 16.67-17.46 cm
- Subject BS: 29-year-old male, 5 ft 7 in, 145 lbs, head circumference 57 cm, neck 34 cm

### Nasal Coupling

- /m/: nasal coupling area = 1.04 cm^2, located ~8 cm from glottis
- /n/: nasal coupling area = 1.26 cm^2, located ~8 cm from glottis (1.09 cm^2 per text)
- /ng/: nasal coupling area = 1.09 cm^2

### Key Observations for Synthesis

1. **Front vowels** (/i/, /I/, /E/, /ae/): Large front cavity, small back cavity. /i/ has peak area ~4.7 cm^2 in oral region, drops to ~0.2 cm^2 near lips.

2. **Back vowels** (/V/, /a/, /O/): Constricted pharyngeal region, widened oral cavity. /a/ achieves largest mouth opening (~6.5 cm^2).

3. **Rounded vowels** (/o/, /u/): Two-chamber structure with tight constriction dividing front/back. /u/ has nearly equal front-back cavities (~6 cm^2 each).

4. **Piriform sinuses**: Widening at 2-3 cm above glottis for all vowels, merging with main tract at ~4-5 cm. Consistent across all vowels.

5. **Constriction locations**: /i/ oral, /a/ pharyngeal, /u/ palatal. /3r/ (schwa) has smallest areas near glottis (~0.1 cm^2 at 2 cm).

6. **Plosive occlusions**: /p/ at lips (area drops to 0 at sections 44), /t/ closer to section 37-38, /k/ at sections 25-28.

7. **Nasal tract**: /m/ and /p/ share similar oral cavity shape (occlusion at lips). /n/ and /t/ similar (alveolar occlusion). /ng/ and /k/ similar (velar occlusion).

### Simulation Parameters

- Wave-reflection analog vocal tract model (Story 1995)
- Sampling frequency: 44,100 Hz
- Speed of sound: 350 m/s
- Section length: 0.396825 cm (= 350 / (2 * 44100))
- Includes: wall losses, fluid viscosity, mouth radiation, piriform sinuses as side branch
- Glottal source: parametrized glottal flow waveform (Titze et al., 1994)

### Area Function Discretization

- Raw area functions have 50-80 points at unequal spacing (0.2-0.4 cm)
- For wave-reflection model: normalize to measured length, fit cubic spline, resample at equal intervals
- Section length = speed_of_sound / (2 * sampling_freq)
- At 44.1 kHz and 350 m/s: 0.396825 cm per section
- 44 sections for 17.46 cm tract

### MRI Parameters (Table I)

- GE Signa 1.5 Tesla scanner
- Fast spin-echo, anterior neck coil
- TE = 13 ms, TR = 4000 ms, ETL = 16 ms
- FOV = 24 cm, NEX = 2
- 256x256 pixels, resolution = 0.938 mm/pixel
- Slice thickness = 5 mm
- 26 contiguous parallel axial slices per image set

### Comparison with Previous Studies

- Baer et al. (1991): two subjects TB and PN, similar shapes but BS areas generally smaller in constricted regions
- Yang and Kasuya (1994): similar shapes, BS glottal end areas 0.5-1.0 cm^2 smaller
- Fant (1960): areas considerably larger than BS (based on x-ray and plaster casts, different subject)
- Vowel centralization observed: MRI shapes somewhat centralized due to fatigue during scanning (~30 repetitions per shape)
