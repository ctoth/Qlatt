## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Pierrehumbert, J. (1980),** *The Phonology and Phonetics of English Intonation* - the tonal grammar this chapter's phonetic model presupposes. Without it the H/L inventory, starred tones, phrase accents, and boundary tones that the A and B configurations decompose into are unmotivated. Essential for any synthesizer that needs a symbolic tune representation feeding the F0 scaling rules.
2. **Fujisaki, H. (1981) and Fujisaki, Hirose & Ohta (1979)** - the command-response superposition model, the main competing framework for F0 generation and the one most widely implemented in synthesizers. This chapter devotes three pages to a careful critique, and reading both sides is the fastest route to deciding which F0 architecture to build.
3. **Anderson, S. (1978)** - the source of the constant-ratio treatment of downstep that equation (5b) implements. Useful for understanding what generalizes from tone languages to English and what does not.
4. **Thorsen, N. (1980a)** - the Danish data that constitute the strongest published challenge to purely local, plan-as-you-go F0 rules, since the lowering there is not confined to the final peak in long sentences.
5. **Pierrehumbert, J. and Liberman, M. (1982)** - the detailed rebuttal of Cooper and Sorensen's preplanning claims, including the statistical objections only summarized here. Relevant if the synthesizer's design hinges on whether phrase-level lookahead is required.
