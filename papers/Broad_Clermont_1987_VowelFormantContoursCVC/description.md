Presents a series of mathematical models for formant-frequency contours of vowels in CVC context, progressing from a simple additive superposition of CV and VC transitions through per-consonant similarity (common-contour shapes scaled by consonant loci), target-locus scaling, and finally exponential transition functions with real-time duration dependence. The models are built from American English data with voiced plosives /b,d,g/ and 10 vowels, providing consonant locus values, exponential time constants, and scale factors for F1-F3. The exponential model naturally explains vowel undershoot at short durations and provides a principled, parameterizable framework for computing formant trajectories in synthesis.
