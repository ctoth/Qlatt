## Key Citations for Follow-up

- **[9] Burkhardt & Sendlmeier (2000)** — Source of the joy (81%) and fear (52%) formant-synthesis rule sets and of the two-stage perceptual-optimisation method. Highest priority for a formant synthesiser: it is the only cited work that derives emotion parameters by perception test on a formant synthesiser rather than copying literature values.
- **[4] Cahn (1990)** — Source of the sadness (91%) and surprise (44%) rule sets. Required reading to decode the -10..+10 abstract parameter scale, which cannot be implemented from this review alone.
- **[6] Murray & Arnott** — HAMLET. Source of the anger rule set in absolute physical units (Hz, semitones, wpm, dB, F4 shift, laryngealisation %), which is the most directly implementable of the six, and of the distractor evaluation paradigm.
- **[10] Mozziconacci (1998)** — Source of the boredom rule set, the highest recognition rate in Table 1 (94%), and of the Dutch intonation-pattern grammar whose pattern identifiers (3C, 5&A, 12) this review does not define.
- **[25] Banse & Scherer (1996)** — The acoustic-profile reference for vocal emotion and the source of the argument that forced-choice testing measures discrimination rather than identification.
- **[30] Kasuya, Maekawa & Kiritani (1999)** — Named as the route by which rule-based synthesis could become natural enough to resolve the flexibility/naturalness trade-off; directly relevant to a source-filter engine.
