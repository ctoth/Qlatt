This paper proposes that the speech gesture is a nonlinear autonomous dynamical system with an anharmonic monostable potential, replacing the standard critically damped linear harmonic oscillator model from Saltzman & Munhall (1989) by adding a negative quartic term to the potential function. The key contribution is demonstrating that this simple nonlinear extension correctly predicts three experimentally observed kinematic properties — near-symmetric velocity profiles, nonlinear amplitude-velocity covariation, and the inverse relationship between peak velocity/amplitude ratio and movement duration — that the linear model fails to capture without invoking external timekeepers. For speech synthesis, this work establishes that articulatory movements toward targets follow sigmoid-like trajectories with soft velocity saturation rather than exponential approach curves, providing principled rules for formant transition shapes and the relationship between transition speed, extent, and segment duration.
