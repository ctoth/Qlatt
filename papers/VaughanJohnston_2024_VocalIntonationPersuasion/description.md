This paper investigates how vocal intonation (falling vs. rising pitch at sentence ends) affects persuasion by signaling speaker confidence and influencing listener message processing. The key finding is that falling intonation increases elaboration under moderate processing conditions, which boosts persuasion for strong arguments but can eliminate or reduce persuasion for weak arguments. For speech synthesis, this provides perceptual validation that terminal F0 contours convey confidence/uncertainty, with practical guidelines (~35 Hz shifts, applied to 50%+ of utterances) for implementing expressive speaking styles.
