# 04 — Prosody, Intonation, and Micro-Imperfection: The Melody of a Living Voice

Design axis: **PROSODY, INTONATION, MICRO-IMPERFECTION** — the melody, rhythm, and tiny
aperiodicities that separate a living voice from a robot. Clean-room design; reuses nothing.

This document is opinionated. Where I pick a framework I say why, and I name the loser.

---

## 1. F0 CONTOUR MODELING — which framework generates the most beautiful melody

Three candidate frameworks, three philosophies:

### A. Fujisaki — phrase + accent commands, physiologically grounded
F0 in the **log domain** is the superposition of a baseline, slow *phrase* components (impulse
responses of a critically-damped 2nd-order system), and fast *accent* components (step
responses of a second such system) (`Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling`):

```
log F0(t) = log Fb + Σ Api·Gp(t−T0i) + Σ Aaj·{Ga(t−T1j) − Ga(t−T2j)}
Gp(t) = α²·t·exp(−αt)                          (phrase impulse response)
Ga(t) = min[1 − (1+βt)·exp(−βt), γ]            (accent step response, γ=0.9)
```

Strengths: the response functions are **physiologically universal** (α≈2–3 rad/s phrase,
β≈20 rad/s accent are "nearly constant across speakers and languages"); only the *commands*
(timing, magnitude, polarity) are language/speaker-specific. The output is **intrinsically
smooth and curved** — no piecewise-linear artifacts — because it is the response of a damped
mechanical system. This is the single biggest beauty win: the curve is the impulse response of
a larynx, so it bends the way a real larynx bends. The model is reported "perceptually
indistinguishable" from natural contours (`Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling`).

Weaknesses: command *extraction* from data needs analysis-by-synthesis (no closed form), and
the mapping from linguistic structure to commands is under-specified — Fujisaki gives you the
generator, not the score. (`Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling` open questions: "How to
automatically derive accent command timing?", "good α/β for American English?").

### B. Autosegmental-Metrical / ToBI — Pierrehumbert & Ladd, phonological targets
Intonation is a string of two tones, H and L, realized as pitch accents (H*, L*, L+H*, L*+H,
H+!H*), phrase accents (H-, L-), and boundary tones (H%, L%), generated by a finite-state tune
grammar `[%T] T* … T* T- T%` (`Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation`, `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`).
F0 between targets is *unspecified* — filled by interpolation. Declination is **iterated local
downstep** (each H = k·previous, k≈0.6), not a global slope (`Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`).

Strengths: this is the **best front-end vocabulary**. It gives a compact, compositional,
linguistically-motivated control surface: choose accent type per stressed syllable, edge tones
per boundary, and the meaning falls out (declarative vs. question vs. continuation). It is the
de-facto standard (ToBI) and maps cleanly to focus/emphasis.

Weaknesses — and these are exactly the *beauty* weaknesses:
- AM **says nothing about the curve between targets** ("between events, pitch is phonologically
  unspecified", `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`). Naive linear interpolation between targets
  is the canonical robot sound. The theory itself warns against it: between two H* there must be
  a **sagging transition** (sag then abrupt rise at the next accented-syllable onset), NOT a
  straight line (`Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`, `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation`).
- The categorical commitments are **probably too rigid**. Ladd's own later critique
  (`Ladd_2021_TroubleWithToBI`) argues H* vs L+H* is *gradient*, not categorical (poor
  inter-transcriber reliability, Pitrelli 1994), and that emphasis is continuous peak-height
  variation that categorical labels throw away. Uptalk likewise is a continuum of rise
  start/steepness/end that L* H- H% vs H* H- H% falsely dichotomizes.

### C. Superpositional/declination rules — O'Shaughnessy
A two-level engine: a High-Level System maps syntax/semantics to prosodic indicators (accent,
break, continuation-rise, tune), and a Low-Level System emits Hz with a declination baseline,
sub-contours (rise–peak–fall on accented syllables), and detailed phonetic micro-adjustments
(`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`). Declination ≈ 3%/100ms at onset decaying to ~0.5%/100ms after
5 s; head-peak and F0-room formulas give concrete Hz.

Strengths: the only framework that is a **complete runnable recipe from text** — word-class
accent hierarchy, break/CR placement, and crucially a rich library of **micro-prosodic phonetic
rules** (consonant-voicing perturbation, vowel-height intrinsic pitch, peak timing ±50 ms around
the vowel). Those micro-rules are gold for aliveness and the other two frameworks largely ignore
them.

Weaknesses: piecewise-linear output ("real F0 is smoother than the piecewise linear output",
`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`), English-only, read-speech-only, no emotion, requires a syntactic
parse. As an *intonational theory* it is superseded by AM (`Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`
"supersedes").

### MY OPINION — a hybrid, with a clear division of labor

**Do not pick one. Layer them, each doing what it is best at:**

1. **Control surface = AM/ToBI (Pierrehumbert/Ladd).** Choose accent types and edge tones from
   linguistic structure. This is the *score*. But treat the H*/L+H* distinction as a **gradient
   peak-height + rise-extent parameter, not two discrete targets** — heed `Ladd_2021_TroubleWithToBI`.
   Expose "emphasis strength" as a continuous knob.

2. **Realization engine = Fujisaki-style damped 2nd-order filtering in the log domain.** This is
   the single most important beauty decision. Instead of interpolating *lines* between AM targets,
   convert each AM target into a **command** (phrase command at boundaries, accent command spanning
   the accented region) and run it through Gp/Ga. The output is a physiologically-curved contour
   for free — it gives you the "sagging transition" and the rounded peaks that AM demands but does
   not generate. You get AM's linguistic clarity AND Fujisaki's organic curve. This is the spine of
   a beautiful melody.

3. **Micro-prosody overlay = O'Shaughnessy's phonetic rules + segmental perturbations.** After the
   smooth macro-contour exists, stamp on the consonant-voicing F0 perturbations, intrinsic-vowel
   pitch, and peak-timing offsets. These are what make it sound *spoken* rather than *drawn*.

Why this and not pure Fujisaki? Because Fujisaki under-specifies the score — you still need AM to
decide *where* accents and phrase commands go and *what they mean*. Why not pure AM? Because AM's
interpolation is unspecified and the obvious fill (lines) is the robot. Fujisaki's filter is the
honest answer to "what is the curve between targets." Why not pure O'Shaughnessy? Its macro-melody
is dated and linear, but its micro-rules are irreplaceable and orthogonal to the macro choice.

**One-line summary: ToBI chooses the notes, Fujisaki bends them, O'Shaughnessy roughens them.**

## 2. THE KNOBS for intonation

All F0 math is in the **log (or semitone) domain** — every source agrees (Fujisaki superposes in
log; Ladd's range model is multiplicative; Ladd-1985 scales log-relative-to-floor). Linear-Hz
intonation is a beginner mistake; it makes high pitches sound disproportionately jumpy.

| Knob | What it does | Value / formula | Source |
|------|--------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Baseline / floor `Fr`** | Speaker bottom-of-range, the anchor everything scales from | ~93 Hz male center; 85 Hz BOR; female 150–200 Hz | `OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`, `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation` |
| **Declination (total drop)** | Global downward drift across the phrase | Total drop ~constant (~14 Hz male, ~33 Hz female), independent of length → slope = drop/duration | `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation` |
| **Declination (rate, alt.)** | Same, as a rate | ~3%/100ms at onset, decaying to ~0.5%/100ms after 5 s | `OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody` |
| **Downstep `k`** | Each accent H = k × previous H (gives exponential decay, the *preferred* model of declination) | **k ≈ 0.6** | `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation`, `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology` |
| **Final lowering** | Last accent dips *below* the k-prediction; a separate effect | extra lowering at utterance-final accent | `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology` |
| **Accent shape** | Rise–peak–fall on stressed syllable | rise ≈ 0.40·local F0-room; fall ≈ −0.20·local F0-room | `OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody` |
| **Accent scaling / prominence** | More prominent → H* up, L* down (continuously variable) | peak ratio c ≈ 1.4–1.5 between successive accents | `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation` |
| **Boundary tones** | H% = final rise (yes/no Q, continuation); L% = no rise (statement/finality) | H% raises to ~20% above highest prior peak; question endpoint 170–190 Hz vs statement 70–100 Hz | `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation`, `OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody` |
| **Phrase accent** | H-/L- holds or drops F0 from nuclear accent to boundary | H- maintains level; L- drops soon after nucleus and spreads | `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology` |
| **Upstep** | After H- phrase accent, boundary tone steps up toward a ceiling | T_{n+1} = (1−a)T_n + a·t, a≈0.2, ceiling t≈3 baseline units | `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation` |
| **Pitch range — level vs span (TWO knobs)** | Level = overall height; Span = width of range. *Independent.* Raising voice mostly expands span up from a ~constant floor | F0 = Fr·T·N·r (+ additive shift) | `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology` |
| **Range parameter `R` (paralinguistic)** | Single continuous expansion knob for affect | F0₂ = Fr·[F0₁/Fr]^R ; R=1.0 neutral, R=1.4–1.7 wide/aroused, R=0.6–0.75 narrow/bored | `Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation` |
| **Segmental anchoring** | Align turning points to segment landmarks, not fixed slopes | rise-onset at accented-syllable start; peak at onset of following unstressed vowel; slope adjusts to fit | `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology` |

Three **independent affect channels** (verified, near-zero ANOVA interaction): F0 **range**,
**contour type**, and **voice quality** (`Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation`). For the
melody layer this means: range (R) and contour shape can be controlled as orthogonal knobs;
range is the *strongest, continuous* affect cue (wide→aroused/emphatic; effect is linear not
categorical). Voice quality is owned by `02-voice-quality.md`.

**Pitch-range layering (the clean model, `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`):**
- *Intrinsic*: H vs L target identity within the current tonal space.
- *Extrinsic*: paralinguistic level+span scaling (the R knob) — emotion/emphasis.
- *Metrical*: structural, local — downstep, nested phrase-level downstep, final lowering, reset.

Keep these three as separate multiplicative stages so emotion never corrupts the linguistic
melody and vice versa.

## 3. MICRO-IMPERFECTION — jitter, shimmer, flutter (THE crucial section)

A perfectly periodic F0 is the deadest possible sound. Real voices are aperiodic at several
timescales. Get the numbers right or it sounds either robotic (too little) or pathological/drunk
(too much).

### 3a. Flutter — the slow, quasi-periodic F0 wander (Klatt)
This is the cheapest, highest-impact aliveness trick. Klatt's flutter adds a low-frequency
multi-sine wander to F0 (`Klatt_1990_VoiceQualityVariations`):

```
Δf0 = (FL/50)·(F0/100)·[ sin(2π·12.7·t) + sin(2π·7.1·t) + sin(2π·4.7·t) ]   Hz
```

- Three **incommensurate** frequencies (12.7, 7.1, 4.7 Hz) so the pattern has a very long period
  before repeating — it never sounds like a periodic vibrato.
- Amplitude scales with F0/100 (proportionally larger wander at higher pitch — correct, since
  perturbation is multiplicative).
- **FL = 25% is Klatt's recommended "realistic pitch variation"** value; FL default in the spec
  is 0. Use ~25 for a neutral natural voice.
- At FL=25, F0=100: peak Δ ≈ (25/50)(1)(3 sines, ±3 max) ≈ up to ±1.5 Hz, i.e. roughly **±1–1.5%
  slow wander**. This sits in the right physiological band.

### 3b. Jitter — cycle-to-cycle F0 perturbation (Titze, the physiological numbers)
Flutter is the *slow* wander; jitter is the *fast*, cycle-to-cycle randomness. Titze derives it
from motor-unit firing in the laryngeal muscles and gives hard numbers
(`Titze_1991_NeurologicAperiodicity`):

- **Normal voice F0 perturbation: 0.2–0.3%** (both JIT and CV) — this is the target for a healthy
  neutral voice, and it matches measured human values.
- Full predicted range across parameter settings: **0.05%–1.2%**. Below ~0.1% sounds synthetic;
  above ~1% starts sounding rough/aged/pathological.
- JIT (mean rectified adjacent-cycle difference) ≤ CV (std of the contour) always — useful sanity
  check on any generator.
- **Spectral character matters more than amplitude.** Real jitter is NOT white noise: the F0
  contour spectrum shows peaks at the **motor-unit firing frequencies (~3, 10, 17 Hz)**; the 3 Hz
  peak is involuntary tremor. White-noise jitter sounds wrong; quasi-periodic low-frequency jitter
  sounds alive. (This is *why* Klatt's three-sine flutter works — it is a crude analog of these
  firing-frequency peaks.)
- Four physiological knobs map to speaker identity: motor-unit count, firing rate, twitch-amplitude
  CV, ISI CV. More motor units → less jitter; higher firing rate → less jitter (louder/effortful
  phonation is steadier — a real, exploitable correlation); higher ISI CV → more jitter. Defaults:
  ~100 motor units, ~25 Hz firing, twitch-amp CV ≈ 1.0, ISI CV ν ≈ 0.14 → 0.2–0.3%.

### 3c. Shimmer — cycle-to-cycle amplitude perturbation
Not deeply covered in this paper set (it lives in the voice-quality/source domain,
`02-voice-quality.md`). Note here only that amplitude perturbation co-occurs with jitter and that
Klatt's **diplophonia (DI)** parameter is the related Klatt-side knob: alternate pulses delayed in
time and attenuated in amplitude (`Klatt_1990_VoiceQualityVariations`). DI > 0 only for
deliberately creaky/rough qualities — keep DI = 0 for a neutral beautiful voice.

### 3d. Segmental micro-prosody (the perturbations that read as "spoken")
Distinct from random jitter — these are *deterministic*, segment-locked F0 dips/bumps:
- **Consonant-voicing perturbation**: after a voiceless consonant F0 starts ~20% higher and falls
  through the vowel; after a voiced consonant it rises through the nucleus
  (`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`).
- **Intrinsic vowel pitch**: high vowels (/i/) carry up to ~23 Hz higher F0 than low (/a/);
  Ladd-1985 used +0.5 st for /i/, −1.0 st for /a/ (`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`,
  `Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation`).
- **Microprosodic consonant dips**: V-shaped F0 dips ~15 Hz deep, ~70 ms wide, centered over
  voiced consonants — explicitly noted to "improve naturalness and intelligibility"
  (`Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation`).

### HOW MUCH IS TOO MUCH — concrete budget for a neutral beautiful voice
- Slow flutter (Klatt FL): **~25%** → ~±1% slow wander. Range 0 (dead) … 50 (loose/old).
- Fast jitter (Titze): **0.2–0.3%** cycle-to-cycle. Floor 0.1% (below = synthetic), ceiling ~1%
  (above = rough/pathological); >1.2% = disorder territory.
- Jitter **spectrum**: concentrate energy at 3/10/17 Hz, NOT white. The 3 Hz tremor component is
  the secret of "warm" vs "buzzy."
- Diplophonia DI: **0** for neutral; only raise for intentional creak.
- These stack: flutter (slow) + jitter (fast) + deterministic micro-prosody (segment-locked) are
  three independent layers. Sum them; do not let any single one exceed its ceiling.

## 4. RHYTHM / TIMING at the prosodic level (high level — segmental duration owned elsewhere)

Segmental durations belong to a separate agent. The prosody layer owns only the *suprasegmental*
shaping of rhythm:

- **Phrasing drives everything.** Break indices (3 = intermediate phrase / phrase accent; 4 =
  intonation phrase / phrase accent + boundary tone) define where the melody resets and where
  pauses go (`Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`). Boundary *strength* is gradient: stronger
  boundary → larger F0 reset (higher) → longer pause. Sentence length feeds back into phrasing:
  short utterance = one phrase, long = multiple phrases each with its own nuclear accent.
- **Pre-boundary / final lengthening** is the dominant rhythmic-beauty cue: the phrase-final
  syllable (especially its rhyme) stretches. The prosody layer should emit a *boundary-strength
  scalar* that the duration agent multiplies into final-syllable length, and that co-times with
  the L%/H% boundary tone and final lowering. (Mechanism owned by duration agent; the *trigger and
  magnitude* are prosodic.)
- **Accent ↔ timing coupling.** Accented syllables are longer as well as pitch-prominent; the
  prosody layer's accent-strength value should also feed duration so pitch and length co-vary
  (they always do in real speech). O'Shaughnessy's peak-timing rules (rise ~50 ms before the
  vowel, fall ~50 ms after onset) are the bridge between the two (`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`).
- **Continuation rises** at non-final phrase boundaries (fall+rise or monotonic rise) are the
  rhythmic "comma" — size scales with boundary strength and length of following material
  (`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`).

High-level contract: prosody emits **(phrase tree, boundary strengths, accent strengths)**; the
duration agent turns those into milliseconds. They share the boundary-strength and accent-strength
scalars; nothing else crosses.

## 5. REQUIREMENTS

### Front-End (prosody layer) — what it COMPUTES
Input: token stream with stress + POS/syntax + focus/given-new info. Output: an F0 command list +
timing scalars.

1. **Accent placement & type** (AM control surface): assign pitch-accent per metrically strong
   syllable (default nuclear = last content word, broad focus; shift for narrow focus/deaccenting),
   plus phrase accents and boundary tones via the finite-state tune grammar
   `[%T] T* … T* T- T%` (`Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation`, `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`).
   Word-class accent priority as the bootstrap when no focus info exists
   (`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`).
2. **Gradient accent strength** — emit a *continuous* prominence/peak-height + rise-extent value
   per accent, NOT a hard H*/L+H* category (`Ladd_2021_TroubleWithToBI`).
3. **Phrasing** — break indices 3/4, gradient boundary-strength scalar per boundary; reset height
   and pause length scale with it (`Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`).
4. **Convert AM targets → Fujisaki commands**: phrase command (impulse, A_p, timing T0 ~200 ms
   before phrase onset) at each phrase reset; accent command (step on/off, A_a, T1/T2 ~40–50 ms
   around the accented vowel) per accent (`Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling`).
5. **Scaling stages (kept separate)**: intrinsic (H/L), metrical (downstep k≈0.6, final lowering,
   nested phrase downstep, reset), extrinsic (range R for affect)
   (`Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`, `Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation`).
6. **Segmental micro-prosody schedule**: consonant-voicing F0 perturbation, intrinsic-vowel pitch,
   V-dips over voiced consonants (`OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`,
   `Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation`).
7. **Timing scalars out to duration agent**: per-boundary final-lengthening multiplier, per-accent
   lengthening multiplier.
8. Everything in **log/semitone domain relative to speaker floor Fr**; convert to Hz only at the
   end.

### Back-End — what the DSP must generate for F0 perturbation / flutter
1. **Fujisaki realization filter**: two critically-damped 2nd-order systems (phrase α≈2–3 rad/s,
   accent β≈20 rad/s, γ=0.9 accent ceiling) summed in log-F0, then exp to Hz
   (`Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling`). This produces the smooth macro-contour; it is the
   beauty-critical primitive.
2. **Flutter generator** (Klatt): `Δf0 = (FL/50)(F0/100)[sin(2π·12.7t)+sin(2π·7.1t)+sin(2π·4.7t)]`,
   default FL≈25 (`Klatt_1990_VoiceQualityVariations`).
3. **Jitter generator** (Titze): cycle-synchronous F0 perturbation at **0.2–0.3%** for neutral
   voice, spectrally shaped to peak at ~3/10/17 Hz (NOT white noise). Either the full motor-unit
   summation model (100 units, 25 Hz firing, ISI CV 0.14) or a cheap band-limited-noise analog
   with the same spectrum and amplitude (`Titze_1991_NeurologicAperiodicity`). Expose a single
   "jitter %" knob that maps to age/effort/health; clamp ≤ ~1% for non-pathological voices.
4. **Diplophonia (DI)**: alternate-pulse delay+attenuation, default 0, raise only for creak
   (`Klatt_1990_VoiceQualityVariations`).
5. **Composition order**: macro-contour (Fujisaki) → add flutter (slow) → add jitter (fast,
   per-cycle) → apply deterministic segmental micro-prosody. All additive in log domain.
6. Per-cycle F0 must be queryable cycle-synchronously so jitter/flutter modulate the *actual
   glottal period*, not a frame-rate approximation.

## 6. OPEN QUESTIONS — and which paper settles each

| Question | Settled by / status |
|----------|--------------------|
| What curve fills the gap between AM targets? | **Fujisaki** — damped 2nd-order response, not linear interp. `Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling`; AM itself only flags the need (sagging transition, `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`). |
| Is declination a global slope or local downstep? | **Iterated local downstep k≈0.6** preferred; fits 3× better than constant-interval. `Pierrehumbert_1980_EnglishIntonation`, `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`. |
| Should H* vs L+H* be categorical? | **No — gradient.** Use continuous peak-height/rise-extent. `Ladd_2021_TroubleWithToBI`. |
| How much jitter for a normal voice? | **0.2–0.3%**, range 0.05–1.2%. `Titze_1991_NeurologicAperiodicity`. |
| Is jitter white noise? | **No** — quasi-periodic peaks at 3/10/17 Hz. `Titze_1991_NeurologicAperiodicity`. |
| What flutter setting sounds natural? | **FL≈25%**, three incommensurate sines. `Klatt_1990_VoiceQualityVariations`. |
| Are range, contour, and voice quality independent? | **Yes**, near-zero interaction → orthogonal controls; range is the strongest, *continuous* affect cue. `Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation`. |
| Level vs span — one knob or two? | **Two independent dims**; raising voice expands span up from ~constant floor. `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`. |
| How to scale F0 range for emotion? | `F0₂=Fr·[F0₁/Fr]^R`, R∈[0.6,1.7]. `Ladd_1985_IndependentFunctionIntonation`. |
| How do tonal targets time-align to segments? | **Segmental anchoring** — anchor turning points to landmarks, let slope vary. `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`. |
| α/β values for American English? | **OPEN** — Fujisaki gives Japanese; English α/β need tuning. `Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling` (own open question). |
| How does jitter couple to the glottal source (OQ etc.)? | **OPEN** — Titze flags tension ripple may modulate OQ too. `Titze_1991_NeurologicAperiodicity`; coordinate with `02-voice-quality.md`. |
| Accent command timing from phoneme durations? | **OPEN / partial** — O'Shaughnessy's ±50 ms peak-timing rules are the best available bridge. `OShaughnessy_1976_F0_Prosody`, `Fujisaki_InformationProsodyModeling` (open question). |
| How does emphasis stay independent of accent placement? | **Metrical strength (DTE) ≠ pitch prominence**; emphasis is gradient range, placement is categorical. `Ladd_2008_IntonationalPhonology`. |

## TL;DR for the builder

- **Melody spine: ToBI score → Fujisaki bend → O'Shaughnessy roughen.** AM picks accents/tones,
  a damped 2nd-order filter renders the organic curve, segmental micro-rules make it spoken.
- **All F0 in log/semitones over a speaker floor `Fr`.** Downstep k≈0.6, total declination
  ~constant, final lowering on top.
- **Two range knobs (level, span)**; one affect knob `R∈[0.6,1.7]`. Range/contour/voice-quality
  are orthogonal.
- **Aliveness budget**: flutter FL≈25% (slow, 3 incommensurate sines) + jitter 0.2–0.3%
  (fast, spectrally peaked at 3/10/17 Hz, NOT white) + deterministic segmental perturbations.
  Jitter ceiling ~1% before it sounds pathological; DI=0 for neutral.
- **Prosody/duration contract**: prosody emits phrase tree + gradient boundary-strength +
  gradient accent-strength scalars; duration agent multiplies them into final lengthening.
