This review article comprehensively surveys the phonetic differences between male and female speech, covering phonation and pitch (F0 averages, pitch range in semitones vs Hz), voice quality (breathiness vs creakiness), vowel articulation (non-uniform formant scaling, larger female vowel space), VOT (longer female VOTs for voiceless plosives), and duration/reduction (males ~11% shorter vowels, more reduction), critically evaluating biophysical versus sociophonetic explanations for each difference. The key insight is that many gender differences emerge before puberty in children with anatomically similar vocal tracts, indicating substantial learned sociophonetic components, and that female formants cannot be derived from male formants by a single uniform scaling constant. For a formant synthesizer, this establishes that male and female voice presets require independent per-vowel formant targets, gender-aware voice quality parameters (breathiness/TL, creakiness/OQ), duration adjustments (~11% longer for females), and gender-specific VOT rules, rather than simple pitch shifting or uniform formant scaling.
