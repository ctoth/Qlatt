This paper uses X-ray microbeam articulatory data and acoustic measurements (F1, F2, F3) to demonstrate that English /l/ varies continuously between light and dark allophones as a function of syllable position and pre-boundary rime duration, rather than exhibiting a categorical two-allophone distinction. The key contribution is the Tip Delay metric (temporal offset between dorsal retraction and apical tip raising) that continuously predicts /l/ darkness, plus the two-gesture model showing that /l/ involves independent vocalic (dorsal) and consonantal (apical) components whose relative timing determines allophonic quality. For speech synthesis, these findings provide specific F2 targets (light ~1300 Hz, dark ~900 Hz) and F3 targets (light ~2900 Hz, dark ~2400 Hz) for implementing syllable-position-dependent /l/ allophony in Klatt formant synthesis, directly informing the dark-L rule in Qlatt's declarative frontend.
