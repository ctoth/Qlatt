Systematic study of glottalization at phrase boundaries in American English, examining 14 speakers (professional and nonprofessional). Defines four acoustic categories of glottalization — aperiodicity, creak, diplophonia, and glottal squeak — and quantifies their distribution by prosodic position (utterance-final vs. medial, full vs. intermediate intonational phrase boundaries). Finds wide inter-speaker variation in both rate (13–88%) and preferred acoustic realization, with utterance-final and full IP boundaries consistently showing higher glottalization rates.
