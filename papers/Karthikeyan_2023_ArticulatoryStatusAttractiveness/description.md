This paper investigates how men's articulatory clarity of word-final /t/ (aspirated vs unreleased) influences women's mating attractiveness evaluations and men's status perceptions, using speech samples from 80 male speakers rated by 91 participants. The key findings show that aspirated /t/ speakers are rated as more attractive for long-term relationships and higher in prestige (but not physical dominance), with jitter as the only acoustic parameter differentiating the two speaker groups, while F0 and formant frequencies do not differ. For speech synthesis voice profiles, this establishes that stop release parameters (VOT, burst amplitude, aspiration) contribute to perceived social status and attractiveness, suggesting that articulation precision should be a controllable dimension in voice preset design.
