## Key Citations for Follow-up

1. **Tabain, M. (1998)** - "Non-sibilant fricatives in English: Spectral information above 10 kHz" - Directly relevant for high-frequency non-sibilant spectra above 10 kHz, which is exactly the A8-A10 range we need.

2. **Strevens, P. (1960)** - "Spectra of fricative noise in human speech" - Foundational data on fricative noise spectra that could provide additional spectral envelope data.

3. **Maniwa, K., Jongman, A., and Wade, T. (2009)** - "Acoustic characteristics of clearly spoken English fricatives" - Includes spectral peak measures over the full 15 kHz range that complement the current paper.

4. **Fox, R. A., and Nissen, S. L. (2005)** - "Sex-related acoustic changes in voiceless English fricatives" - Gender-specific spectral differences relevant for male vs female voice parameter sets.

5. **Fant, G. (1970)** - Acoustic Theory of Speech Production - Provides the theoretical source-filter model for fricatives, including the "main fricative formant" at 8500 Hz for /f/ from the front cavity resonance.
