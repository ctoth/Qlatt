Serra and Smith present spectral modeling synthesis (SMS), which decomposes a sound into a deterministic part of tracked sinusoids with piecewise-linear amplitude and frequency envelopes plus a stochastic part obtained by subtracting the resynthesized sinusoids from the original magnitude spectra and fitting the residual with a line-segment spectral envelope. Analysis uses short-time Fourier transform peak detection with parabolic three-bin interpolation and a frequency-guide peak-continuation algorithm with birth, sleeping, death, and recursive conflict resolution, while synthesis combines additive synthesis of the sinusoids with overlap-add inverse FFT of the residual envelope driven by random phase. For a formant speech synthesizer this is the origin of harmonic-plus-noise modeling: it gives a principled recipe for extracting an aspiration and frication noise floor from real speech and for shaping it with a time-varying filter, plus the exact windowing and random-phase rules that keep block-based noise generation free of frame-rate artifacts.
