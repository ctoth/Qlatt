This paper uses MRI scans of 129 subjects ages 2-25 to establish normative data on vocal tract length and segment proportions, providing regression equations relating VTL to body size (height: r=0.926; log weight: r=0.941) and documenting that VTL scales allometrically as approximately the cube root of body mass. The key finding is that male-female vocal tract length differences arise at puberty through disproportionate pharyngeal elongation in males (pharynx accounts for ~6.8 mm of the 12.9 mm total sex difference), while oral cavity segments grow roughly uniformly and show no significant sex differences. For the Qlatt synthesizer's speaker profile system, this provides the quantitative basis for deriving vocal tract length from speaker body size parameters, establishes that oral and pharyngeal sections must be scaled independently (not uniformly) when modeling different speaker types, and demonstrates that child-to-adult voice changes require segment-specific growth curves rather than simple uniform VTL scaling.
