Scherer, Ladd, and Silverman (1984) test two competing models of how vocal cues signal speaker affect: the "covariance" model (acoustic parameters vary continuously and independently of text) versus the "configuration" model (affect is conveyed by categorical intonation patterns interacting with linguistic context). Through judgment experiments using full audio, transcripts, and acoustically masked stimuli (low-pass filtered, random-spliced, reversed), they show that voice quality cues (spectral energy distribution) convey affect independently of text as predicted by the covariance model, while intonation contour type (rise vs. fall) conveys affect only in interaction with question type and text, as predicted by the configuration model. The paper concludes that both models are needed and that paralinguistic (voice quality) and linguistic (intonation category) features of F0 must be distinguished in affect research.
