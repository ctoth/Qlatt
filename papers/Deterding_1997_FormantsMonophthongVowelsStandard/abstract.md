# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

The formants of the eleven monophthong vowels of Standard Southern British (SSB) pronunciation of English were measured for five male and five female BBC broadcasters whose speech was included in the MARSEC database. The measurements were made using linear-prediction-based formant tracks overlaid on digital spectrograms for an average of ten instances of each vowel for each speakers, These measurements were taken from connected speech, allowing comparison with previous formant values measured from citation words. I was found that the male vowels were significantly less peripheral in the measurements from connected speech than in measurements from citation words.

(Note: "I was found" appears verbatim in the published abstract, apparently an uncorrected typo for "It was found".)

---

## Our Interpretation

The paper asks whether formant values measured from natural connected speech differ from the more common citation-word measurements typically used as reference vowel targets. It answers that, at least for male speakers, connected speech is measurably more centralized (less peripheral/less extreme) than citation-word speech, while for female speakers the difference did not reach statistical significance. It is relevant here as a compact, corpus-grounded per-sex Hz formant table for Standard Southern British monophthongs, and as direct evidence that isolated-word vowel targets likely need to be pulled toward the vowel-space centroid for synthesizing natural continuous speech.
