This paper applies sparse symbolic regression (SINDy) to 13,742 articulatory gesture trajectories from the X-Ray Microbeam corpus to discover the dynamical equations governing speech movements, finding that a second-order linear model achieves R-squared of 0.98 but that approximately one third of trajectories require a nonlinear cubic restoring force term. The key finding is that articulatory gestures are under-damped rather than critically damped as assumed by the standard task-dynamic model, with the discovered damping coefficient dramatically below the critical damping threshold, and that a virtual target halfway between initial position and empirical target captures the dynamics during the gesture activation interval. For speech synthesis, this work provides empirical evidence that formant transition trajectories may exhibit slight overshoot dynamics rather than monotonic target approach, and offers a principled dynamical framework for generating articulatory trajectories that could inform coarticulation modeling and duration rules in Klatt-style synthesizers.
