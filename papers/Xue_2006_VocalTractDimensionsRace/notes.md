# Normative Standards for Vocal Tract Dimensions by Race as Measured by Acoustic Pharyngometry

**Authors:** Steve An. Xue, Jianping G. Hao
**Year:** 2006
**Venue:** Journal of Voice, Vol. 20, No. 3, pp. 391-400
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jvoice.2005.05.001

## One-Sentence Summary
Provides normative vocal tract length and volume measurements (oral, pharyngeal, total) for White American, African American, and Chinese male and female speakers aged 20-30, using acoustic pharyngometry.

## Problem Addressed
No prior study had directly compared vocal tract dimensions across racial groups with controlled height, weight, and age. Existing vocal tract data came from small samples (often Fant's 7+7 Swedish speakers) and lacked volumetric measurements or racial diversity.

## Key Contributions
- First cross-racial normative database of vocal tract dimensions (6 parameters x 3 races x 2 genders)
- Demonstrated significant racial effects on oral volume and total vocal tract volume
- Confirmed gender effects on vocal tract length and pharyngeal volume across all three races
- Validated acoustic reflection (AR) technology for vocal tract morphological measurement

## Methodology
- 120 subjects: 20M + 20F per race (White American, African American, Chinese)
- Age range: 20-30 years, controlled for height and weight
- Acoustic pharyngometry (Eccovision Acoustic Pharyngometer) measured area-distance curves from lips to glottis
- Subjects sustained mental /æ/ configuration during measurement
- Oral/pharyngeal boundary defined at oropharyngeal juncture (OPJ) by cross-sectional area minimum
- Two-way MANOVA (race x gender), followed by one-way MANOVA per gender, Tukey HSD post hoc

## Key Equations

No novel equations presented. The study relies on acoustic reflection technology (impulse response method) where cross-sectional area is derived from reflected acoustic wave amplitude and arrival time.

## Implementation Details

### Measurement Protocol
- Eccovision Acoustic Pharyngometer (Sensormedics Corp.)
- Short-duration acoustic impulse sent through wave tube into vocal tract
- Reflected wave recorded by microphone, digitized
- Cross-sectional area computed as function of distance from lips
- Oral region: incisors to anterior margin of OPJ
- Pharyngeal region: soft palate to glottis opening
- Subject configuration: mental /æ/ posture, mouth breathing

### Reliability
- Intrajudge: Pearson r = 0.98-0.99 for all parameters
- Interjudge: Pearson r = 0.98-0.99 for all parameters
- Absolute differentials: 0.00-0.04 across all measures

## Results Summary

### Gender Effects (Two-way MANOVA, all races combined)
- Male > Female: oral length (F(1,114)=9.24, P<0.01), pharyngeal length (F(1,114)=14.36, P<0.001), total vocal tract length (F(1,114)=52.63, P<0.001), pharyngeal volume (F(1,114)=29.81, P<0.001), total vocal tract volume (F(1,114)=14.04, P<0.001)
- Oral volume: male larger but not significant

### Race Effects (Two-way MANOVA)
- Significant for: oral volume (F(2,114)=6.98, P=0.001), total vocal tract volume (F(2,114)=4.41, P<0.05)
- Chinese > WA and AA in oral volume (P<0.01)
- Chinese > AA in total vocal tract volume (P=0.01)
- No significant WA vs AA differences in volume

### Race x Gender Interaction
- Significant interaction for total vocal tract length only
- Chinese females: significantly shorter VTL than WA and AA females
- WA females: significantly larger pharyngeal volume than AA and Chinese females

### Male Speakers by Race
- Chinese males: significantly larger oral volume than WA (P<0.01) and AA (P=0.001)
- Chinese males: significantly larger total vocal tract volume than WA (P<0.05) and AA (P<0.01)
- No significant racial differences in length parameters for males

### Female Speakers by Race
- Chinese females: significantly shorter vocal tract length than WA and AA (P<0.05)
- WA females: significantly larger pharyngeal volume than AA and Chinese (P<0.05)

## Limitations
- Only three racial groups studied (no Hispanic, Native American, etc.)
- Age range limited to 20-30 years
- /æ/ configuration only — no data for other vowel postures
- No formant frequency measurements to correlate with dimensions
- Height/weight controlled but not perfectly matched across groups (up to 2 cm height, 5 kg weight difference)
- 20 subjects per cell — adequate for group means but limited for individual variation characterization
- Acoustic pharyngometry measures area along a single axis, not full 3D shape

## Testable Properties
- Male total vocal tract length must exceed female total vocal tract length on average (~17 cm vs ~15.5 cm)
- Male pharyngeal length > female pharyngeal length across all three races
- Oral length + pharyngeal length = total vocal tract length
- All vocal tract lengths must be in range [12, 20] cm for healthy adults aged 20-30
- Oral volume + pharyngeal volume = total vocal tract volume
- Total vocal tract volume must be in range [40, 90] mL for healthy adults aged 20-30
- Male/female VTL ratio should be approximately 1.10 (17.03/15.54 = 1.096)

## Relevance to Project
This paper provides normative vocal tract dimensional data that can inform speaker-specific vocal tract length scaling in Qlatt's formant synthesis. The male/female VTL ratio (~1.10) and absolute length values (males ~17 cm, females ~15.5 cm) are directly relevant for setting uniform formant scaling factors. The racial variation data (Chinese males having larger oral volumes, White American females having larger pharyngeal volumes) could inform speaker profile presets. The two-tube model boundary definition (oral = incisors to OPJ area minimum, pharynx = OPJ to glottis) aligns with Fant's acoustic theory framework used throughout the project.
