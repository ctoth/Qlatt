Holmes describes a computer-simulated parallel formant synthesizer capable of producing speech nearly indistinguishable from natural speech under critical earphone listening, using an inverse-filtered glottal pulse as the voiced source. The paper compares inverse-filtered, cosine-segment, and phase-manipulated glottal sources, finding that inverse-filtered pulses sound most natural but that idealized pulses are nearly as good. A key practical finding is that glottal pulse phase structure is perceptually irrelevant for loudspeaker listening in reverberant rooms, as room acoustics randomize harmonic phases.
